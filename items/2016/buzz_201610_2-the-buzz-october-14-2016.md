Health law and worker privacy.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this written testimony (https://www.eeoc.gov/eeoc/meetings/10-13-16/ajunwa.cfm) before the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, Ifeoma Ajunwa (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/iajunwa) summarizes " a number of practices that employers have begun to deploy to collect information on employees, and my concerns that such information could ultimately be acquired and sold by data brokers or stored in databanks."

------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/phaduong
Learning resilience online through Minecraft.
------------------------------------------------------------
Paulina Haduong (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/phaduong)  explores how we can support young people in learning how to productively engage in dialogue and operate as courteous digital citizens in this piece (http://blogs.lse.ac.uk/parenting4digitalfuture/2016/10/12/learning-resilience-online-through-minecraft/) for the Parenting for a Digital Future blog. She looks at one particular online community, Connected Camps, that offers a more positive experience for young people and takes into account users’ youth and desire to learn.
------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jwolff
Security in Internet governance debates.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this paper (http://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/what-we-talk-about-when-we-talk-about-cybersecurity-security-internet-governance) in the Internet Policy Review, Josephine Wolff (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jwolff) discusses "how different stakeholders define and frame internet security issues in the context of governance debates and analyse[s] how these conflicting notions of security continue to shape emerging controversies."
------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz
Universities and patent trolls.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://medium.com/@yardenkatz/universities-have-turned-over-hundreds-of-patents-to-patent-trolls-99d5cdec1d8a#.l9vz6dtal) on Medium, Yarden Katz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz) traces the origins of patents that are now owned by Intellectual Ventures, "a high-powered patent troll," and finds that many of these once belonged to universities - including those that have promised in the past not to work with patent trolls.

------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford
The surprising backbone of the IoT.
------------------------------------------------------------
"With streetlights and signal poles, a city stands a chance of pushing along a competitive and innovative world of Internet of Things and sensors and data transmission, as long as it acts decisively to open those street lights and signal poles on a standard technical basis ," writes Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) in this piece (https://backchannel.com/the-surprising-backbone-of-the-internet-of-things-4330301084b0#.jyyfktysm) for Backchannel.
------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier
Saving the Internet from the IoT.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://motherboard.vice.com/read/we-need-to-save-the-internet-from-the-internet-of-things) for Motherboard, Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) describes a recent DDos attack that recruited IoT devices like CCTV cameras, digital video recorders, and home routers to take down a website. "Much has been written about how the IoT is wildly insecure. In fact, the software used to attack Krebs was simple and amateurish. What this attack demonstrates is that the economics of the IoT mean that it will remain insecure unless government steps in to fix the problem."
