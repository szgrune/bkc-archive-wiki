A grand bargain to make tech companies trustworthy.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (http://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2016/10/information-fiduciary/502346/) for The Atlantic, Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) and Jack M. Balkin (https://www.law.yale.edu/jack-m-balkin) propose a path for tech companies to be "information fiduciaries" as a way to protect individual privacy rights. "To deal with the new problems that digital businesses create, we need to adapt old legal ideas to create a new kind of law—one that clearly states the kinds of duties that online firms owe their end users and customers," they write.

**
NH ban on "ballot selfies" and Mass SJC ruling on cell phone seizure.
------------------------------------------------------------
Last week, the Cyberlaw Clinic blog (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/blog/) caught us up on two cases in which it filed amicus briefs. First, the NH ban on ballot selfies was held unconstitutional (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2016/09/29/nh-ban-on-ballot-selfies-held-unconstitutional/)  in a finding by the First Circuit that the law "could not survive even the lower threshold of intermediate scrutiny." In the second case (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2016/09/30/mass-sjc-issues-ruling-on-cell-phone-seizure/) , the Mass SJC held that "probable cause to seize a phone 'may not be based solely on an officer’s opinion that the device is likely to contain evidence of the crime under investigation.'"

The invisible politics of Bitcoin.
------------------------------------------------------------
Inthis paper (http://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/invisible-politics-bitcoin-governance-crisis-decentralised-infrastructure) in the Internet Policy Review, Primavera De Filippi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pdefilippi)  "examines the political economy of Bitcoin, in light of a recent dispute that divided the Bitcoin community with regard to a seemingly simple technical issue: whether or not to increase the block size of the Bitcoin blockchain"

** Toward constructive technology criticism.
------------------------------------------------------------
Inthis report (http://www.cjr.org/tow_center_reports/constructive_technology_criticism.php)  for the Tow Center for Digital Journalism, Sara Watson (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swatson)  draws on "interviews with journalists and critics, as well as a broad reading of published work, to assess the current state of technology coverage and criticism in the popular discourse, and to offer some thoughts on how to move the critical enterprise forward."
