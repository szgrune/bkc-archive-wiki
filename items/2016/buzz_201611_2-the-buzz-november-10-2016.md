WikiLeaks isn't Whistleblowing.
"Demanding transparency from the powerful is not a right to see every single private email anyone in a position of power ever sent or received," writes Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) in this piece (http://www.nytimes.com/2016/11/05/opinion/what-were-missing-while-we-obsess-over-john-podestas-email.html) for The New York Times about the chilling effects of mass releases of hacked emails. "Wanton destruction of the personal privacy of any person who has ever come near a political organization is a vicious but effective means to smother dissent."

Iranians, the US Election, and
House of Cards.
"Frank Underwood, the lead character of the popular Netflix TV series “House of Cards,"  has an infamous line: “Democracy is so overrated”. This much-quoted line, especially since the show began airing in Iran, has become a poignant touchstone for Iranians following the US presidential elections this year, reminding them of their own political sphere's struggles for democracy," writes Simin Kargar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/skargar) in this piece (https://globalvoices.org/2016/11/07/to-some-iranians-watching-the-us-elections-democracy-really-seems-like-a-house-of-cards/) for Global Voices.

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier

American elections will be hacked.
"It’s over. The voting went smoothly. As of the time of writing, there are no serious fraud allegations, nor credible evidence that anyone hacked the voting rolls or voting machines. And most important, the results are not in doubt," writes Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  in this piece (http://www.nytimes.com/2016/11/09/opinion/american-elections-will-be-hacked.html) for The New York Times. "While we may breathe a collective sigh of relief about that, we can’t ignore the issue until the next election. The risks remain."

Public information and the price of Internet access services.
"Drawing from their experience studying trends in internet services pricing across the country, a team of researchers, including Berkman Klein Center Research Director Rob Faris, Cyberlaw Clinic project coordinator Kira Hessekiel, Berkman Klein fellow David Talbot, and HLS ’18 student Danielle Kehl, submitted a comment (https://www.ntia.doc.gov/files/ntia/publications/berkmanklein.pdf)  to the National Telecommunications and Information Administration and National Science Foundation to advocate for more comprehensive public information on the price of internet access services." Read more about the comment on the Cyberlaw Clinic blog (http://blogs.harvard.edu/cyberlawclinic/2016/11/07/cyberlaw-clinic-and-berkman-klein-researchers-submit-ntia-comment-on-broadband-research-agenda/) .
Listen: Judith Donath on Signaling, Design, and the Social Machine
http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2016/10/judith_donath_o.html

In this episode (http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2016/10/judith_donath_o.html)  of EconTalk, Judith Donath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jdonath) , author of The Social Machine, discusses the ideas in her book--an examination of signaling, online identity, and online community. Donath argues that design elements in technology play a key role in our interactions with one another. The conversation closes with a discussion of data collection by corporations and the government.
