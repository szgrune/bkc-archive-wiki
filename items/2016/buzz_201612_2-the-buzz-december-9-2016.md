New Publications Examine Harmful Speech Online
The Berkman Klein Center's Harmful Speech Online project (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/harmfulspeech) has just released four new publications (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/99716) authored by a global and multidisciplinary group of project collaborators. The papers include:
* a paper that explores the definitional and framing questions that complicate efforts to study and address harmful speech online;
* a study of the legal foundations of harmful speech regulation in India;
* an assessment of the efforts of civil society organizations to address racist speech in Brazil and Colombia; and
* a research note that offers reflections and observations on the state of research related to harmful speech online.

Learn more about the project and the papers. (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/harmfulspeech)

Predictions for 2017 and 2027.
In this post (http://blogs.microsoft.com/next/2016/12/05/17-17-microsoft-researchers-expect-2017-2027/#sm.0000od71v4fmjdqgtlj1c53rgtxn6)  on the Microsoft Blog featuring the company's researchers on what to expect, Mary L. Gray (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mgray) writes, "By 2027, nearly 30 percent of adults in the United States will do some form of gig work, delivering AI-driven goods and services like tax advice or healthcare support. Consumers may or may not know when they are reliant on a human worker-in-the-loop."

Encounters with Other Intelligences
In this reflection (https://www.digitalasiahub.org/2016/12/02/encounters-with-other-intelligences-reflections-from-digital-asia-hubs-ai-in-asia-conference/) from the Digital Asia Hub (https://www.digitalasiahub.org/) 's recent AI in Asia conference, Sara M.
