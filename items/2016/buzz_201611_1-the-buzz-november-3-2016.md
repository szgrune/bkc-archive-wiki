Pepe, Nasty Women, and the Memeing of American Politics.
------------------------------------------------------------
"If the Obama campaign of 2008 will be remembered for its innovative use of the Internet, the Trump and Clinton campaigns of 2016 will be remembered for embracing the Internet in full force, using Twitter, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, text messages and other social networking platforms to reach potential voters," writes An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina) in this post (http://www.beaconbroadside.com/broadside/2016/11/pepe-nasty-women-and-the-memeing-of-american-politics.html) for Beacon Broadside.

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom
Twitter’s New @Replies re-design isn’t just stupid; it’s really stupid.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this Medium post (https://medium.com/@tressiemcphd/twitters-new-replies-re-design-isn-t-just-stupid-it-s-really-stupid-c471ca254f0a#.u613dhamg) , Tressie (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom) McMillan Cottom (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom) explains why a new Twitter feature that makes it hard to see who you're replying to poses a series of problems, particularly for users who have identities that make them more vulnerable to harassment. "Without the security of knowing to whom I am replying, I cannot safely tweet."

------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier
How your WiFi-connected thermostat can take down the whole Internet.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2016/11/03/your-wifi-connected-thermostat-can-take-down-the-whole-internet-we-need-new-regulations/) in The Washington Post, Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) explains the security threats we face from IoT and argues that we need regulations now -- before a catastrophe strikes. "Regardless of what you think about regulation vs. market solutions, I believe there is no choice," writes Schneier. "Governments will get involved in the IoT, because the risks are too great and the stakes are too high." (And in this piece (https://securityintelligence.com/lessons-from-the-dyn-ddos-attack/) for Security Intelligence, Schneier outlines some lessons from the recent Dyn DDoS attack.)

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford
Why The AT&T-Time Warner Merger Must Be Stopped.
------------------------------------------------------------
"This deal invites unfair competition and locks in our horrible access problems," writes Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) in this piece (https://backchannel.com/the-at-t-time-warner-merger-must-be-stopped-5b5353d5a0dc#.r3c743qyt) for Backchannel. "There is zero reason for the Department of Justice or the Federal Communications Commission — who will pool their resources to examine this deal — to approve this transaction. And there are more reasons to say no than there are channels on DirectTV."
