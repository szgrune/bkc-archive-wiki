Why fake news stories thrive online.
"In the world of social media, of Facebook and Twitter, news is shared not just to inform or even to persuade. It is used as a marker of identity, a way to proclaim your affinity with a particular community," writes Judith Donath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jdonath) in this piece (http://www.cnn.com/2016/11/20/opinions/fake-news-stories-thrive-donath/index.html) for CNN about the power of fake news and some ideas for responding to it.

Technology for inequality: how chatbots can help shape an (even more) uneven world
In a future where chatbots are personal customized assistants, access to datasets can be considered an important factor for the quality of the services of the robot itself, according to Yasodara Córdova (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ycordova) . Open Data and parity of information are, more than ever, a matter of human rights, she explains in this piece (https://medium.com/@yaso/technology-for-inequality-how-chatbots-can-help-shape-an-even-more-uneven-world-29688907126#.5jun0oa7e) on Medium.

The European Court of Human Rights and Access to Information.
"On 8 November 2016, the Grand Chamber of the European Court of Human Rights handed down a much-anticipated judgment on the right of access to information," write Nani Jansen Reventlow (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/njansen) and Jonathan McCully in this post (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2016/11/22/the-european-court-of-human-rights-and-access-to-information-clarifying-the-status-with-room-for-improvement/) for the Cyberlaw Clinic blog. "While the Court was clearer and firmer than it had ever been before on the status of the right to access information as part of the right to freedom of expression guaranteed by Article 10 of the European Convention, it stopped short of acknowledging access to information as a fully-fledged right under the provision."
