'Created' in China
"China is amidst a massive transition from a country primarily of manufacturers to one of makers," writes An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina)  in this piece (http://fusion.net/story/338939/created-in-china-shenzhen-hardware-startups/) for Fusion. "Internet platforms are speeding up production efficiency and enabling rapid, decentralized innovation through sharing and iteration. The implications of the internet meeting Shenzhen’s long-established environment for production point at substantial changes in what hardware can look like and who it can come from."

Conspiracy theories and Internet.
------------------------------------------------------------
"Conspiracy theories are like mosquitoes that thrive in swamps of low-trust societies, weak institutions, secretive elites and technology that allows theories unanchored from truth to spread rapidly," writes Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) in this piece (http://www.nytimes.com/2016/09/13/opinion/campaign-stops/did-you-hear-the-latest-about-hillary.html?_r=0) for The New York Times. "The new, internet-driven financing model for news outlets is great for spreading conspiracy theories."

"Voice" and ABC's new TV series "Speechless"
------------------------------------------------------------
"We cannot abstract “voice” from institutions, like schooling and policing, that regularly do a poor job of listening to the concerns of those less privileged in society by virtue of their disability, as well as their race, ethnicity, gender, and class." In this piece (http://motherboard.vice.com/en_uk/read/what-abcs-new-tv-series-speechless-can-tell-us-about-voice) for Motherboard, Meryl Alper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/malper) discusses the new sitcom, which features a lead character who uses an augmentative and alternative communication system and some of the issues it raises.

New Research from the Berkman Klein Publication Series (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications)

Hammering Square Pegs into Round Holes: The Geographical Scope of Application of the EU Right to be Delisted (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2016/squarepegs)
by Michel Reymond
"This paper explores the extraterritorial effects of the Google Spain decision rendered in May 2014 by the European Court of Justice. Through a methodology inspired by private international law, the author examines the geographical reach of the so-called ‘Right to be Forgotten,’ which is more correctly identified as a ‘Right to be Delisted.’"

Culture Change and Digital Technology: The NYPD under Commissioner William Bratton, 2014-2016 (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2016/NYPD)
by Susan Crawford and Laura Adler
"At this early stage of digital technology adoption, the NYPD’s attempt under Commr. William Bratton (2014-2016) to change the culture of policing by enriching communications between police and neighborhoods holds lessons for public agencies across the U.S. during a period of intense volatility." This paper examines several digital strategies employed by the NYPD during the last two years.
