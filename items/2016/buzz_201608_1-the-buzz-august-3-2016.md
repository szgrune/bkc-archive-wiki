I'm with the banned.
------------------------------------------------------------
Laurie Penny (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/lpenny) explains in this Medium piece (https://medium.com/welcome-to-the-scream-room/im-with-the-banned-8d1b6e0b2932#.a1z785git) what an evening with Milo Yiannopoulos told her "about Twitter’s biggest troll, the death of reason, and the crucible of A-list con-men that is the Republican National Convention." She writes, "This is a story about how trolls took the wheel of the clown car of modern politics. It’s a story about the insider traders of the attention economy. It’s a story about fear and loathing and Donald Trump and you and me."

WikiLeaks put women in Turkey in danger, for no reason.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (http://www.huffingtonpost.com/zeynep-tufekci/wikileaks-erdogan-emails_b_11158792.html) http://www.huffingtonpost.com/zeynep-tufekci/wikileaks-erdogan-emails_b_11158792.htmlfor the Huffington Post, Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) explains why the sharing of 300,000 emails by WikiLeaks, "was irresponsible, of no public interest and of potential danger to millions of ordinary, innocent people, especially millions of women in Turkey," and how a lack of good reporting by Western media outlets allowed this information to spread.

** Western media and local partnerships in India.
------------------------------------------------------------
"If a publisher wants rapid success in India, a hugely attractive but notoriously difficult market, it might be best to set up a partnership than go it alone: One Indian media giant claims it can help boost audience and revenue by a factor of at least four," writes Hasit Shah (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/hshah) in this piece (http://www.niemanlab.org/2016/07/for-western-news-companies-looking-to-india-partnering-with-local-publishers-is-a-path-in/) for Nieman Lab.

** Russian hackers and our voting machines.
------------------------------------------------------------
If Russia really is responsible, there's no reason political interference would end with the DNC emails, argues Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) in this piece (https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2016/07/27/by-november-russian-hackers-could-target-voting-machines/) for the Washington Post. "We have to accept that someone is attacking our nation’s computer systems in an apparent attempt to influence a presidential election. This kind of cyberattack targets the very core of our democratic process."

------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/dgillmor
The DIY approach to internet access.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://backchannel.com/forget-comcast-heres-the-diy-approach-to-internet-access-ef1e37bc09e1#.ipzsnz5a7) for Backchannel, Dan Gillmor (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/dgillmor) describes how a Spanish engineer got tired of waiting for telecom companies to wire his town — so he did it himself. "For people who want to see an Internet at least partly liberated from the grip of rapacious, government-connected telecommunications giants, Guifi is one of the most hopeful developments to date," he writes.
------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/scrawford
The limits of Net Neutrality.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://backchannel.com/net-neutrality-is-only-a-start-f2f539dd6e5a#.9f1oylzgc) for Backchannel, Susan Crawford (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) argues that open access to the Internet needs stronger curbs on big network operators. "In the US and Europe, there’s authority to impose these," she writes. "That’s easier than it sounds."
