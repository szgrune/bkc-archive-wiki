Read: Recent news and commentary from around the community
https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ugasser
Recoding Privacy Law: Reflections on the Future Relationship Among Law, Technology, and Privacy
In this (http://harvardlawreview.org/2016/12/recoding-privacy-law-reflections-on-the-future-relationship-among-law-technology-and-privacy/) Harvard Law Review commentary (http://harvardlawreview.org/2016/12/recoding-privacy-law-reflections-on-the-future-relationship-among-law-technology-and-privacy/) , Urs Gasser (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ugasser) writes, "Taken together, the development of privacy tools that aim to integrate legal and technical approaches could help pave the way for a more strategic and systematic way to conceptualize and orchestrate the contemporary interplay between law and technology in the field of information privacy."

------------------------------------------------------------
https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom
A Path through the Media's Coming Legitimacy Crisis
For the Nieman Lab's 2017 "Predictions for Journalism" series, Tressie McMillan Cottom (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom)  wrote this piece (http://www.niemanlab.org/2016/12/a-path-through-the-medias-coming-legitimacy-crisis/) about how newsrooms need to become more diverse in order to rebuild their legitimacy in the "post-fact" world.

------------------------------------------------------------
https://wwz.unibas.ch/en/people/profile/person/matter/
Interest Groups, Public Attention, and the Effect on SOPA/PIPA
Visiting Research Ulrich Matter (https://wwz.unibas.ch/en/people/profile/person/matter/) and Alois Stutzer write in their new paper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2016/PolicyPositionsonSOPA-PIPA) about the role that "public attention plays in determining the effect that campaign contributions funded by interests groups have on legislators’ policy positions."
https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias
How Can Tech Companies Protect Our Safety and Civil Liberties?
J. Nathan Matias (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias)  coauthored this Medium post (https://medium.com/mit-media-lab/the-obligation-to-experiment-83092256c3e9#.7fgq6vnf6)  about the obligation of technology companies to experiment in order to mitigate risks to users. In it, he writes, "where a common use of technology poses substantial risk or benefit to life or liberty, it should be tested systematically" and "the results of those tests should be public knowledge."

------------------------------------------------------------
https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swatson
There is No Neutral Interface
Sarah M. Watson (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swatson) contributed to the Nieman Lab's 2017 "Predictions for Journalism" series with a piece about individualized interfaces (http://www.niemanlab.org/2016/12/there-is-no-neutral-interface/) . Watson asks: "What are we optimizing for? Is it for civic responsibility? Personal relevance? Quality? Truthiness? Diversity of sources and viewpoints? Time on site?"

------------------------------------------------------------
https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz
Patently Biased: Commercialization of Scientific Research
In this piece for Jacobin (https://www.jacobinmag.com/2016/12/science-journalism-crispr-patents-research-funding/) , Yarden Katz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz) writes about the role the media plays in the commercialization of academic biomedical research, including the patent battle over the genome-editing technique CRISPR.
