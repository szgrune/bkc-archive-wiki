** Spike in Online Gig Work: Flash in the Pan or Future of Employment?
------------------------------------------------------------
"Pay attention to online gig work because it is dramatically reshaping our society," write Mary Gray (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mgray)  and Siddharth Suri in this post (https://socialmediacollective.org/2016/11/17/spike-in-online-gig-work-flash-in-the-pan-or-future-of-employment/) on the Social Media Collective Research Blog discussing a new study (http://www.pewinternet.org/2016/11/17/gig-work-online-selling-and-home-sharing/) by the Pew Research Center for Internet, Science and Tech. "Pew’s study is robust proof that the world of work — what we spend most of our time doing — is quickly moving online... There is no evidence that this trend will reverse and every indication that the move to on-demand gig work is well underway."

** Mark Zuckerberg Is in Denial.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (http://www.nytimes.com/2016/11/15/opinion/mark-zuckerberg-is-in-denial.html) for The New York Times, Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep)  argues that the Facebook CEO's assertion that fake news had no impact on the election is not only doing damage to American democracy, but also contradict's the company's own research.

(And on a related note, in this series of tweets (https://storify.com/zittrain/of-fake-news-and-filter-bubbles) , Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) shares his thoughts on the "shallow" and "deep" problems of fake news on platforms like Google and Facebook.)

** Investigating Surveillance Around Standing Rock.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this post (https://www.justsecurity.org/34449/investigating-surveillance-standing-rock/) for Just Security, Mailyn Fidler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mfidler)  and Collin Anderson report on their findings from tracking surveillance of the pipeline protesters since August. "Regardless of one’s opinion of the Dakota Access pipeline or the protests against it, the rules governing local and state use of electronic surveillance equipment in the US are opaque, piecemeal, and subject to fluctuations in the political climate."
