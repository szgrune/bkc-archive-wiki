The Black Pete Tradition.
------------------------------------------------------------

Open Access Week (Oct 24-28).
------------------------------------------------------------
In this blog post (https://plus.google.com/+PeterSuber/posts/QWBnpPmcp1Y) , Peter Suber (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/psuber) encourages people to talk to colleagues about Open Access and suggests some readings for those who want to learn more. "But lead with face-to-face conversation, not readings. Welcome the back and forth. Show that you can answer the actual concerns your colleague may have, not to mention the frequently heard questions, objections, and misunderstandings. Help your colleague understand that there's a serious problem and a beautiful solution."

Mass hacks of private emails.
------------------------------------------------------------
Recent mass hacks of private emails aren't whistleblowing, they are at odds with it, argues Jonathan ZIttrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) in this post (https://www.justsecurity.org/33677/mass-hacks-private-email-arent-whistleblowing-odds-it/) for Just Security. "[B]efore we consume the fast food meal of messages containing private citizens’ unguarded moments with friends and move on, we should consider the longer term effects on the body politic of partaking too often, and too indiscriminately."
Watch: Machine Learning Makes Human Morals more Important

http://www.ted.com/talks/zeynep_tufekci_we_can_t_control_what_our_intelligent_machines_are_learning

Machine intelligence is here, and we're already using it to make subjective decisions. But the complex way AI grows and improves makes it hard to understand and even harder to control. In this cautionary talk, (http://www.ted.com/talks/zeynep_tufekci_we_can_t_control_what_our_intelligent_machines_are_learning) techno-sociologist Zeynep Tufekci explains how intelligent machines can fail in ways that don't fit human error patterns — and in ways we won't expect or be prepared for.

Listen: A Proposal To Protect Your Privacy On The Internet

http://www.wbur.org/radioboston/2016/10/18/privacy-internet-zittrainWe tell our physicians some of the most private things about our lives, and we hold them to a specific set of ethical standards. Increasingly, we are giving Internet companies that very same same personal information and much more.

On this episode (http://www.wbur.org/radioboston/2016/10/18/privacy-internet-zittrain) of WBUR's Radio Boston, Jonathan Zittrain discusses an idea he wrote about recently (http://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2016/10/information-fiduciary/502346/) in The Atlantic: What if we held online companies to similar standards as our physicians?
