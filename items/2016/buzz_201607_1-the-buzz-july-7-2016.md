** Diversity and freedom of expression.
------------------------------------------------------------
“The American experiment is, at its highest form, about diversity and free expression coexisting,” writes faculty co-director John Palfrey in this essay (http://www.nytimes.com/2016/06/23/education/no-light-between-diversity-and-free-expression.html?_r=1)  in The New York Times. “The idea of diversity and free expression opposing one another may serve some immediate political purposes, but it will not serve our society in the long run.”
------------------------------------------------------------

** Privacy and Student Data.
------------------------------------------------------------
As technology becomes further integrated into classrooms, schools must remain alert to growing privacy concerns for sensitive student information.This new paper (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/node/99515) from the Cyberlaw Clinic (http://cyberlawclinic.berkman.harvard.edu/) , Youth and Media (http://youthandmedia.org/) , and the Student Privacy Initiative (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/research/studentprivacy) , written by Dalia Topelson Ritvo (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/dritvo) , provides an overview of federal laws impacting student information collected through networked technologies.

** Landlords and your Internet access.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (https://backchannel.com/the-new-payola-deals-landlords-cut-with-internet-providers-cf60200aa9e9#.o9w3lrjhz) for Backchannel, faculty co-director Susan Crawford describes an “enormous, decentralized payola scheme” among network operators like Comcast and developers and landlords that keeps Internet access prices high and choices few for millions of tenants.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhTb3pKjfzA

** Going Dark or Getting Personal? The Battle Between Data, Privacy & Intrusion
------------------------------------------------------------

On this episode (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhTb3pKjfzA) of the The Legal Edition, Bruce Schneier and David O'Brien discuss the the recent report "Don't Panic: Making Progress on the "Going Dark" Debate (https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/pubrelease/dont-panic/) ," the role of law enforcement and privacy rights, revelations of government spying, and offer analysis of the iPhone encryption litigation.
