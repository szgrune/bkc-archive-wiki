** Georgetown's slavery announcement.
------------------------------------------------------------
"Let me be clear," writes Tressie McMillan Cottom (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom) in this piece (http://www.vox.com/2016/9/2/12773110/georgetown-slavery-admission-reparations) for Vox about Georgetown's recent announcement of plans to "reconcile" its role in benefitting from slavery. "Excavating this history and acknowledging how it shapes present inequalities is important. In this context, Georgetown’s plan is a fine thing. It is not, however, reparations."

Pokémon GO and our future.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this piece (http://www.kmworld.com/Articles/Column/David-Weinberger/Perspective-on-knowledge-Pok%C3%A9mon-GO-is-our-future-113125.aspx) for KMWorld, David Weinberger (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dweinberger) explains how Pokémon GO is more than a game. "It’s a reframing of mobile computers. They were screens. Now they’re windows," he writes. "In this case it’s Pokémon characters, but in the next case it will be anything that Augmented Reality can dream up."

Where “Digital India” Ends.
------------------------------------------------------------
"There has been no mobile internet in Indian-administered Kashmir for nearly two months. What would be a disaster for most Americans has become the mundane reality for millions of Kashmiris," writes Hasit Shah (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/hshah) in this piece (http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/future_tense/2016/09/india_champion_of_web_access_cuts_off_mobile_internet_in_kashmir.html) for Slate that describes the country's "deliberate restrictions on internet access [that] contrast sharply with India’s otherwise forward-thinking technology polices."

** https://cyber.law.harvard.edu/people/bschneier
New leaks and the NSA.
------------------------------------------------------------
The National Security Agency is lying to us," writes Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) in this piece (http://www.vox.com/2016/8/24/12615258/nsa-security-breach-hoard) for Vox. "We know that because of data stolen from an NSA server was dumped on the internet. The agency is hoarding information about security vulnerabilities in the products you use, because it wants to use it to hack others' computers. Those vulnerabilities aren't being reported, and aren't getting fixed, making your computers and networks unsafe."
