New strategies for securing our private lives.
------------------------------------------------------------
"If the media is understandably going to publish newsworthy stuff, regardless of its provenance, and people are going to continue to use email and other communications that leave a record, what should be done?" In this piece (https://www.lawfareblog.com/new-strategies-securing-our-private-lives) for Lawfare,Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) offers some tactical short-term ways to mitigate risk.

Countering dangerous speech in a season of Trump.
------------------------------------------------------------
“Trump may well be undermining the extent to which his supporters trust the essential institutions and practices of U.S. democracy,” according to Susan Benesch (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sbenesch) , quoted in this profile (https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/inspired-life/wp/2016/10/24/this-professor-devotes-her-life-to-countering-dangerous-speech-she-cant-ignore-donald-trumps/) in The Washington Post. “Some of them — those who are most susceptible to being inflamed by such messages — may therefore be more likely to commit violence."

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz
How the press covers science.

In this essay (http://www.3ammagazine.com/3am/cheerleading-agenda-press-covers-science/) for 3:AM Magazine, Yarden Katz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz) examines how science journalism often functions as a marketing service for powerful research institutes, and how the resulting coverage sidelines matters of public interest (such as the drive to corporatize academic research and the rise of IP in laboratories).
------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mfidler
Africans and Cross-Border Data Access Reform.
------------------------------------------------------------
Inthis piece (http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2016/10/26/africans-want-cross-border-data-access-reform-but-they-might-get-left-out/) for the Council on Foreign Relations Net Politics blog, Mailyn Fidler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mfidler) reports on a growing concern about African data access problems at the 2016 Forum on Internet Freedom in Africa and warns that countries "left out" of the process for cross-border data requests "will have few viable options for accessing data and may turn to damaging alternatives."

------------------------------------------------------------

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier
What journalists need to know about hacking.
------------------------------------------------------------
In this interview (http://journalistsresource.org/studies/society/internet/hacking-bruce-schneier-journalists-cyberattacks-ddos) with the Journalists' Resource, Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) shares what aspects of digital security and hacking he thinks journalists should be paying attention to, red flags to look out for, and the technical concerns he has for Election Day.

** https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford
What last week's Internet shut down really means.
------------------------------------------------------------
"The Internet of Things era is already here. It could take down our cities if we let it," writes Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) in this piece (https://backchannel.com/the-internet-of-things-is-taking-over-cities-8ca5ffa013ac#.p5u22mbqc) for Backchannel. "Cities are poised to privatize information flowing from the public right of way — data relating to streets, sidewalks, and uses of public infrastructure — without much policy work or consensus about what values they’re trying to serve."
