** Isolating malicious AIs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15499)
------------------------------------------------------------

With a nod to nuclear containment, BKC Director James Mickens, Sarah Radway, and Ravi Netravali treat advanced AI as a potentially rogue actor, proposing “Guillotine, a hypervisor architecture for sandboxing powerful AI models…” Guillotine would be a digital containment chamber—air-gapped, hardware-enforced, and fail-safed. ------------------------------------------------------------

At a workshop organized by Faculty Associate Elettra Bietti, Faculty Associate Aileen Nielsen, and Laura Aade, participants—including a passel of BKC community members—explored how digital platforms commodify attention. The event framed attention as a legal, clinical, and design challenge that is ripe for its own field of study. ** Feeling watched (https://thehumanist.com/features/articles/when-artificial-intelligence-meets-emotional-intelligence)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Magda Romanska reflects on how the growing use of AI to read emotions echoes past surveillance regimes. Drawing from her childhood in communist Poland, she warns that emotion-tracking tech threatens cognitive liberty. “If AI can read us like an open book, without the need for our consent, what effect does this have on...the entire trajectory of our lives?” Read here » (https://thehumanist.com/features/articles/when-artificial-intelligence-meets-emotional-intelligence)

https://www.techpolicy.press/trumps-social-media-surveillance-social-scoring-by-another-name/

** Post punishment (https://www.techpolicy.press/trumps-social-media-surveillance-social-scoring-by-another-name/)
------------------------------------------------------------

What if a tweet could cost you a visa? Faculty Associate Petra Molnar warns that AI-driven visa revocation programs echo China’s social credit system. By targeting online speech, these tools turn digital expression into grounds for punishment, especially for marginalized communities. Read here » (https://www.techpolicy.press/trumps-social-media-surveillance-social-scoring-by-another-name/)

** Normal technology (https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/18/podcasts/meta-on-trial-is-ai-a-normal-technology-hatgpt.html?)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Arvind Narayanan joined the New York Times podcast Hard Fork to unpack his paper with Sayash Kapoor, AI as Normal Technology (https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology) , which challenges utopic and dystopic notions of AI. “Power is not just a property of the AI system itself, it's a property both of the AI system and the environment in which it is deployed. And that environment is something that we control...” Listen here » (https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/18/podcasts/meta-on-trial-is-ai-a-normal-technology-hatgpt.html?)
