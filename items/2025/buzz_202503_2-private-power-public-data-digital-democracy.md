** Data danger (https://theconversation.com/doge-threat-how-government-data-would-give-an-ai-company-extraordinary-power-250907)
------------------------------------------------------------

As “DOGE” captures more and more government data, triggering concerns around automation, security, accountability, and privacy, RSM Visiting Scholar Allison Stanger warns against the blurring of public and private power. “The threat of a private company accessing government data transcends individual privacy concerns.” Read here » (https://theconversation.com/doge-threat-how-government-data-would-give-an-ai-company-extraordinary-power-250907)

** AI disaster (https://www.techpolicy.press/doge-plan-to-push-ai-across-the-us-federal-government-is-wildly-dangerous/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Ben Green argues that the “AI-first strategy” of “DOGE” is hubristic and alarming. “[W]e can look at prior examples of integrating AI into government decision-making and operations. There are already many instances of AI-driven governance gone wrong—particularly in cases where the goal is to root out fraud and cut budgets.” Read here » (https://www.techpolicy.press/doge-plan-to-push-ai-across-the-us-federal-government-is-wildly-dangerous/)

https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/data-colonialism--and-the-political-economy-of-big-tech

** Data colonialism (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/data-colonialism--and-the-political-economy-of-big-tech)
------------------------------------------------------------

Affiliate Chinmayi Arun reviews Ulises Mejias and Faculty Associate Nick Couldry's Data Grab: The New Colonialism of Big Tech and How to Fight Back, digging into the extractive practices of major tech companies. “[Mejias and Couldy] show how the legacy of colonialism means that harms are unevenly distributed.” Read here » (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/data-colonialism--and-the-political-economy-of-big-tech)

https://www.techpolicy.press/threats-to-democracy-in-brazil-the-rise-of-technofeudalism-and-the-assault-on-democratic-institutions/

** Assault on democracy (https://www.techpolicy.press/threats-to-democracy-in-brazil-the-rise-of-technofeudalism-and-the-assault-on-democratic-institutions/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate David Nemer breaks down how digital monopolies erode democracy in Brazil, fueling inequality, precarious labor, and disinformation. “If companies like X, Meta, and Google can ignore judicial rulings and manipulate political discourse, then the very foundation of democracy—accountability, transparency, and equal representation—is at risk.” Read here » (https://www.techpolicy.press/threats-to-democracy-in-brazil-the-rise-of-technofeudalism-and-the-assault-on-democratic-institutions/)

https://www.plutobooks.com/9780745351216/beyond-the-final-whistle/

** BKC Happenings
------------------------------------------------------------

In a journal article about storytelling (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14759551.2025.2454377) , Faculty Associate Vasilis Kostakis (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkostakis) and his coauthors ask what kinds of writing can contribute to meaningful change. Vasilis answers with a new book, Beyond the Final Whistle: Soccer for a Better World (https://www.plutobooks.com/9780745351216/beyond-the-final-whistle/) , that explores a new philosophy of football, showing how sport could lead to social change and liberation.
The application deadline for BKC's new program with HLS, AI and the Law: Navigating the New Legal Landscape (https://go.execed.law.harvard.edu/l/868411/2024-12-10/n7rfhn) , has been extended to Friday, March 28.
