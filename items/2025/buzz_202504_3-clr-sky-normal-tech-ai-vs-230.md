** CLR:SKY (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2025-04/asml-fellow-launches-clrsky)
------------------------------------------------------------

ASML Fellow Fay Johnson launches CLR:SKY (http://www.clrsky.ai/) , an AI-powered civility overlay for the Bluesky platform grounded in behavioral science. The tool offers real-time feedback on post toxicity, suggests empathetic rewrites, and encourages perspective-taking to foster healthier online conversations. Read Johnson's full intro to CLR:SKY here » (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2025-04/asml-fellow-launches-clrsky)

** Normal technology (https://www.aisnakeoil.com/p/ai-as-normal-technology)
------------------------------------------------------------

With Sayash Kapoor, Faculty Associate Arvind Narayanan challenges notions of AI as an existential threat. They view AI as a “normal” technology, akin to electricity: "To view AI as normal is not to understate its impact…But it is in contrast to both utopian and dystopian visions of the future of AI which have a common tendency to treat it akin to a separate species.” Read here » (https://www.aisnakeoil.com/p/ai-as-normal-technology)

** Beyond Section 230 (https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/beyond-section-230-principles-for-ai-governance/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In a Harvard Law Review note, Affiliate Dylan Moses explores how generative AI challenges legal frameworks long relied on to manage platform liability, arguing that we can’t afford to repeat mistakes made in an earlier internet era. He offers a set of high-level principles for navigating the next phase of AI governance. Read here » (https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/beyond-section-230-principles-for-ai-governance/)

** Automated guidance (https://islamiclaw.blog/2025/04/10/roundtable-chatgpt-and-the-marjaʿ/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Zahra Takhshid explores how large language models will impact authority in Shia Islam. While AI can assist with routine religious queries, it lacks contextual understanding. “[I]t is now the role of scholars to engage with these important questions on the feasibility of relying on LLMs for religious inquiries…” Read here » (https://islamiclaw.blog/2025/04/10/roundtable-chatgpt-and-the-marjaʿ/)

** BKC Happenings
------------------------------------------------------------

Congratulations to Fellow Caio Vieira Machado (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/caio-vieira-machado) and coauthors on receiving Best Paper Award from the New England NLP Workshop at Yale! AI Alignment at Your Discretion (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10441v1) demonstrates that LLM models exercise discretion in principle-based AI alignment (constitutional AI) and, using pairwise preferences, discovers the values that annotators (humans and LLMs) prioritize.
