** Note from the Executive Director
------------------------------------------------------------

Hello, dear readers of the BKC Buzz.

I’m Alex Pascal (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alex-pascal) , the Berkman Klein Center’s new Executive Director. It’s the Center’s annual “launch week,” and our community, near and far, is gathered to kick off the academic year, renew connections, and explore new collaborations and initiatives. As this is my first “launch,” I had no idea what to expect. (I’m pictured, above, listening to James Mickens (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jmickens) during one of the launch panels.) For years, I’ve been hearing from friends and colleagues what an incredible, dynamic, and warm community BKC is. It’s been amazing to see BKC alive with so many students and community members buzzing (no pun intended) around our spaces, engaging in generative conversations, and building new relationships.

I’m thrilled to be joining BKC at such a pivotal moment, as rapid and sweeping changes in digital technology raise profound questions, challenges, and, if we can harness it right, opportunities for us all. As a policymaker and scholar, I was drawn to BKC because it felt like the rare place where people were asking the same hard questions I was—and doing so with rigor, creativity, and a deep sense of public purpose. It’s been so energizing to see these core BKC traits on display this week—and such an honor to lead the Center as we work to produce new ideas, initiatives, collaborations, and technologies that make people’s lives better and improve the lived experience of technology.

I’m not the only new member of the team this year. Josh Joseph (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/joshua-joseph) joined BKC as our first Chief AI Scientist. Josh and others will be leading exciting research that investigates both the promises and problems of AI, as previewed in our recent annual report (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/bkc2024-25update) : How should AI agents be designed and governed? How might we conceptualize, understand, and measure AI’s features of “mind” beyond “intelligence?” How do AI systems understand and perceive us, and should we have a say in that? How is AI affecting or even changing the experience of being human?

Alongside burning questions about artificial intelligence, BKC’s Applied Social Media Lab (https://asml.cyber.harvard.edu/) continues its public interest technology development work under Senior Director Meg Marco (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/meg-marco) . ASML’s current tooling projects explore topics ranging from online safety and transparency to interoperability, public discourse, and the secondary effects of AI.

Finally, we’re delighted to kick off our Fall Speaker Series with What Is Intelligence? Lessons from AI About Evolution, Computing, and Minds (https://cyber.harvard.edu/events/what-intelligence-lessons-ai-about-evolution-computing-and-minds) with Blaise Agüera y Arcas, on Wednesday, September 24. Stay tuned for future events and details.

We’re so grateful for our readers and broader community. I look forward to engaging with you at our events—and at large—throughout the year.

—Alex

P.S. The Buzz got a small glow-up; we hope you like it. You can expect to hear more about the research, people, and tools that are driving BKC forward—every other week

Robots, rights, and remix culture: This summer Harvard Law School Executive Education and the Berkman Klein Center launched AI and the Law: Navigating the New Legal Landscape (https://hls.harvard.edu/executive-education/programs/in-person-programs/ai-and-the-law-navigating-the-new-legal-landscape/) . ** Digital Policy Roundup:
Navigate 2025 Recap
------------------------------------------------------------

The future of digital policy can feel uncertain, with pressing concerns around privacy, cybersecurity, and online safety demanding bold solutions. At BKC and IAPP’s inaugural Navigate: Digital Policy Leadership Retreat (https://iapp.org/conference/navigate-leadership-retreat/) , Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) struck an optimistic note: “The cavalry’s coming,” he told participants, before adding with a grin, “the bad news is, you’re the cavalry.” And the cavalry rose to the occasion. Leaders from across geographies, sectors, and perspectives came together to tackle some of today’s thorniest digital dilemmas, from copyright regimes for AI training to the proliferation of non-consensual images online. Learn more (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2025-08/inside-navigate-retreat-thats-redefining-digital-leadership) about what today’s digital policy leaders discussed.

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

The Buzz was on hiatus this summer, but the BKC community was active, publishing, speaking, and traveling. Here’s a (tiny!) sample:

2024-2025 Rebooting Social Media Visiting Scholar Noah Giansiracusa (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/noah-giansiracusa) ’s book Robin Hood Math: Take Control of the Algorithms That Run Your Life (https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/744578/robin-hood-math-by-noah-giansiracusa/) arrived with much fanfare. He suggests some practical ways that individuals can attempt to mitigate the impact of tech and financial algorithms on their lives. Read an excerpt, How the Secret Algorithms Behind Social Media Actually Work (https://time.com/7308120/secret-algorithms-behind-social-media/) , in TIME, and check out Harvard Law Today’s eye-opening interview with Noah (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/how-delta-airlines-and-other-companies-use-dynamic-pricing-to-determine-how-much-you-pay/) about dynamic pricing.

BKC Faculty Chair Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) visited the Before AGI podcast for an expansive discussion of the growing challenges—legal, ethical, societal—raised by AI agents acting on behalf of people (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KeoGZLlfMig) .

In the CommonWealth Beacon, Executive Director Alex Pascal (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alex-pascal) and Affiliate Nathan Sanders (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/nathan-sanders) argued for Massachusetts to take the lead on state regulation of AI and to resist Congress’s efforts to keep states from doing so. Read their opinion piece: Mass. must resist Congress’s proposed moratorium on state AI regulation (https://commonwealthbeacon.org/opinion/mass-must-resist-congresss-proposed-moratorium-on-state-ai-regulation/) .

With Chelse Swoopes and Elena Glassman, Faculty Associate Aileen Nielsen (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/aileen-nielsen) posted a new paper that critically engages with interface design in jurists’ use of LLMs. They argue that “thoughtfully designed, AI-resilient interfaces” could remedy “problems of opacity, reproducibility, and conceptual brittleness”; they reject the use of consumer-facing chat interfaces in adjudication. Read their Law is vulnerable to AI influence; interface design can help (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5387231) on SSRN.

Tech Policy Press featured a condensed version (https://www.techpolicy.press/policy-directions-on-encrypted-messaging-and-extreme-speech/) of Encrypted Messaging and Extreme Speech: Policy Directions (https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/127250/1/Encrypted_Messaging_and_Extreme_Speech_Policy_Report.pdf) , a new report from an international research group including 2024-2025 Fellow Sahana Udupa (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sahana-udupa) . Amid perennial government efforts to legislate access to encrypted data, Sahana and her collaborators offer recommendations for making encrypted messaging platforms safe, secure, and aligned with human rights principles.

Last but not least, Faculty Associate Paola Ricaurte (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/paola-ricaurte) was included in the TIME100 AI 2025 (https://time.com/collections/time100-ai-2025/7305857/paola-ricaurte-quijano/) , TIME’s list of the 100 most influential people in AI: “Ricaurte has risen to become a leading voice challenging today’s AI industry dynamics and imagining an alternative that places greater weight on the perspectives of the marginalized.” 👏

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
