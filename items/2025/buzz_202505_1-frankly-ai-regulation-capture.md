** Introducing Frankly (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2025-04/asml-invites-open-source-collaborators-to-frankly)
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC's Applied Social Media Lab has open-sourced Frankly, a video-based platform designed to foster constructive dialogue. Under Professor Lawrence Lessig’s direction, Frankly offers features like structured discussion guides and group matching across perspectives. Frankly is now inviting contributions from developers, civic organizations, and public interest technologists. Read here » (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2025-04/asml-invites-open-source-collaborators-to-frankly)

** The Silicon Valley effect (https://law.stanford.edu/publications/the-silicon-valley-effect/)
------------------------------------------------------------

“The most influential AI companies are shaping AI’s legal order and regulatory discourse to protect their business interests…” Affiliate Chinmayi Arun critiques Big Tech's outsized influence on global AI regulation and argues that this dominance sidelines marginalized voices. Read here » (https://law.stanford.edu/publications/the-silicon-valley-effect/)

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4736131

** Smoke & Mirrors (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4736131)
------------------------------------------------------------

Affiliate Maroussia Lévesque criticizes the prevailing model of AI governance, where major tech firms are both regulators and regulated. While the EU's enforcement mechanisms offer some promise, she calls for US policymakers to reclaim authority and ensure AI serves society. “Policymakers must reckon with industry capture.” Read here » (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4736131)

https://www.techpolicy.press/ai-regulation-versus-ai-innovation-a-fake-dichotomy/

** Fake dichotomy (https://www.techpolicy.press/ai-regulation-versus-ai-innovation-a-fake-dichotomy/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Virgílio Almeida and coauthors argue that framing regulation and innovation as opposing forces is misleading. This narrative serves corporate interests by resisting oversight and threatening democratic institutions. “We have the choice between algorithms that can replace inclusive institutions or algorithms that reflect a process of extractive institutionalization.” Read here » (https://www.techpolicy.press/ai-regulation-versus-ai-innovation-a-fake-dichotomy/)

https://www.ipie.info/research/sr2024-3

** Audit overhaul (https://www.ipie.info/research/sr2024-3)
------------------------------------------------------------

The International Panel on the Information Environment’s latest report, chaired by Affiliate Wendy Chun, critiques AI audits for lacking independence, evidence, and global perspective. A panel of experts, including Faculty Associate Christo Wilson, propose new frameworks to ensure AI systems are evaluated for real-world risks. Read here » (https://www.ipie.info/research/sr2024-3)

** BKC Happenings
------------------------------------------------------------

Fellow Upol Ehsan (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/upol-ehsan) co-organized his fifth Human-Centered XAI Workshop (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/uehsan_hcxai-chi2025-activity-7321728139720642560-FY5B/) at CHI! This year’s main event featured Maryam Ashoori (IBM), Faculty Associate Mary Gray (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mgray) (Microsoft), and Jacob Metcalf (Data & Society).

RSM Visiting Scholar AJ Christian (https://communication.northwestern.edu/faculty/aymar-jean-christian.html) and ASML Fellow Rudy Fraser (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rudy-fraser) , creator of BlackSky, recently discussed (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QkOeQHjml6I) how BlackSky blends open-source tech, community care, and ancestral practices to build a new social media experience.

Harvard Divinity School's Religion and Public Life program hosted the Humanity Meets AI symposium (https://rpl.hds.harvard.edu/news/2025/04/18/video-humanity-meets-ai-symposium-ai-and-religion) , delving into the intersection of AI and religion. The event featured scholars, technologists, and ethicists including Affiliate Jenn Louie (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jenn-louie) .
