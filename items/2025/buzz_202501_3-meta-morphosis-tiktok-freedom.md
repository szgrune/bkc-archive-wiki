** "Dumbest timeline" (https://www.techpolicy.press/the-dumbest-timeline-the-supreme-court-rules-on-tiktok/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Kate Klonick discusses the Supreme Court's ruling on TikTok. "They preface this entire decision saying, 'This is a very narrow and rushed opinion and so do not use it for much,' but that hasn't ever stopped lawyers. So we'll see what it does. It's not a great day for the First Amendment and freedom of expression versus plausible claims of national security threats." ** Dead in the water (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-the-tiktok-ban-is-already-dead-in-the-water/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Jon Penny argues that the law banning TikTok has failed. "[A]s of this writing, TikTok remains accessible in the U.S. But it has failed even if it eventually succeeds in forcing ByteDance...to sell the app. That’s because all the threats the law aims to protect against – privacy, national security, and Chinese geopolitical influence – would remain." Read here » (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-the-tiktok-ban-is-already-dead-in-the-water/)

https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/01/internet-censorship-tiktok-ban/681361/

** America's free internet? (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/01/internet-censorship-tiktok-ban/681361/)
------------------------------------------------------------

With its increased restrictions, the US faces scrutiny over internet freedom. Faculty Associate Ethan Zuckerman discusses the widening opinion gap between the country's politicians and its public. "I'm encouraged that American TikTok users realize that banning the popular platform directly contradicts America's values. If only America's leaders were so wise." Read here » (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/01/internet-censorship-tiktok-ban/681361/)

** Online child stars (https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/01/10/opinion/protecting-child-stars-digital-age/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Leah Plunkett tackles the unregulated world of child influencers. "Where is the line between children appearing occasionally in online family videos and child labor? Which practices violate privacy rights and which ones go beyond that? Our legal and political systems are navigating...new questions." ** Fair use post-Warhol (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2025/01/22/cyberlaw-clinic-files-amicus-brief-for-authors-alliance-in-favor-of-a-flexible-fair-use-doctrine-post-warhol/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Cyberlaw Clinic filed an amicus brief on behalf of Authors Alliance in Sedlik v. Von Drachenberg. "The brief supports Katherine von Drachenburg, a celebrity tattoo artist who gave her friend a tattoo based on a photograph of Miles Davis taken by photographer Jeff Sedlik," arguing that "Warhol should be interpreted and applied to support a robust conception of fair use."
