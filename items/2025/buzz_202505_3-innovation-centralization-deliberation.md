------------------------------------------------------------

On an episode of Lawfare Daily, Fellow Ben Brooks discusses the rise of open source AI and its double-edged impact. While openness boosts innovation, it also exposes national security risks. “I would love to see a clear recognition that safe diffusion is how we're going to boost productivity and innovation.” Listen here » (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/lawfare-daily--ben-brooks-on-the-rise-of-open-source-ai)

** Stifling state innovation (https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2025/05/14/artificial-intelligence-regulation-congress-reconciliation/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Scott Brennen and Affiliate Zeve Sanderson criticize an “alarming provision” in a US House committee's budget reconciliation bill: a ten-year ban on state regulation of AI. This would undo vital protections and halt needed regulatory experimentation, they argue. “The proposed moratorium on state AI policymaking would be far worse than Congress’s inaction on the issue.” Read here » (https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2025/05/14/artificial-intelligence-regulation-congress-reconciliation/)

------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Salomé Viljoen posits that the political right has embraced data governance, pointing to the Trump administration's efforts to centralize data systems for enforcing policies like immigrant exclusion and union tracking. “So far, the left has mostly been playing defense against this powerful vector of governance…” Read here » (https://lpeproject.org/blog/the-right-understands-that-all-governance-is-data-governance/)

** Transparency tenets (https://iddp.gwu.edu/case-transparency)
------------------------------------------------------------

Affiliate Brandon Silverman and Naomi Shiffman advocate for stronger platform transparency, citing case studies where open data access helped expose disinformation, shape policy, and protect elections. As platforms tighten access, Silerman and Shiffman warn civil society is being locked out and call for greater accountability. Read here » (https://iddp.gwu.edu/case-transparency)

** Deliberative democracy (https://www.mediatechdemocracy.com/conversation-networks)
------------------------------------------------------------

“The need for meaningful civic discourse has never been greater, and the tools to support it are within reach.” With Audrey Tang, Faculty Associates Lawrence Lessig and Deb Roy contributed an essay on “Conversation Networks” to a new series (https://www.mediatechdemocracy.com/deliberative-approaches-to-inclusive-governance) from McGill's Centre for Media, Technology and Democracy, exploring how deliberative democratic processes can strengthen AI governance. Read here » (https://www.mediatechdemocracy.com/conversation-networks)
