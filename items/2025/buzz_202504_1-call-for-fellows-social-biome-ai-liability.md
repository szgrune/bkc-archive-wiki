** AI liability (https://blog.mozilla.org/netpolicy/2025/04/01/new-mozilla-research-civil-liability-along-the-ai-value-chain/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Beatriz Botero Arcila tackles the thorny question of who’s on the hook when AI goes wrong. Her new report, commissioned by Mozilla, outlines a three-step liability framework to ensure that accountability and innovation stay on track—and that individuals have effective ways of seeking redress and justice for harms. Read here » (https://blog.mozilla.org/netpolicy/2025/04/01/new-mozilla-research-civil-liability-along-the-ai-value-chain/)

https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/03/28/signal-chat-leak-trump-technology-security-houthis-group-defense-nsa/

** Leaked chats (https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/03/28/signal-chat-leak-trump-technology-security-houthis-group-defense-nsa/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In Foreign Policy, Affiliate Bruce Schneier explains why a leaked Signal chat among US troops could reshape how we think about secure communications. He warns of the growing national security risks tied to everyday encryption tools. Read here » (https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/03/28/signal-chat-leak-trump-technology-security-houthis-group-defense-nsa/)

Plus, Schneier and Davi Ottenheimer argue that a Web 3.0 future demands something more fundamental than decentralization: data integrity (https://cacm.acm.org/opinion/web-3-0-requires-data-integrity/) .

https://iapp.org/conference/navigate-leadership-retreat/

** BKC Happenings
------------------------------------------------------------

Registration is now open for Navigate: A Digital Policy Leadership Retreat (https://iapp.org/conference/navigate-leadership-retreat/) , presented by the IAPP and BKC, June 25–26 in Portsmouth, New Hampshire. Participants will explore the complex web of digital regulation, risk, and responsibility.

In publications news, Faculty Associate Mark Esposito coauthored a new book on Digitalization in Emerging Economies (https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/digitalization-in-emerging-economies/9E5DD6BDFCB457854464760AADBFBA95#access-block) .

Library Innovation Lab Fellow Katy Gero won a Best Paper Award (https://programs.sigchi.org/chi/2025/program/content/188767) at ACM CHI for her work (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14281) on how creative writers view their role and rights in training LLMs. And Century Scale Storage (https://lil.law.harvard.edu/century-scale-storage/) , an essay by Library Innovation Lab Fellow Maxwell Neely-Cohen, designed by Shelby Wilson and Alexander Miller, was nominated for a Webby for Best Individual Editorial Feature—vote here (https://vote.webbyawards.com/PublicVoting#/2025/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-individual-editorial-feature-indbrandorg) !
