** Missing guardrails (https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/society/2024/why-regulating-ai-is-so-hard-and-so-necessary)
------------------------------------------------------------

RSM Visiting Scholar Allison Stanger advocates for regulatory guardrails in the AI landscape. "It's not going to be the companies — that's not their job. It's the government's job to look out for the common good and ensure that companies aren't overstepping certain boundaries and harming people." Read here » (https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/society/2024/why-regulating-ai-is-so-hard-and-so-necessary)

** Silicon Valley effect (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5109858)
------------------------------------------------------------

Affiliate Chinmayi Arun lays out AI's emerging legal order and shows how relying solely on domestic regulations is shortsighted. "The major AI companies rely on global value chains and global markets. Capitalism drives them to exploit and experiment on vulnerable populations in permissive regulatory environments." Read here » (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5109858)

https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-will-write-complex-laws

** Drafting complexities (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-will-write-complex-laws)
------------------------------------------------------------

AI's role in drafting laws could revolutionize policymaking, but at what cost? Affiliates Nathan Sanders and Bruce Schneier explain that "There are real risks to AI-written law, but those risks are not dramatically different from what we endure today. AI-written law trying to optimize for certain policy outcomes may get it wrong (just as many human-written laws are misguided). Read here » (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-will-write-complex-laws)
