** Digital Crime Scenes (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2022/digital-crime-scenes)
------------------------------------------------------------

In this report, Afsaneh Rigot draws on years of in-depth research to understand the role of digital evidence in the persecution of LGBTQ people in Egypt, Lebanon, and Tunisia. The report concludes with recommendations for protecting vulnerable users, including steps that tech companies can take. Read the report. (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2022/digital-crime-scenes)

** Improving Data Security Laws (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4043111)
------------------------------------------------------------

Data breaches have become more frequent as digital connections have become pervasive. How can laws surrounding these breaches be better? Daniel Solove and Woodrow Hartzog have devised a series of recommendations in Breached! Why Data Security Law Fails and How to Improve It. Check out Chapter 1. (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4043111)

** Futures of Digital Governance (https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2022/3/258905-futures-of-digital-governance/fulltext)
------------------------------------------------------------

Urs Gasser and Virgílio Almeida take a historical look at the evolution of Internet governance to help inform the future. "While innovation is typically associated with technology development, the scale, scope, and speed of next-generation digital technologies, such as the metaverse, point toward a need for another type of innovation: innovation in the realm of governance."

** Forged Vaccine Cards (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/03/why-covid-vaccine-cards-easily-forged/626971/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Cybersecurity expert Bruce Schneier explains that paper vaccine cards strike a good balance between ease and security. “We’re not looking for perfection. If most everyone follows the rules and doesn’t cheat, we win. Making these systems easy to use is the priority. The alternative just isn’t worth it.”

https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/columns/how-wikipedia-gets-to-define-whats-true-online-google-knowledge-graph

** Wikipedia in a Post-Truth Internet (https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/columns/how-wikipedia-gets-to-define-whats-true-online-google-knowledge-graph)
------------------------------------------------------------

Ethan Zuckerman traces Wikipedia's transformation from controversial to trusted source. "When seen as magical, Wikipedia becomes impossible to replicate. When seen as explicable, perhaps it could become something to emulate and reproduce elsewhere."
