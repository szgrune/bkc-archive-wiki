** Twitter vs. rocket science (https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/twitter-is-not-rocket-science-its-harder/)
------------------------------------------------------------

For Scientific American, Joe Bak-Coleman weighs the difference between engineering and the challenges of managing human behavior. "On a social network, interactions between individuals create dizzying feedback loops and chaotic interactions that render simple mathematical models next-to-useless for predicting the future, let alone controlling it."

https://clinics.law.harvard.edu/blog/2022/12/advocating-for-fair-and-transparent-data-practices/

** Toward fair data practices (https://clinics.law.harvard.edu/blog/2022/12/advocating-for-fair-and-transparent-data-practices/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Cyberlaw Clinic submitted a comment in response to a request from the FTC for feedback on an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking related to commercial surveillance and data privacy. “My hope is that if the FTC gets over the hurdles to policy making in this area, that we can enhance the effectiveness of any mechanisms they create, by translating insights from these longstanding [BKC] projects,” said Jessica Fjeld.

https://texaslawreview.org/8413-2/

** Self-regulating platforms (https://texaslawreview.org/8413-2/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Elettra Bietti explores the disintctions and assumptions around platforms' functioning in markets. "Digital platforms such as Facebook, Google, and Amazon are new market forms in the information economy...ambiguously referred to as highly efficient commercial spaces while at the same time being seen as sovereigns, governors, and constitutive spaces."

https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2022/11/21/in-brazil-twitter-users-fear-impact-of-musks-rule

** Inclusion and online speech (https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2022/11/21/in-brazil-twitter-users-fear-impact-of-musks-rule)
------------------------------------------------------------

David Nemer speaks to concerns about Twitter's lack of regulation and the spread of disinformation under Elon Musk's tenure, especially in the context of Brazilian politics. “Absolutism in freedom of expression is a bad thing in Brazil as it directly hits the core of democracy while it discourages people from different social strata, race and sexual orientation to be part of the platform.”

https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/12/cryptocurrency-mining-environmental-impact-solution/672360/

** Decarbonizing crypto (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/12/cryptocurrency-mining-environmental-impact-solution/672360/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier and Christos Porios write for The Atlantic about the opportunity that crypto litigation presents to curb its massive environmental impact. "The good news is that cryptocurrencies don’t have to be carbon intensive. In fact, some have near-zero emissions."
