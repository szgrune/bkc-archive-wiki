** Bridging systems (https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2023/01/11/social-media-can-be-polarizing-new-type-algorithm-aims-change-that/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In the Washington Post, Aviv Ovadya and co-author Luke Thorburn make the case for bridging systems (https://bridging.systems/) : social media algorithms that burst rather than enforce ideological bubbles. “Social media doesn’t have to divide us. It doesn’t have to incentivize outrage and hate.”

https://www.petramolnar.com/stories/technologies-of-violence-at-the-worlds-sharpest-edges

** Technologies of violence (https://www.petramolnar.com/stories/technologies-of-violence-at-the-worlds-sharpest-edges)
------------------------------------------------------------

Petra Molnar outlines the frontiers of technological violence, the theme of her forthcoming Artificial Borders (The New Press, 2024). “Trying to document this system of technological oppression is itself a risky business – one fraught with trying to unravel opaque government decisions, secretive private sector players, and witnessing horrific policy choices that challenge our common humanity.”

https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/01/06/online-disinformation-researcher-joan-donovan-on-the-power-of-memes

** The power of memes (https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/01/06/online-disinformation-researcher-joan-donovan-on-the-power-of-memes)
------------------------------------------------------------

Joan Donovan speaks about the history of memes in America and how the current power of memes threatens to undermine democracy. "Our culture has always had different ways that subculture signal to one another. Through the internet that kind of subcultural or identity expression has become very, very important, particularly signaling what kind of political subculture you might be involved in."
