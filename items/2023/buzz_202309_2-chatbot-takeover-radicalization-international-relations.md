** Crowdsourcing's decline (https://techpolicy.press/will-ai-degrade-online-communities/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kalie Mayberry explains how increased use of generative AI may degrade online communities. "Turning away from [crowdsourcing] forums in favor of generative AI chatbots may mean we engage increasingly only with ourselves, interacting primarily with this tool that we might start to provide humanoid attributes to, considering it a partner in our work."

https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk2031

** Extremism online (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk2031)
------------------------------------------------------------

Aaron Shaw describes the relationship between social media and extremism. "Engaging with and contributing to communities of like-minded extremists may not have caused these individuals to adopt such radicalized beliefs in the first place, but the social support that they found online may have catalyzed them to adopt even more extreme views and to take actions they once might have considered taboo."

https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/algorithmic-governance-and-the-international-politics-of-big-tech/3C04908735A5F2EE8A70AFED647741FB

** Algorithmic governance (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/algorithmic-governance-and-the-international-politics-of-big-tech/3C04908735A5F2EE8A70AFED647741FB)
------------------------------------------------------------

Swati Srivastava explains why international relations researchers should become more involved in the global research on algorithmic governance. "Global relations are increasingly undergirded by proprietary and opaque algorithms that organize the world’s information and connections."

https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/ideas/technology/62810/ai-artificial-intelligence-trains-itself-zuckerman

** AI's training material (https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/ideas/technology/62810/ai-artificial-intelligence-trains-itself-zuckerman)
------------------------------------------------------------

Ethan Zuckerman describes roadblocks to AI's continued training and learning. "Even if AI entrepreneurs can find their way through these legal thickets, another more existential barrier may cap the growth of large language models: the limits of human creativity."

https://niemanreports.org/articles/twitter-x-journalist-diversity/

** X versus diversity (https://niemanreports.org/articles/twitter-x-journalist-diversity/)
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Research Assistant Pratika Katiyar argues that X's new policies put diversity in journalism at risk. "If the platform continues to evolve in ways that hinder journalists’ abilities to meaningfully engage, a vital channel for networking, exposure, and information-sharing risks being lost, posing long-term consequences for the industry’s diversity and inclusion of young voices."

Plus, BKC Research Assistant Krista Cezair edited a new anthology, PRISM: Mental Health Through the Lens of Difference (https://bcph.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Prism-online-view-1.pdf) , via the HPHR Journal.
