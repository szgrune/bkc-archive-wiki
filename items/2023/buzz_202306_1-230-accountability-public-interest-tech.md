** Unusual vindication (https://klonick.substack.com/p/how-236471-words-of-amici-briefing#footnote-1-124666236)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kate Klonick explains the path to Gonzalez v. Google's 565 word per curium opinion. "Far more interesting than anything you could read about the legal issues discussed in the thousands of pages of briefing or ignored in the breviloquent final decision, was the delta in word-count between them, which belied a two-year long narrative arc of legal realism."

https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/may/29/rumman-chowdhury-interview-artificial-intelligence-accountability

** Taking responsibility (https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/may/29/rumman-chowdhury-interview-artificial-intelligence-accountability)
------------------------------------------------------------

Rumman Chowdhury discusses her approach to regulating AI, “welcoming of progress and firm in the assertion that ‘mechanisms of accountability’ should exist.” While she believes that many applications of AI can be addressed with compromise, she draws a hard line at “surveillance capitalism.” “We cannot put lipstick on a pig. I do not think ethical surveillance can exist.”

https://pitcases.org/portfolio/pit-future-of-data-science-ai-afua-bruce/

** Positive advancements (https://pitcases.org/portfolio/pit-future-of-data-science-ai-afua-bruce/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Afua Bruce explains how principles of public interest technology can positively shape new technologies. "Public interest technology’s emphasis on equity and justice provides the necessary lens to guide the development and use of" data science and artificial intelligence.

https://cyberbullying.org/generative-ai-risks-harms-platforms-users

** Harm prevention (https://cyberbullying.org/generative-ai-risks-harms-platforms-users)
------------------------------------------------------------

Sameer Hinduja provides guidance for platforms and users aiming to reduce risks associated with generative AI. "While a variety of stakeholders can and will shape how generative AI impacts society, platforms and users are currently best positioned to protect against misuse and abuse in specific and tangible ways."

https://slate.com/technology/2023/05/ai-regulation-open-source-meta.html

** Open-source LLMs (https://slate.com/technology/2023/05/ai-regulation-open-source-meta.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier discusses how the open-source community is changing AI. "Now that the open-source community is remixing LLMs, it’s no longer possible to regulate the technology by dictating what research and development can be done; there are simply too many researchers doing too many different things in too many different countries."
