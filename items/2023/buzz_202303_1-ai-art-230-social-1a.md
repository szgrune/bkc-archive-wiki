** AI as artists (https://www.lawfareblog.com/ai-generated-works-artists-and-intellectual-property)
------------------------------------------------------------

Ryan Merkley discusses AI-generated art, IP, and future creativity. "The story of generative AI is built on the historical undervaluing of art in our society, twisted and confused by the byzantine elements of copyright law, and torqued up to blockbuster-movie intensity by the looming reality that robots will soon be able to compete with, or even replace, human creativity."

** Views on First (https://knightcolumbia.org/content/views-on-first)
------------------------------------------------------------

The final installment of Evelyn Douek's podcast series "Views on First" has been released. In the five episode series, Douek "talks to guests from the tech industry, legal practice, and academia about some of the thorniest questions that arise when social media collides with the First Amendment."

** Section 230's future (https://themarkup.org/hello-world/2023/02/25/section-230-is-a-load-bearing-wall-is-it-coming-down)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kate Klonick and James Grimmelmann spoke with The Markup's Nabiha Syed about Gonzalez v. Google and the future of Section 230. Klonick: "It is a constant surprise to me that Section 230 has become this black hole for big tech agita, and I think it’s most constructive to focus on its history and legislative intent."

https://techpolicy.press/tiktoks-api-guidelines-are-a-minefield-for-researchers/

** TikTok's research ToS (https://techpolicy.press/tiktoks-api-guidelines-are-a-minefield-for-researchers/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Joe Bak-Coleman writes about the complicated nature of TikTok's API guidelines for researchers. "All API ToS's [terms of service] are violated at times, but the conditions TikTok has created seem distinct in that there appears to be no plausible pathway to published research without violating the ToS."
