** Ruling on online speech (https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/13/opinion/federal-judge-biden-social-media.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kate Klonick argues that the trend of using state authorities and cherry-picked federal forums to decide issues of national internet regulation is concerning. "The future of online freedom shouldn’t be shaped by ideologically lopsided courts in far-flung federal jurisdictions or local politicians angling for the national stage."

https://cdt.org/insights/report-sharing-the-health-guidance-for-schools-when-procuring-mental-health-technologies/

** Mental health technologies (https://cdt.org/insights/report-sharing-the-health-guidance-for-schools-when-procuring-mental-health-technologies/ )
------------------------------------------------------------

Hannah Quay-de la Vallee, with the support of Sandra Cortesi, Madeline McGee, Leah Plunkett, and Elisabeth Sylvan, offers guidance for schools looking to procure and implement tech for mitigating student mental health crises. “If schools do not ensure that any products they use take protection and governance of this sensitive data seriously, they may place their students at risk.”

https://ethanzuckerman.com/2023/07/07/competition-for-twitter-is-good-threads-thus-far-is-not/

** S (https://ethanzuckerman.com/2023/07/07/competition-for-twitter-is-good-threads-thus-far-is-not/) ocial (https://ethanzuckerman.com/2023/07/07/competition-for-twitter-is-good-threads-thus-far-is-not/) media after Twitter (https://ethanzuckerman.com/2023/07/07/competition-for-twitter-is-good-threads-thus-far-is-not/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Ethan Zuckerman argues that, despite its improvements over Twitter and other alternatives, Meta’s Threads is rife with accessibility and usability problems while minimizing users’ control over their own feeds and replicating Meta’s security track record. “We need new models for how [online public] spaces are moderated, governed, and supported.”

https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-tech-regulation-can-leverage-product-experimentation-results

** E (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-tech-regulation-can-leverage-product-experimentation-results) xperimenting with tech (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-tech-regulation-can-leverage-product-experimentation-results)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nathaniel Lubin and Ravi Iyer suggest that tech companies’ use of product experimentation merits greater attention in conversations about the regulation and governance of platforms. “Because experiments are happening all the time across teams and groups, there is a good chance that most individuals who have used the platforms for a significant length of time have been in one of these experiments even if they did not know it.”

https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2023/06/29/ai-pay-americans-data-00103648

** AI dividends (https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2023/06/29/ai-pay-americans-data-00103648)
------------------------------------------------------------

Barath Raghavan and Bruce Schneier propose that large tech companies pay into an AI Dividend fund to compensate the American public for its contributions to generative AI. “Our plan can’t make sure there are no downsides to the development of AI, but it would ensure that all Americans will share in the upsides – particularly since this new technology isn’t possible without our contribution.”

https://www.politico.com/newsletters/digital-future-daily/2023/07/07/5-questions-for-rumman-chowdhury-00105237

** 5 questions for Chowdhury (https://www.politico.com/newsletters/digital-future-daily/2023/07/07/5-questions-for-rumman-chowdhury-00105237)
------------------------------------------------------------

Rumman Chowdhury responds to questions about under- and over-rated tech ideas and suggests that the government invest in third-party legal protections for those who identify flaws in extant technologies. “You’re actually only working on behalf of society. You should be empowered to identify and report serious problems with technology.”
