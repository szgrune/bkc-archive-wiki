** Platform accountability (https://www.platformaccountability.com)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nathaniel Lubin and Thomas Krendl Gilbert released a white paper (https://www.platformaccountability.com)  for addressing the systemic harms of platforms. “Just as new rules were needed to meet the public health challenges of the 19th and 20th centuries, platforms require accountability infrastructure that places limits on optimization in order to engage at-risk populations safely and legitimately.”

Nathaniel and Thomas also essayed their proposed approach in The Atlantic (https://urldefense.proofpoint.com/v2/url?u=https-3A__www.theatlantic.com_ideas_archive_2023_06_germ-2Dtheory-2Ddigital-2Dplatforms-2Dinternet-2Dmetaphore_674458_&d=DwMFaQ&c=WO-RGvefibhHBZq3fL85hQ&r=3yoMkF0b_gwqt7HwvTSFQzixfJIAZc4UvuZy3uNIbYI&m=pFgN3eABmWG4Te8d9q2f546PjCwicONQj19KMPhd8BycyfTdEEFbM-YfjR7SjdrY&s=WHkTozK03S0CFbFxFsb5q604yGT0FUMoovISgK0HTlo&e=)  and Tech Policy Press (https://urldefense.proofpoint.com/v2/url?u=https-3A__techpolicy.press_how-2Dto-2Dassess-2Dplatform-2Dimpact-2Don-2Dmental-2Dhealth-2Dand-2Dcivic-2Dnorms_&d=DwMFaQ&c=WO-RGvefibhHBZq3fL85hQ&r=3yoMkF0b_gwqt7HwvTSFQzixfJIAZc4UvuZy3uNIbYI&m=pFgN3eABmWG4Te8d9q2f546PjCwicONQj19KMPhd8BycyfTdEEFbM-YfjR7SjdrY&s=kc72FPNrvHxDHRXit4yaSnAAccEtj38XlyJluyKCG60&e=) .

** Arts & generative AI (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh4451)
------------------------------------------------------------

In Science, Jessica Fjeld joins her Investigators of Human Creativity colleagues in exploring the relationship between generative AI and the arts. “We express our humanity through art, so understanding and shaping the impact of AI on creative expression is at the center of broader questions about its impact on society.”

Jessica and collaborators also map out what generative AI may mean for copyright law in The Conversation (https://urldefense.proofpoint.com/v2/url?u=https-3A__theconversation.com_generative-2Dai-2Dis-2Da-2Dminefield-2Dfor-2Dcopyright-2Dlaw-2D207473&d=DwMFaQ&c=WO-RGvefibhHBZq3fL85hQ&r=3yoMkF0b_gwqt7HwvTSFQzixfJIAZc4UvuZy3uNIbYI&m=pFgN3eABmWG4Te8d9q2f546PjCwicONQj19KMPhd8BycyfTdEEFbM-YfjR7SjdrY&s=jtZamJA76Yr9Jx9ykuJG5BHl8iz85n2GwuHuSVuQmfQ&e=) .

** Governing AI (https://deliverypdf.ssrn.com/delivery.php?ID=586024090004110064094097023117114105036053067045062087091090095119013071085097088104033016012031048048013086080030099091112019009094094009064093080016112095117093127055062048111127125084102127070000091095026085088010069003018102083029007001110018098101&EXT=pdf&INDEX=TRUE)
------------------------------------------------------------

Noha Lea Halim and Urs Gasser juxtapose the U.S. Algorithmic Accountability Act (AAA) and the EU AI Act (AIA) and offer some reflections on what the commonalities and differences might teach us about the future direction of AI governance. "The US AAA and the EU AIA show that questions of good AI governance will not end with the introduction of laws, as important as they might be."

** Black culture & technology (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbc-eN8EDcY)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jabari Evans discusses race and technological innovation with James Riley of blackbox Lab on his podcast Uncommon Naledge. “Technology has always been embedded within black popular culture as well as hip-hop more specifically. There’s very direct correlations…certain types of methods for creating digital art have spawned out of hip-hop.”

https://www.openglobalrights.org/human-rights-AI-driven-game-lottery/?lang=English

** Probabalistic human rights (https://www.openglobalrights.org/human-rights-AI-driven-game-lottery/?lang=English)
------------------------------------------------------------

Juan Ortiz Freuler explains how AI has contributed to the shift towards a probabalistic worldview. "If there is space to reform the values underlying these technologies to make them serve the public interest, governments will need to reassert their democratic legitimacy as architects of social relationships and replace the market incentives that are fueling technological development with public interest goals."

https://law.yale.edu/isp/publications/digital-public-sphere/uniformity-and-fragmentation-digital-public-sphere/imagine-community-obscenitys-history-and-moderating-speech-online

** "Community" standards (https://law.yale.edu/isp/publications/digital-public-sphere/uniformity-and-fragmentation-digital-public-sphere/imagine-community-obscenitys-history-and-moderating-speech-online)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kendra Albert explains the history of the community standards doctrine. "With modern day doctrinal developments, it becomes clear that the 'community' allows courts to avoid the reality that obscenity is a First Amendment doctrine designed to do exactly what justices have decried in other contexts – have the state decide 'good speech' from 'bad speech' based on preference for certain speakers and messages."

https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/scaling-trust/

** Scaling trust online (https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/scaling-trust/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Task Force for a Trustworthy Future (including Sue Hendrickson, Rumman Chowdhury, Leah Plunkett) argues that we are at a pivotal moment for online spaces. "A rare combination of regulatory sea change that will transform markets, landmarks in technological development, and newly consolidating expertise can open a window into a new and better future."
