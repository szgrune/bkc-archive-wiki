** It's (more) complicated (https://lpeproject.org/blog/how-not-to-regulate-digital-platforms/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In considering how to regulate digital platforms, Elettra Bietti argues against “framing the landscape of possibilities as a choice between ‘breaking up’ and ‘democratizing’ Big Tech.” Instead, she advocates “a practice of regulatory experimentalism…as the best way forward in technology platform markets.”

https://www.npr.org/2023/11/05/1210734163/ftx-is-now-defunct-does-crypto-have-a-future

** Crypto after the collapse (https://www.npr.org/2023/11/05/1210734163/ftx-is-now-defunct-does-crypto-have-a-future)
------------------------------------------------------------

On NPR, Molly White discusses crypto's future after the collapse of FTX. "If [cryptocurrency] companies are required to play by the same rules as the traditional financial system, I think it could really stifle a lot of the profit that has come in the industry and also a lot of the fraud, fortunately."

** Copyright and generative AI
------------------------------------------------------------

Ruth Okediji and Rebecca Tushnet comment on the interactions of copyright, fair use, and generative AI. Okediji moderated a Rappaport Forum (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/intellectual-property-experts-discuss-fair-use-in-the-age-of-ai/) on what's at stake, and Tushnet joined the Hard Fork podcast (https://www.nytimes.com/2023/11/03/podcasts/hard-fork-executive-order-ai-copyright.html?) to explain the latest developments in a lawsuit against the creators of AI image-generation tools.
