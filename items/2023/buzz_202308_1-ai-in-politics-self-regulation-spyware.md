** Learning from the RSM Assembly Fellows (https://rebootingsocialmedia.org/fellows-summary/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Institute for Rebooting Social Media recently wrapped up its inaugural RSM Assembly Fellowship. Thirteen interdisciplinary researchers, entrepreneurs, and professionals came together to develop and demonstrate ideas for what a healthy information ecosystem might look like.

This month RSM is spotlighting individual fellowship projects. Check out Nate Lubin's work on accountability infrastructure (https://rebootingsocialmedia.org/2023/08/02/platform-accountability-assembly-fellowship-spotlight/) and stay tuned for more.

https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/07/28/1076756/six-ways-that-ai-could-change-politics/

** Tracking AI in politics (https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/07/28/1076756/six-ways-that-ai-could-change-politics/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier and Nathan Sanders articulate six benchmarks that might be used to measure the growing influence of AI on electoral politics in both legislative hearings and campaigns. "The greatest challenge to most of these milestones is their observability: will we know it when we see it?"

https://www.marketplace.org/shows/marketplace-tech/should-the-ai-makers-also-be-the-ai-regulators/

** Technology self-regulation (https://www.marketplace.org/shows/marketplace-tech/should-the-ai-makers-also-be-the-ai-regulators/)
------------------------------------------------------------

On Marketplace Tech, Lily Jamali and Rumman Chowdhury discuss tech industry players' efforts to address the risks posed by AI through the formation of the Frontier Model Forum as a regulatory body. "I think a global governance body should be uniquely tasked to ensure that good things happen for humanity using these AI systems."

https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/regulating-commercial-spyware

** Spyware & the rule of law (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/regulating-commercial-spyware)
------------------------------------------------------------

Asaf Lubin proposes his multi-stakeholder vision for regulating commercial spyware: the Commercial Spyware Accreditation System (CSAS). "I see my proposal as a modular solution, to pick and choose from as the parties to the code continue to debate a final framework in the years to come."
