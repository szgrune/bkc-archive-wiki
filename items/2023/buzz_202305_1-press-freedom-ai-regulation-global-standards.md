** Harassment and journalism (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/online-harassment-threat-to-world-press-freedom-540adb768c54)
------------------------------------------------------------

For World Press Freedom Day, Elodie Vialle talks with Pratika Katiyar about defending journalists online. "So acknowledging that women and marginalized groups are disproportionately targeted by online harassment, newsrooms that are committed to work on enhancing diversity can put in place better safety measures."

https://www.cigionline.org/articles/ai-regulation-should-not-be-a-blank-cheque-for-government/

** AI regulation in Canada (https://www.cigionline.org/articles/ai-regulation-should-not-be-a-blank-cheque-for-government/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Maroussia Lévesque and Florian Martin-Bariteau unpack the proposed Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA). "AIDA is an empty shell that falls short of providing much-needed clarity about rights and obligations pertaining to AI systems. Undefined terms gesture toward obligations for entities responsible for AI systems, with the blanks supposedly to be filled in down the line."

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01679-0

** A Paris agreement for AI bias (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01679-0)
------------------------------------------------------------

Yong Jin Park considers the fight against climate change for lessons for regulating AI. "Just like the global standard on carbon emission, the global standard on AI bias is possible, and as the first step, the use of racially identifiable personal data can be banned in AI attached to specific sectors, such as medical and financial services."

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4392918

** ChatGPT3 in Africa (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4392918)
------------------------------------------------------------

Gregory Gondwe takes a critical look at ChatGPT, interviewing journalists in Africa about using it. "While most participants focused on the negative aspect of technology, i.e., job redundancy, loss of rigorous journalism practices, and amplified stereotypical biases, a few talked about how they could positively leverage technology."
