** The public option for AI (https://slate.com/technology/2023/04/ai-public-option.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier, Henry Farrell, and Nathan Sanders make a case for building "assistive A.I. for democracy." "We should apply A.I. through piecemeal democratic engineering, carefully determining what works and what does not. The best way forward is to start small, applying these technologies to local decisions with more constrained stakeholder groups and smaller impacts."

** Don't be a lawyer, ChatGPT (https://peertube.dair-institute.org/w/o6sb7f7RwapWBJd9VC61t4)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kendra Albert guests on the Distributed AI Research Institute's Mystery AI Hype Theater 3000 for a discussion of large language models and legal expertise. "The idea that generating a first draft with one of these [AI] tools and then correcting it is a similar skill to one that lawyers already have is just totally bonkers to me; and I say this as someone who teaches people how to practice law."

https://harvardlawreview.org/forum/vol-136/of-systems-thinking-and-straw-men/

** Thinking content moderation (https://harvardlawreview.org/forum/vol-136/of-systems-thinking-and-straw-men/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kate Klonick responds to Evelyn Douek's recent Content Moderation as Systems Thinking (https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-136/content-moderation-as-systems-thinking/) . "To best understand content moderation as systems thinking, one would have to accede that content moderation contains individual decisions, automations, governance, governments, external influence, internal politics, constitutions, norms, legality, human judgment and biases, administration, bureaucracy, multistep processes, long legislative-like meetings, people, corporate courthouses, actual courthouses, stakeholders, economies, the media, and iterative dynamic changes."
