Jessica Fjeld speaks with BKC Research Assistant Krista Cezair about intellectual property in the context of the rise of generative AI. "We want to reward the artists who are contributing to social progress through all of the important work they’re doing, and we also want to reward the technologists."

** Image manipulation (https://www.miamiherald.com/opinion/op-ed/article272914855.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

Juliana Castro Varon explains photo manipulation and generative AI. "When damaging images are so easily created and distributed at warp speed, the stakes of understanding the basics of how AI works — its reliance on datasets, and who made those datasets — are higher than ever before."

Plus: Castro recently spoke with Elizabeth Dubois about the past, present, and future of image manipulation (https://www.polcommtech.com/post/image-manipulation-juliana-castro-varon) .

https://www.wired.com/story/platforms-combat-harassment-but-theyre-focusing-on-the-wrong-kind/

** Chronic harassment (https://www.wired.com/story/platforms-combat-harassment-but-theyre-focusing-on-the-wrong-kind/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In Wired, Rumman Chowdhury and Gina Neff argue that platforms have been focused on fighting the wrong kind of online abuse when developing mitigation tools. “But for journalists, politicians, scientists, actors—anyone, really, who relies on connecting online to do their jobs—[those tools] are woefully insufficient.”

https://www.polcommtech.com/post/meta-oversight-board-with-julie-owono

** Independent oversight (https://www.polcommtech.com/post/meta-oversight-board-with-julie-owono)
------------------------------------------------------------

Julie Owono discusses content moderation and the Meta Oversight Board with Elizabeth Dubois. "The role of the Oversight Board is not necessarily to tell a company like Meta what it should tolerate or not. Rather, we think our added value is helping the company have clear processes, rules, and principles that allow them to make the necessary balance between conflicting rights."

https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/03/14/1069717/how-ai-could-write-our-laws/?utm_campaign=coschedule&utm_source=twitter&utm_medium=HarvardAsh&utm_content=How%20AI%20could%20write%20our%20laws

** Lobbying with AI (https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/03/14/1069717/how-ai-could-write-our-laws/?utm_campaign=coschedule&utm_source=twitter&utm_medium=HarvardAsh&utm_content=How%20AI%20could%20write%20our%20laws)
------------------------------------------------------------

In the MIT Technology Review, Nathan Sanders and Bruce Schneier unpack how AI has the potential to “hack” lobbying through microlegislation and explore potential steps to minimize adverse effects. "The unavoidable conclusion is that AI will make lobbying more guileful, and perhaps more successful."
