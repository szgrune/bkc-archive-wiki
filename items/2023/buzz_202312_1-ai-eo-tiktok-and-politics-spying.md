** Executive Order on AI (https://lpeproject.org/blog/seven-reactions-to-bidens-executive-order-on-artificial-intelligence/)
------------------------------------------------------------

On the Law and Political Economy Project blog, Salomé Viljoen, Ifeoma Ajunwa, and Elettra Bietti are among a group of scholars who provide initial reactions to President Biden's Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence. Their posts focus on provisions for non-industry led AI research (Viljoen), inadequate safeguards for workers' rights (Ajunwa), and tension between competition and innovation (Bietti), among other features of the EO. The full set of reactions is collected here. (https://lpeproject.org/blog/seven-reactions-to-bidens-executive-order-on-artificial-intelligence/)

https://www.techpolicy.press/members-of-congress-dont-dance-on-tiktok/

** TikTok politicking (https://www.techpolicy.press/members-of-congress-dont-dance-on-tiktok/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Anupam Chander, Donara Aghanjani, and Alyanna Apacible discuss the fraught dynamic between Congressional distrust of TikTok and TikTok's potential for campaign messaging. "As the 2024 election nears, it will be interesting to see how Congressional use of TikTok evolves...Will some decide that the reach enabled by TikTok is worth any risk that its use entails and any political criticism that may follow?"

https://theconversation.com/online-likes-for-toxic-social-media-posts-prompt-more-and-more-hateful-messages-218220

** Hate bonding (https://theconversation.com/online-likes-for-toxic-social-media-posts-prompt-more-and-more-hateful-messages-218220)
------------------------------------------------------------

Joseph Walther explains the social approval theory of online hate: that posters of hateful messages are motivated by the approval of like-minded individuals. "When you look at online hate messages, you start to notice clues that suggest, more often than not, that hatemongers are posting messages to each other, not to those their messages implicate and denigrate."

https://slate.com/technology/2023/12/ai-mass-spying-internet-surveillance.html

** Spying without spies (https://slate.com/technology/2023/12/ai-mass-spying-internet-surveillance.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier argues that AI is on track to reduce spying's current limiting factor—the need for human labor. "Unlike humans, AI tools can be replicated by the millions and are improving at astonishing rates. They’ll get better next year, and even better the year after that. We are about to enter the era of mass spying."
