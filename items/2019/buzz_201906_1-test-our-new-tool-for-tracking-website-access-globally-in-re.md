** Track website access, worldwide, in real-time (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/announcing-accesscheck)
------------------------------------------------------------
AccessCheck (https://accesscheck.thenetmonitor.org/) - a new tool from our Internet Monitor project - lets users check to see if a website is available in over 50 countries around the world, in real-time.

Test the tool for yourself. Visit accesscheck.thenetmonitor.org (https://accesscheck.thenetmonitor.org/) , enter a URL and a country, and the tool will show you if that URL is accessible to web users in that country, and share any errors encountered along the way.

This tool is intended to inform and support the efforts of those engaged in related public interest work, including journalists, academic researchers, and civil society organizations.

** Artificial Intelligence in Society (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/artificial-intelligence-society)
------------------------------------------------------------
Over the past year, the Berkman Klein Center has made significant substantive contributions to two Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) initiatives that will help ensure that AI is developed and governed in a manner that respects the rule of law and benefits the whole of society.

Through contributions to the OECD’s book on AI & Society (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/artificial-intelligence-society) and through our work on OECD’s recently adopted AI Principles (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/what-are-oecd-principles-ai) , BKC has built on its long standing relationship with the OECD.

These contributions translate our scholarship and expertise on issues from AI & inclusion to transparency & accountability into resources that will benefit decisionmakers around the world as they grapple with the opportunities and risks of AI technologies.
https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/youtubes-digital-playground

** YouTube's Recommendation Algorithm Under Scrutiny for Surfacing Harmful Content (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/youtubes-digital-playground)
------------------------------------------------------------

While conducting research on YouTube’s impact in Brazil, BKC’s Jonas Kaiser and Yaso Córdova, along with Adrian Rauchfleisch, discovered an alarming phenomenon: the platform's recommendation algorithm drives engagement with content that enables child exploitation.

The discovery, first reported in the New York Times, led to a policy change (https://youtube.googleblog.com/2019/06/an-update-on-our-efforts-to-protect.html)  from YouTube, formal responses from politicians, and a great deal of worldwide coverage, including late night talk shows (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwEM0ib6ZbU) .

Learn more (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-06/youtubes-digital-playground)
