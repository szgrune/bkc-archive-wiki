** Data Protection and Digital Agency for Refugees (https://www.cigionline.org/publications/data-protection-and-digital-agency-refugees)
------------------------------------------------------------

For the millions of refugees fleeing conflict and persecution every year, access to information about their rights and control over their personal data are crucial for their ability to assess risk and navigate the asylum process.

In this paper from the World Refugee Council, BKC's Dragana Kaurin analyzes how the vast amount of data collected from refugees is gathered, stored, and shared today, and considers the additional risks this collection process poses to an already vulnerable population navigating a perilous information-decision gap.

Read more... (https://www.cigionline.org/publications/data-protection-and-digital-agency-refugees)

** Enveloped in Suspicion (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2019/05/ever-strava-ai-human-ignorance/589306/)
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC's Mary Gray was interviewed for an article on the hidden uses of data. “Think about it like food,” she says. “When you know the conditions of the people who are growing and picking food, you also know the conditions of the food you’re eating.”

** Democracy's Dilemma (https://bostonreview.net/war-security/henry-farrell-bruce-schneier-democracys-dilemma)
------------------------------------------------------------

Democracies rely on the free exchange of ideas and information, writes Bruce Schneier, but that freedom can also be weaponized to erode democratic debate. How can democratic societies protect—and protect themselves from—the free flow of digital information?

Read more in Boston Review (https://bostonreview.net/war-security/henry-farrell-bruce-schneier-democracys-dilemma)

“It seems that market-based conceptions of data governance cannot alone remedy our discontent with the platform economy, let alone act as panacea data governance solutions,” writes BKC affiliate Elettra Bietti in a post for Harvard University’s Edmond J. Safra Center for Ethics.

** French media is polarizing. But not in the way we expected. (https://www.cjr.org/politics/french-media-polarization.php)
------------------------------------------------------------

Building on research from Yochai Benkler and the Berkman Klein Center into the media ecosystem during the 2016 presidential election, Ethan Zuckerman examines whether French media was experiencing the same breed of political polarization as has emerged in American news and digital media.

Read more in Colombia Journalism Review (https://www.cjr.org/politics/french-media-polarization.php)
