** Responding to Hate Online & Off (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-08/combating-hate-speech-through-counterspeech)
------------------------------------------------------------
With the tragedies in El Paso, Texas and Dayton, Ohio this week attention is refocused on the spread of ideologically driven violence and the role of the press and social media.

Should 8chan - a key platform for spreading hateful ideology and sharing the manifestos of those who commit violent acts - be regulated or taken down altogether? Incoming BKC fellow Mutale Nkonde spoke to the Boston Globe (https://www.bostonglobe.com/news/nation/2019/08/05/what-online-forum-chan/6C9Oj2owvh7daZb8rJnwhK/story.html) about the need to create a legal definition for harmful speech online. Incoming BKC affiliate Evelyn Douek wrote for the Atlantic (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2019/08/how-disabling-8chan-became-cloudflares-job/595606/)  about how 8chan could be tackled through pressure from within the private sector - via their own hosting service.

BKC affiliate Joan Donovan shared some thoughts from the Shorenstein Center's blog (https://shorensteincenter.org/dr-joan-donovan-on-el-paso-8chan-and-whats-next/)  on the use of manifestos as a tactic, and explains how extremists manipulate the media to control public conversation. And while we often think about those who commit mass violence as acting individually, Donovan spoke to NBC News (https://www.nbcnews.com/think/opinion/el-paso-shooter-wasn-t-lone-wolf-his-so-called-ncna1039201) about the fact that these extremists are never alone online.

Friend of the Center and UN Special Rapporteur on freedom of expression David Kaye also shared 4 questions about online hate speech (https://onezero.medium.com/four-questions-about-online-hate-speech-ae3e0a134472) ; Siva Vaidhyanathan surveyed the online landscape of white supremacist content for the Guardian (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2019/aug/06/trump-seeds-violent-white-nationalism?fbclid=IwAR2ZFjiLLMOY8zR7ZotoGZnDRk6s-u2ROVKSyI93goYFvjVaSbtE_RFnZ_U) ; and Cathy Buerger of BKC Faculty Associate Susan Benesch's Dangerous Speech Project (https://dangerousspeech.org/)  shared some of her extensive research on the efforts of counterspeech practitioners (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-08/combating-hate-speech-through-counterspeech)  to combat hate speech directly online.

** Facial Recognition Is Suddenly Everywhere. Should You Worry? (https://www.wired.com/story/facial-recognition-everywhere-should-you-worry/)
------------------------------------------------------------
The rapid adoption of facial recognition technology by the government has triggered a debate about the algorithms’ trustworthiness and how they are being used.

BKC affiliate Gretchen Greene says that even if you trust the technology, wider government use requires careful debate. “I would rather live in a world where I feel I have some privacy, even in public spaces,” she says. “If people know where you are, you might not do those things that are the bedrock of what we think people in this country should be able to do.”

** Embedded EthiCS: Integrating Ethics Across CS Education (https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2019/8/238345-embedded-ethics/fulltext)
------------------------------------------------------------
Embedded EthiCS is a collaboration between philosophers and computer scientists, including BKC fellow Lily Hu. Embedded EthiCS employs a distributed pedagogy that makes ethical reasoning an integral component of courses throughout the standard computer science curriculum.

“The strategy of integrating the teaching of ethical reasoning skills with the teaching of computational techniques into existing computer science coursework not only provides students valuable experience identifying, confronting, and working through ethical questions, but also communicates the need to identify, confront, and address ethical questions throughout their work in computer science,” the team writes in an article for Communications of the ACM.
