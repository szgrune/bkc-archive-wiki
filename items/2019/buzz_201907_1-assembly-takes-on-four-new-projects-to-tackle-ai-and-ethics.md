** What We Created During Assembly 2019 (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/what-we-created-during-assembly-2019-450c77a0cda1)
------------------------------------------------------------
“If there’s one line through the projects, its thinking about the duties — moral and otherwise — of those who are helping with the rapid deployment of technologies loosely grouped around artificial intelligence… Thinking about what those duties are, who should be mindful of them, and who is responsible for them," said BKC's Jonathan Zittrain, of this year's cohort of Assembly participants.

Now in its third year as a unique collaboration between the Berkman Klein Center and MIT's Media Lab, Assembly brought together 19 technologists, managers, policymakers, and other professionals for three months of learning, ideating, and creating.

Assembly teams developed four projects confronting some of tech’s biggest problems, from mitigating bias in AI systems, to interrogating the creation of classification systems, to highlighting the risks of pursuing surveillance-related work in AI, to examining the expansion of Natural Language Processing / Natural Language Understanding systems.

Learn more about the 2019 Assembly program (and what we're cooking up for 2020!) (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/what-we-created-during-assembly-2019-450c77a0cda1)
From our Community

** Three reflections on decentralization (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/three-reflections-on-decentralization-112bd8751e41)
------------------------------------------------------------
"A handful of platforms have become what most people know as 'the internet,'"  write Juan Ortiz Freuler and Rosemary Leith, the co-chairs of BKC's Re-decentralization of the Web Reading Group. "The ongoing centralization of the web is taking its toll on security, innovation and our trust, in the web and the internet."

** Smile, Your City Is Watching You (https://www.nytimes.com/2019/06/27/opinion/cities-privacy-surveillance.html)
------------------------------------------------------------
Local governments must protect your privacy as they turn to “smart city” technology, argues BKC’s Ben Green in an Op Ed for The New York Times.

“Certainly, this data can be used for beneficial outcomes: reducing traffic, improving infrastructure and saving energy. But the data also includes detailed information about the activities of everyone in the city — data that could be used in numerous detrimental ways."

** The hidden labor supporting algorithms (https://today.law.harvard.edu/the-hidden-labor-supporting-algorithms/)
------------------------------------------------------------
“Labor is a question of our political and moral will to value people’s contributions to our productivity and exchanges in society,” said BKC’s Mary Gray, discussing her book “Ghost Work: How to Stop Silicon Valley from Building a New Global Underclass" at the Harvard Bookstore. “We keep looking for market solutions to something that, arguably, is a social question – a cultural question – of how to value each other.”

** Could ‘fake text’ be the next global political threat? (https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2019/jul/04/ai-fake-text-gpt-2-concerns-false-information)
------------------------------------------------------------
The most damaging instances of fake news are written by political extremists and trolls, and tend to be about controversial topics that “trigger deep-seated hatred”, like election fraud or immigration, says BKC’s Yochai Benkler. “The simple ability to generate false text at scale is not likely to affect most forms of disinformation.”

** Do Schools Really Kill Creativity? (https://www.edsurge.com/news/2019-06-26-do-schools-really-kill-creativity-jonny-sun-on-what-schools-get-right)
------------------------------------------------------------
“In my experience, students feel seen when we discuss the internet with them in complex ways that respect the fact that they have an incredible amount of experience with the internet—and perhaps, in many cases, more complex experiences than those who teach them,” says BKC affiliate Jonny Sun.

** Why Libra Needs A Humanitarian Fig Leaf (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/why-libra-needs-a-humanitarian-fig-leaf-79ae6a463c8)
------------------------------------------------------------
“Facebook says its Libra payment platform is a social impact project that promises benefits to the poor and unbanked. Others say it’s an obvious play to be a global financial services giant with a virtual currency of its own invention, brazenly ignoring the global financial regulatory system. NGO Mercy Corps, the only humanitarian aid agency involved, is lending its hard-earned credibility to that social impact promise, but at what cost?” writes BKC’s Dragana Kaurin.
