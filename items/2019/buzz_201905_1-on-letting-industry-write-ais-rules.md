** Don’t let industry write the rules for AI (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-01413-1)
------------------------------------------------------------

"Companies’ input in shaping the future of AI is essential, but they cannot retain the power they have gained to frame research on how their systems impact society or on how we evaluate the effect morally," writes BKC Director Yochai Benkler in Nature. "Governments and publicly accountable entities must support independent research, and insist that industry shares enough data for it to be kept accountable."

Read more in Nature... (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-01413-1)

** Industry efforts aren’t about to disrupt tech’s ethics problem (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/industry-efforts-arent-about-to-disrupt-tech-s-ethics-problem-f5d70899a61e?source=email-9769e0227770--writer.postDistributed&sk=d3b3d73adb183639e87d7af7388f5637)
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Affiliate Sara Watson explores the use and efficacy of frameworks like Ethical OS through her essay on the tech industry’s attempts to alleviate ethical challenges. “Ethical OS itself is a future signal of the industry’s trending efforts to reflect and correct course. They just can’t build the future alone."

** The hidden global workforce that is still fighting for an eight-hour workday (https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2019/05/01/hidden-global-workforce-that-is-still-fighting-an-eight-hour-workday/?utm_term=.f6c24ef75dbc)
------------------------------------------------------------

"As we tap endlessly on those friendly icons to 'like' social media content, summon deliveries from favorite restaurants and call rides to the airport, we’re overlooking a global workforce," writes BKC Fellow Mary L. Gray. "Millions of workers are doing on-demand work to keep the Internet running smoothly, and they are now fighting for similar rights that full-time employees won decades ago."

Read more in the Washington Post... (https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2019/05/01/hidden-global-workforce-that-is-still-fighting-an-eight-hour-workday/?utm_term=.f6c24ef75dbc)

** Is Artificial Intelligence Good for Our Health? (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/is-artificial-intelligence-good-for-our-health-d091aab2ba8e)
------------------------------------------------------------

The increasing use of mobile technology and AI tools has blurred the distinction between privacy and individual rights and the extent to which businesses can mine data. By using a transdisciplinary approach, Ashveena Gajeelee and BKC’s Health Tech Working Group explore the implications of the use of AI in healthcare.

Read more on Medium (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/is-artificial-intelligence-good-for-our-health-d091aab2ba8e)

It may seem like a straightforward question: Can algorithms themselves be biased? BKC fellow Momin M. Malik explains that the better question is: can statistical models be marginalizing, apart from data?

UPCOMING: Data as Development on May 23

There are a number of questions for developing countries on whether and how gains from AI and big data can be leveraged in ways that are equitable. While governance frameworks and new technology architectures abound on the Internet, developing countries seem to be lagging behind.

This workshop, co-sponsored by the Berkman Klein Center and Digital HKS, and held May 23, 2019 at Harvard's Kennedy School, will focus on questions of data typologies for developing countries, with a view to understanding how to create data markets and governance frameworks that are more beneficial to the needs of local businesses, societal participation and overall welfare in developing countries.
Learn more and RSVP (https://cyber.harvard.edu/events/2019-05-23/data-development)
