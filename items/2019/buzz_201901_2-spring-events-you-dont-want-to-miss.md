Why Do People Fall for Fake News? (https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/19/opinion/sunday/fake-news.html)

"What makes people susceptible to fake news and other forms of strategic misinformation? And what, if anything, can be done about it?... [Authors Dave Rand and Gordon Pennycook] recently ran a set of studies (http://davidrand-cooperation.com/s/2018-Pennycook-Rand-Cognition.pdf)  in which participants of various political persuasions indicated whether they believed a series of news stories... We found that people who engaged in more reflective reasoning were better at telling true from false, regardless of whether the headlines aligned with their political views."

Read more at the New York Times... (https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/19/opinion/sunday/fake-news.html)

DuckDuckGo's private searches will pull up Apple Maps results (https://mashable.com/article/duckduckgo-apple-maps/#oJb_D9N9_PqH)

David O'Brien, a digital privacy expert at BKC noted that "anonymity doesn't really exist on the internet for individuals." But for what DDG and Apple Maps are offering he said both companies won't be collecting as much information as other internet services. Even if both companies live up to their data collection standards, said he's wary of how the privacy policies are implemented. "The devil can be in the implementation details and in how people are actually using these services," O'Brien said.

Is Privacy a Right? (https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/privacy-right)

"Today, the story we're telling about our right to privacy is mostly about privacy laws (such as the GDPR and California's AB 375) and their effects—especially on companies that need to comply. It's an interesting story, but one that distracts us away from work that needs to be done.

"The work we need to do most is on tech that gives each of us ways to guard our private spaces and signal to others what's okay and what's not okay."

Spotlight On: Our work with the Digital Asia Hub
https://www.digitalasiahub.org/announcing-fatasia-hong-kong-11-12-january-2019/

https://www.digitalasiahub.org/announcing-fatasia-hong-kong-11-12-january-2019/

A global community has converged around the themes of fairness, accountability, and transparency, as part of the attempt to address opaque systems, power and information asymmetries, and due process. The Berkman Klein Center and our Global Network of Internet & Society Centers (http://networkofcenters.net/)  partner Digital Asia Hub (https://www.digitalasiahub.org/announcing-fatasia-hong-kong-11-12-january-2019/fatasia-program/)  co-sponsored the Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (FAT)/Asia (https://www.digitalasiahub.org/announcing-fatasia-hong-kong-11-12-january-2019/)  conference at the University of Hong Kong, January 11-12 to interrogate the ethical challenges to human freedom posed by AI in the Asian context. We also got a chance to meet with Taiwanese Minister of Justice Tsai Ching-hsiang to discuss our ongoing collaborations on governance and AI.

Learn more about FAT/Asia 2019 (https://www.digitalasiahub.org/announcing-fatasia-hong-kong-11-12-january-2019/)

A new publication from the Digital Asia Hub collects the outputs of a two-day speculative fiction–writing workshop held in December 2018 as part of Digital Asia Hub’s "AI in Asia" series. This workshop was the original speculative sprint, designed to produce all of the material for a collection in just two intensive days. The final output of the workshop was a set of new speculative pieces of fiction, imagining our technological future, generated by the participants.
