New Research: Internet Censorship and Geopolitical Conflict in the Middle East and North Africa
https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/internet-censorship-and-the-intraregional-geopolitical-conflicts-in-the-middle-east-and-north-africa

The latest report (https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/internet-censorship-and-the-intraregional-geopolitical-conflicts-in-the-middle-east-and-north-africa) from the Internet Monitor project's Helmi Noman analyzes how conflicts between states in the Middle East & North Africa region translate into Internet censorship practices.

The study finds that blocked content often contains messaging that opposes the censoring government’s own narrative of the conflicts, and that governments block content that could potentially spark internal dissent.

For example, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, and Egypt block Iranian websites that promote Iran’s official narrative on local and foreign policies and that are critical of Saudi policies towards Iran and the region. On the other side of the conflict, Iran blocks content originating from Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, UAE, and Egypt.

Detain/Release Simulating algorithmic risk assessments at pretrial (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/detain-release-simulating-algorithmic-risk-assessments-at-pretrial-375270657819)

A legal education simulation developed by Berkman Klein Affiliate Keith Porcaro called Detain/Release puts students in the role of a county judge at a bail hearing, and prompts them to detain or release individual defendants pending trial. Going through the simulation conveys the lesson of the framing power of software; that the mere presence of a risk assessment tool can reframe a judge’s decision-making process and induce new biases, regardless of the tool’s quality.

Read more at the Berkman Klein Medium Collection... (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/detain-release-simulating-algorithmic-risk-assessments-at-pretrial-375270657819)
