** The Hidden Costs of Automated Thinking (https://www.newyorker.com/tech/annals-of-technology/the-hidden-costs-of-automated-thinking)
------------------------------------------------------------
"A world of knowledge without understanding becomes a world without discernible cause and effect, in which we grow dependent on our digital concierges to tell us what to do and when."

BKC's Jonathan Zittrain describes the risks of the "
answers first, explanations later" conditions that guide how new discoveries and innovations proliferate — from new drug discoveries, to AI and machine learning.

Read more in the New Yorker (https://www.newyorker.com/tech/annals-of-technology/the-hidden-costs-of-automated-thinking) , or explore the expanded essay on Medium (https://medium.com/@zittrain/from-technical-debt-to-intellectual-debt-in-ai-e05ac56a502c) .

** Ransomware, Data Breaches Expose Gaps in Cyber Insurance Market (https://news.bloomberglaw.com/privacy-and-data-security/ransomware-data-breaches-expose-gaps-in-cyber-insurance-market)
------------------------------------------------------------
Two landmark cases making their way through US courts center on whether insurance companies can refuse to cover the costs of a cyberattack linked to nation-state actors.

“These determinations will have long-lasting impacts on our understanding of tailor-made exclusions in standalone cyber policies, such as state-sponsored attacks,” says incoming BKC affiliate Asaf Lubin.

** 10 tips for covering white supremacy and far-right extremists (https://journalistsresource.org/tip-sheets/reporting/white-supremacy-alt-right-wing-tips/)
------------------------------------------------------------
“Before perpetrating mass violence, some [white supremacists] will litter the internet with a manifesto and other posts loaded with keywords and references to white supremacist content. To limit the reach of this ideology, some newsrooms have adopted a policy of only publishing the perpetrator’s name once — and never in a headline.”

BKC Affiliate Joan Donovan and Jessie Daniels offer this, and nine other tips, for journalists covering white supremacy in a Q&A with Journalist’s Resource, part of the Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy.

** As schools become suffused with ed-tech, is the only response to constant surveillance the right to remain silent? (https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/impactofsocialsciences/2019/07/16/as-schools-become-suffused-with-ed-tech-is-the-only-response-to-constant-surveillance-the-right-to-remain-silent/)
------------------------------------------------------------
“While information from school data can help to adapt pedagogy, evaluate school quality and assess student academic performance, it is crucial to strike a balance by taking a student-centered approach that enables their equitable participation in these processes."  BKC’s Velislava Hillman asks whether the constant monitoring, micromanagement and data collection of students can also protect student's privacy.
