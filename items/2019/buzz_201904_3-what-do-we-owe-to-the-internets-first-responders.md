** The Government’s Computer Fraud and Abuse Act Case Against Assange (https://slate.com/technology/2019/04/julian-assange-computer-fraud-abuse-act-wikileaks-arrest.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

A person can be found guilty of conspiring to commit a crime even if they do not actually commit the crime. "A successful conspiracy charge does not require you to actually violate the underlying statute,” explains BKC's Kendra Albert. “The government doesn’t need to prove that the CFAA was actually violated.”

Read more at Slate... (https://slate.com/technology/2019/04/julian-assange-computer-fraud-abuse-act-wikileaks-arrest.html)

** The Ability to Say NO On the Internet (https://medium.com/codingrights/the-ability-to-say-no-on-the-internet-b4bdebdf46d7)
------------------------------------------------------------

"If consent is a function of power, not all the players have the ability to negotiate nor to reject the conditions imposed by the Terms of Services (ToS) in platforms," says BKC Affiliate Joana Varon and co-author Paz Peña. "[B]eyond 'Agree' to the usage of our personal data, what most of people do is simply 'Obey' the company’s will. Therefore, confronting the fantasy of digital technologies functioning as vehicles of empowerment and democracy, what we have are data societies where control is validated by a legal contract and a bright button of agreement."

Read more at Medium... (https://medium.com/codingrights/the-ability-to-say-no-on-the-internet-b4bdebdf46d7)
