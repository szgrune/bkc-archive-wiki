** Adversarial attacks on medical AI: A health policy challenge (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-03/adversarial-attacks-medical-ai-health-policy-challenge)
------------------------------------------------------------

In the medical context, adversarial attacks on machine learning systems – tiny perturbations to model inputs intended to trick models into making incorrect classifications – have yet to make a significant impact in the wild. But such attacks have significant implications across a wide range of industries.

Read more in Science... (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2019-03/adversarial-attacks-medical-ai-health-policy-challenge)

** Fall from Grace (https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2019/03/21/jill-abramson-journalism-fall-from-grace/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Critic Paul Starr reviews Yochai Benkler, Robert Faris, & Hal Roberts' Network Propaganda alongside Jill Abramson's Merchants of Truth. Network Propaganda analyzes how political news was linked, liked, and shared from 2015 to 2018 and how the news media either amplified or checked the diffusion of falsehoods. "Even though perfect objectivity is impossible and truth is 'necessarily provisional,' Benkler and his colleagues write, truth-seeking organizations function differently from organizations set up to produce propaganda."

Read more at the New York Review of Books... (https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2019/03/21/jill-abramson-journalism-fall-from-grace/)

** Can The Cities Of Tomorrow Be Both Technologically Advanced And Socially Just? (https://today.law.harvard.edu/the-smart-enough-city/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The term “smart city” conjures a promise of streamlined traffic patterns, reduced crime, and open accessibility to WiFi. But, according to BKC Affiliate Ben Green, these proposed technological developments often fail to acknowledge the complex political and social factors of cities, which may cause short and long term harms to residents and democracy.
