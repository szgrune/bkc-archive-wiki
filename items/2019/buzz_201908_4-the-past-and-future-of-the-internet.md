** How YouTube Misinformation Resolved a WhatsApp Mystery in Brazil (https://www.nytimes.com/2019/08/15/the-weekly/how-youtube-misinformation-resolved-a-whatsapp-mystery-in-brazil.html)
------------------------------------------------------------
BKC affiliate Yasodara Cordova tells The New York Times that Brazilians in poorer areas turn to WhatsApp instead of YouTube because they cannot afford the data, but instead share clips from YouTube videos via WhatsApp. Research from BKC faculty associate Virgilio Almeida into the use of WhatsApp in Brazil supports this finding.

** Apply for the Assembly: Disinformation Fellowship (https://www.bkmla.org/fellowship-2020apply)
------------------------------------------------------------
The Assembly Fellowship (https://www.bkmla.org/fellowship-2020about) is an intensive non-residential four-month fellowship for technologists, managers, and policymakers. Assembly Fellows confront problems related to disinformation by creating collaborative provocations or prototypes aimed at better understanding, drawing attention to, and countering disinformation campaigns. Over the course of the fellowship, Assembly Fellows learn and build together with the support of our community. The Assembly Fellowship was first piloted in 2017 and was co-developed with the MIT Media Lab. This year, it is hosted by the Berkman Klein Center.

Applications are open from August 14th to September 15th, 2019.
