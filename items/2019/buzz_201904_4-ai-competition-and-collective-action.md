** Having our cake and eating it too
------------------------------------------------------------
Monday, April 29
Harvard Law School

It has been argued that competitive pressures could cause AI developers to cut corners on the safety of their systems. If this is true, however, why don't we see this dynamic play out more often in other private markets?

Amanda Askell (http://www.amandaaskell.com/) , research scientist in ethics and policy at OpenAI, shares a conceptual framework to help identify levers to improve the prospects for cooperation in collective action problems.

** India may be witnessing the next ‘WhatsApp election’ — and the stakes couldn’t be higher (https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2019/04/25/india-could-see-next-whatsapp-election-stakes-couldnt-be-higher/?utm_term=.2af4a399056b)

** Beyond Bias: Re-imagining the Terms of "Ethical AI" in Criminal Law (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3377921)
------------------------------------------------------------

Chelsea Barabas, a research scientist with the BKC and MIT Media Lab's Ethics and Governance of Artificial Intelligence Initiative, writes that "attempts to render AI-branded tools more accurate by addressing narrow notions of 'bias,' miss the deeper methodological and epistemological issues regarding the fairness of these tools," and calls for an "abolitionist understanding of the role and function of the carceral state, in order to fundamentally reformulate the questions we ask, the way we characterize existing data, and how we identify and fill gaps in existing data regimes of the carceral state."

Read more (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3377921)

** Ifeoma Ajunwa Discusses Artificial Intelligence Used In Hiring (https://www.npr.org/2019/04/08/711169802/cornell-professor-ifeoma-ajunwa-discusses-artificial-intelligence-used-in-hiring)
------------------------------------------------------------

“What we call AI are really machine learning algorithms. And so the people who are creating them do have to be conscious of the ways that human bias could still be encoded in those algorithms. And they would have to make really conscious decisions to eliminate those bias to begin with,” BKC Faculty Associate Ifeoma Ajunwa says in an NPR segment on the use of AI hiring systems.

Read more on NPR (https://www.npr.org/2019/04/08/711169802/cornell-professor-ifeoma-ajunwa-discusses-artificial-intelligence-used-in-hiring)

** Machine Learning Widens the Gap Between Knowledge and Understanding (https://onezero.medium.com/machine-learning-might-render-the-human-quest-for-knowledge-pointless-5425f8b00a45)
------------------------------------------------------------

In an excerpt from David Weinberger’s new book Everyday Chaos, Weinberger explains how machine learning is just one of many tools and strategies that have been increasingly bringing us face-to-face with the incomprehensible intricacy of our everyday world. But this benefit comes at a price: We need to give up our insistence on always understanding our world and how things happen in it.

Read more on Medium (https://onezero.medium.com/machine-learning-might-render-the-human-quest-for-knowledge-pointless-5425f8b00a45)
