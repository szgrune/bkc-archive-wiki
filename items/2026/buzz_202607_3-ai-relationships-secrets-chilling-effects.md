** BKC’s 3 Questions with Amelia Miller
------------------------------------------------------------

What is most misunderstood about Human-AI relationships? In 10 years, what will we wish we had done differently? What keeps you up at night about AI?

The Berkman Klein Center continues its 3 Questions Series with Human-AI Relationship Coach and BKC Fellow Amelia Miller (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/amelia-miller) . Amelia coaches people on their relationships with AI tools to help ensure that the way they talk to chatbots doesn't erode their capacity to connect with real people. BKC had the opportunity to speak with Amelia back in April after she spoke at BKC Co-founder Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) ’s Harvard Law School course on the AI Triad (https://hls.harvard.edu/courses/debates-on-frontier-artificial-intelligence-governance-the-ai-triad/) .

** What’s Your AI Secret?
------------------------------------------------------------

“People shouldn’t feel a need to lie. We should be curious about — even intrigued by — our own mixed feelings, and work toward normalizing appropriate, responsible AI use.”

The Harvard Gazette recently spoke with metaLAB Director Sarah Newman (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/snewman) about her project where she collects anonymous “confessions” about how people are using AI. The project aims to spark conversations on the areas in our life where AI has already outpaced our ability to define appropriate and inappropriate uses of these tools.

The Gazette also spoke with BKC Faculty Director Leah Plunkett (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lplunkett) about difficulties kids often face when AI etiquette is undefined. “It can be hard to get them to understand that actually different roles in a school or community ecosystem have always been permitted, and at times required, different tools and resources, because they do different activities.”

Read the full article here (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2026/07/do-you-have-a-guilty-ai-secret/) .

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Faculty Associate Paola Ricaurte (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/paola-ricaurte) joined REAL Resistance to discuss how AI perpetuates colonial legacies across Brazil, Mexico, and Kenya, and described her community-led work in Querétaro, where AI companies are seizing land for data centers (https://www.themaybe.org/podcast/real-resistance-the-global-fight-against-technocolonialism) . "We are trying to open the conversation about the territories and the communities and their memories...so that we can speak about these infrastructures."

In Foreign Policy, BKC Affiliate Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) argues that the recent OpenAI hack against Hugging Face demonstrates why policy makers should shift their focus from controlling these models to boosting their defense capabilities (https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/07/30/openai-hack-genie-bottle-defense/) ."...in the absence of any international consensus on AI regulation, we need the best AI on the defense. The U.S. government needs to make it clear—or whatever passes for that clarity in this capricious administration—that it will not ban models with sophisticated cyber capabilities."

BKC Faculty Associate Jon Penney (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jpenney) joined the Tech Policy Press Podcast to discuss his latest book, Chilling Effects: Repression, Conformity, and Power in the Digital Age (https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/chilling-effects/22383D541B3BC45C9145E85DA4824E10) , and the ways many governments across the globe use new technological and legal tools to stifle dissent (https://www.techpolicy.press/a-roadmap-for-confronting-the-chilling-effects-of-censorship-surveillance-and-new-technology/) . "What's different in the book and different in the context that we're living is that...these threats with this technology [are] now being increasingly weaponized in sophisticated ways…And the way we think about the concept that we think about the public policy challenge is deeply flawed. We need a new way of thinking about it and we need a robust public policy response."

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
