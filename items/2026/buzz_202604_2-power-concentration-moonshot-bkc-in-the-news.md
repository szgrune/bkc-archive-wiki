** AI and the Concentration of Power
------------------------------------------------------------

“The way that I want to enter this conversation is…concretely, what is being turned into information and who holds that information? And what are the consequences for workers, consumers, and society?”

Last week, BKC hosted a conversation between Junior Fellow at the Harvard Society of Fellows Zoë Hitzig and BKC Director Yochai Benkler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ybenkler) to explore how AI systems are reshaping where knowledge resides, how decisions are made, and how that information is being used by AI firms to make a profit.

The conversation also dove into the language used to talk about AI, including the term “artificial intelligence” itself. “I just wish we called it something different,” said Hitzig. “I think that the entire project of calling this thing ‘artificial intelligence’ is part of what contributes to everyone’s confusion. There’s nothing artificial about it.”

Watch a recording of the event here. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GAOqfU6I9iw)

** ASML’s Frankly Awarded Inaugural “Moonshot” Seed Grant
------------------------------------------------------------

Frankly, the Applied Social Media Lab’s video-based civic discourse platform, has been awarded the Laude Institute (https://www.laude.org/) ’s inaugural “Moonshot” seed grant to explore how AI can improve civic discourse and decision making at scale.

Led in part by BKC Faculty Associate Lawrence Lessig (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/llessig) along with professors from Harvard and MIT, the team is one of eight that have been awarded $250,000 for their project. “Democracy has always struggled with the reality that meaningful deliberation does not scale,” the team writes. “This project aims to change that, building on an existing open-source video deliberation platform to enable millions of people to participate in structured, meaningful civic conversations simultaneously.”

Read the full announcement from Harvard’s School of Engineering and Applied Sciences.  (https://seas.harvard.edu/news/using-ai-banish-echo-chamber)

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Director Rebecca Tushnet (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rtushnet) joins NPR’s All Things Considered to discuss the challenges of policing AI deepfakes and holding perpetrators accountable (https://www.npr.org/2026/04/05/nx-s1-5770462/amid-a-high-profile-scandal-germany-considers-deepfake-porn-punishments) , referencing the US’s Take it Down Act from 2024 and Germany’s current efforts to pass stricter laws. “I think we face a bunch of different problems. One of them is just a lack of strong social norms against this.”

Fellow DZ Kalman (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dz-kalman) https://www.jellomenorah.com/p/faith-in-anthropicwrites (https://www.jellomenorah.com/p/faith-in-anthropic) https://www.jellomenorah.com/p/faith-in-anthropicin his Substack, Jello Menorah, about his experience attending an AI and religion event hosted by Anthropic (https://www.jellomenorah.com/p/faith-in-anthropic) . “In this post I want to talk about what it means that a leading AI firm is interested in engaging with religious leaders and religious thought. I am not going to speak much about the details of the convening itself… At the moment, the details matter less than the interest itself.”

Faculty Associate Petra Molnar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/petra-molnar) writes in Tech Policy Press about the rise of autonomous technologies being deployed for border patrol (https://www.techpolicy.press/ghost-sharks-robodogs-and-the-theater-of-border-technologies/) , and how the conversations around these tools may be missing the bigger picture. “Because of their visceral, sci-fi-like qualities and draconian interventions, robo-dogs and Ghost sharks can obscure the less visible but equally harmful projects… And with the normalization of technology in facets of everyday life, focusing only on the most dramatic tools risks overlooking the vast iceberg of harm hiding beneath the surface.”

Faculty Associate Noah Giansiracusa (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/noah-giansiracusa) asks young readers in Science News Explores: How nutritious is your social media diet? (https://www.snexplores.org/article/how-algorithms-work-online-social) Containing too much social media “junk food,” he says, can have deleterious effects, so individuals should make efforts to diversify their social media diets.

In The Guardian, Affiliate Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) and Ada Palmer point out that LLM training misses the bulk of verbal communication (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/apr/14/ai-language-human-speech) because they are only trained on written, rather than oral, text. "By excluding the overwhelming majority of language production on the planet – people talking, fully and naturally, to each other – these models are being trained to mirror everything but us at our most authentically human.”

Faculty Associate Mark Esposito (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mark-esposito) and Bruno S. Sergi chronicle in Tech Policy Press the shifting legislative landscape about technology (https://www.techpolicy.press/technology-restrictions-have-become-a-central-instrument-of-economic-statecraft/) , highlighting in particular rising tensions between the legislature and the White House. "The current moment calls for something harder [than a merely defensive posture]: a clear articulation of what the US is actually trying to achieve."
