** The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist
------------------------------------------------------------

Will AI be a beneficial technology for society, or will it spell our collective doom? Or perhaps something in between?

Last weekend, BKC Faculty Director Lawrence Lessig hosted a special screening of “The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist” directed by Daniel Roher and Charlie Tyrell. The documentary follows Daniel, whose wife at the time of filming was expecting a child, as he interviews his way across a range of top AI researchers and CEOs to ask a critical question: are we going to be okay?

The screening, hosted at AMC Boston Common, was followed by a panel discussion featuring BKC Co-Founder Jonathan Zittrain and MIT Professor Bailey Flanigan, as well as surprise guests Tristan Harris and Aza Raskin, co-founders of the Center for Humane Technology and both interviewed in the documentary. The panelists shared their personal responses to the documentary, and dove further into some of the challenges the technology poses, but also how AI is exposing pre-existing rifts across governance and society. “This is not really an AI-specific problem,” said Zittrain. “It’s that we haven’t been talking to one another in the ways that meld us into a polity.”

Special thanks to Future of Life Institute for their generous support of this event! If you’d like to read more about the documentary, check out this review by Lessig (https://lessig.substack.com/p/review-the-ai-doc-or-how-i-became?triedRedirect=true) .

** Digital Identity Symposium: Toward User-Owned Identity
------------------------------------------------------------

What if there were a better way to prove your identity online, one that gives you greater control over the information you share in a secure way, without sacrificing access or participation?

Join the Applied Social Media Lab on Thursday, April 16 for the Digital Identity Symposium: Toward User-Owned Identity featuring the public launch of KeyRing, an open source application that demonstrates how technology can enable individuals to take control of their digital identity in a secure and portable way. The symposium will include a live demo, as well as a panel discussion on the future of user-controlled identity including BKC Co-Founder Jonathan Zittrain, BKC Faculty Director James Mickens, and ASML Senior Director Meg Marco.

This is an in-person event at the Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society for Harvard ID holders. Check out the agenda and register here.

** AI & Cybersecurity, Power, and Consciousness
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC has several AI-related events coming up over the next few weeks:

Friday, April 3

Agentic AI and Cybersecurity: Threats, Governance, and Strategy

The same capabilities that make AI valuable for cybersecurity, including autonomous operation, rapid decision-making at scale, and minimal human oversight, are also the ones most likely to cause harm. How can we balance the potential benefits agentic AI can provide against those potential harms?

This panel discussion, generously sponsored by Orrick, Herrington, & Sutcliffe LLP,  will drill down on several questions at the heart of this paradox: what does agentic AI mean for the threat landscape and offense/defense balance in cybersecurity; how should defenders think about the opportunities and drawbacks of employing agentic systems; and who bears liability when AI causes or fails to prevent a breach? ** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Co-Director Rebecca Tushnet (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rtushnet) is quoted in this article from Bloomberg Law on the Court's decision to throw out a copyright-infringement decision against Cox Communications Inc. (https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/cox-wins-supreme-court-ruling-that-curbs-music-copyright-suits) Tushnet notes that the Court's "justices accepted Cox’s argument that the music industry isn’t entitled to an automatic kill switch for internet connections that were used for illegal downloads...partly because it’s too hard to avoid punishing innocent bystanders."

Faculty Associate Leah Plunkett (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lplunkett) spoke with Harvard Law Today about the impact AI is having on the digital landscape (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/ai-content-and-algorithms-is-coming-for-your-kids/) — especially for young people — and how we can protect young consumers from potentially harmful content.

Affiliate Dylan Moses (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dylan-moses) asks in Tech Policy Press how far the "potential" to invoke violence or harm can take us in regulating online prank content (https://www.techpolicy.press/does-youtubes-algorithm-reward-risky-prank-content/) , writing that the risk in allowing such content to be posted lies in "passively endorsed[ing] the increasing escalation towards potential for real-world harm."

Faculty Associate Desmond Patton (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dpatton) , along with coauthors Thomas Abt and Joseph Richardson Jr., argues in Tech Policy Press that most community violence organizations lack the expertise needed to monitor and address social media-fueled violence (https://www.techpolicy.press/social-media-fuels-community-violence-prevention-needs-an-upgrade/) , and suggests stakeholders need to coordinate among themselves: "That means implementing community-led virtual intervention roles, developing ethical early warning systems centered on de-escalation, and instituting clear standards for interpreting online content that distinguish credible threats from protected expression.”

Also in Tech Policy Press, Affiliate Alan Raul (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alan-raul) writes that the Trump-Anthropic dispute (following the tech company's insistence on maintaining guardrails (https://www.techpolicy.press/trumps-anthropic-ban-is-lawless-congress-must-respond-with-a-law/) ) (https://www.techpolicy.press/trumps-anthropic-ban-is-lawless-congress-must-respond-with-a-law/) underscores the need for a national policy to govern transformative AI, particularly when such technology is used for military purposes.

Faculty Associate Woodrow Hartzog (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/woodrow-hartzog) and alum Jessica Silbey (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jsilbey) join The Tech Policy Press Podcast to argue that even intentionally-used AI erodes the foundations of democratic institutions (https://www.techpolicy.press/considering-how-ai-destroys-democratic-institutions/) .

In an article for the International Journal of Communication, Faculty Associate Ethan Zuckerman and Ryan McGrady argue that platforms like YouTube function as accidental cultural archives (https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/25773) "through [their] accumulation of a vast quantity of audiovisual materials by and about humans," comparing these archives to a set of late-20th-century photographs and the ruins of Pompeii.

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
