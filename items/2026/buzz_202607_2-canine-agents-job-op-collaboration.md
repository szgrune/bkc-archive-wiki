** Is That Bot a Pomeranian or a Wolf?
------------------------------------------------------------

What happens when agentic AI causes harm? And who’s liable for the harm caused?

BKC spoke with HLS Lecturer and BKC advisor Jordi Weinstock to learn more about the Canine Agentic Framework, a way to measure an agentic system’s “domesticity”—how much you can control the system, and the system’s dangerousness—and determine who should be held accountable when a bot goes rogue.

“To some people it may seem like this analogy between canines and AI systems is a strain, but as agentic systems are developing and being deployed in the world, they really do seem to be mirroring this rubric.”

Read the full Q&A in The Harvard Gazette here. (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2026/07/is-that-bot-a-pomeranian-or-a-wolf-and-who-to-sue-when-it-bites/)

** BKC @ Navigate 2026
------------------------------------------------------------

Last month, BKC and the IAPP gathered global leaders at the crossroads of data, technology, and societal impact at the Navigate 2026: Digital Policy Leadership Summit. For two days in Portsmouth, NH, policymakers, technologists, academics, and civil society leaders came together to grapple with AI's hardest questions—exchanging ideas, surfacing tensions, and mapping out what effective policy could look like across sectors.

Across all the discussions and presentations, at least one thing was clear: addressing these questions and resolving the growing tension around AI will require collaboration. From protecting human attention, to navigating high-stakes, rapid policymaking, the path forward depends on moving beyond silos and building trust.

Stay tuned for more insights from Navigate within the next few weeks.

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

In an interview with The Philosopher, Co-founder Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) discusses the challenges of regulating agentic AI in an increasingly powerful and evolving space (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cmlaShsyA6A) , and shares his thoughts on regulatory potential. "There’s all sorts of infrastructure that could be built that wouldn’t solve every single problem…but would go a long way to some of the chronic bad things that would be hard to fix later. And it’s kind of shocking to me that there’s not, as yet, any concerted effort."

Affiliates Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) and Nathan Sanders (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/nathan-sanders) write in The Guardian that the AI datacenter debate has overshadowed broader questions about AI's impact on our lives (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jul/09/ai-datacenter-company-politics) . Opposing datacenter expansion, the pair argues, should be a starting point rather than the crux of the AI debate. "As for AI itself, the concentration of power and wealth in these tech companies is the greatest existential risk facing society today. This means we must limit corporate power, especially corporations’ ability to exploit the public and manipulate our political system."

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
