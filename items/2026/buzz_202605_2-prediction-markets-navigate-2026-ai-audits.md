** An Interview with Polymarket’s Shayne Coplan
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC Co-founder Jonathan Zittrain sat down with Polymarket CEO Shayne Coplan at the 2026 Entrepreneurship Summit hosted by Xfund and the Harvard Business School Entrepreneurship Club for a conversation on the emergence of prediction markets and their broader societal implications.

From March Madness and insider injury information to the ousting of Nicolás Maduro, geopolitical upheaval in Iran, and even the sartorial choices of Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the two explored market manipulation, self-fulfilling political dynamics, and the ramifications of turning uncertainty into a tradable product.

The video was referenced by journalists at the New York Times (https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/13/technology/polymarket-insider-trading.html) and Wall Street Journal (https://www.wsj.com/finance/polymarket-bet-disputes-fb1b8c6a) .

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

Faculty Associate Marc Aidinoff (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/marc-aidinoff) and Affiliate Lena Armstrong (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lena-armstrong) , with former Fellows Ellery Roberts Biddle, Karrie Karahalios, J. Nathan Matias, and Christian Sandvig, and with Esha Bhandari, Motahhare Eslami, Danaé Metaxa, Alondra Nelson, and Kristen Vaccaro – together, the Marquand House Collective – authored a valuable new book, Auditing AI (https://auditingai.cc/) . It shows how to audit AI systems and why it’s important.

In a piece for the Knight First Amendment Institute, Affiliate Chinmayi Arun (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/carun) explores the challenges foreign social media platforms pose to US governance schemas (https://knightcolumbia.org/content/the-right-to-access-foreign-communicative-infrastructure) , and argues that these platforms are unique associational and speech infrastructures that should be treated differently from other foreign infrastructure, challenging the Supreme Court's view of these platforms in TikTok Inc. v. Garland.

Faculty Associate Jonathan Zong (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jonathan-zong) and Frank Elavsky highlight the hypocrisy of web developers making sites accessible to AI (https://www.techpolicy.press/the-web-is-being-made-accessible-for-ai-not-people/) while ignoring the accessibility needs of people with disabilities. “When the audience is AI, accommodation is offered with a wink. Beep boop! But when the audience is a disabled person, it has historically been treated as an afterthought.”

Faculty Associate Magda Romanska (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/magda-romanska) reflects on the return to in-person arts programming after COVID-spurred digitization, noting that some audiences — particularly disabled people — found themselves once again shut out from cultural happenings. Read the article in Transforming Society (https://www.transformingsociety.co.uk/2026/05/12/after-lockdown-streaming-disabled-audiences-were-shut-out-again/) .

Faculty Associate Leah Plunkett (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lplunkett) weighs in on emerging concerns about children's digital safety and age assurance laws in this article from USA Today (https://www.usatoday.com/story/tech/2026/05/10/united-kingdom-children-age-verificiation-survey/89985745007/) . "Plunkett believes parents can educate themselves about harmful online content and how easy it is for their children to access it ‒ whether intentionally or not."

Faculty Associate Petra Molnar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/petra-molnar) writes in Tech Policy Press about the impact surveillance technology has had on journalists in Kashmir, India (https://www.techpolicy.press/surveillance-technology-is-silencing-journalists-in-kashmir/) , and interviews a journalist on the ground in the region about the digital and physical challenges.

Faculty Associate Mark Esposito (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mark-esposito) and Bruno Sergi analyze how Europe is using AI to tackle public-sector corruption. Read coverage and access the full analysis in this article from the EU Reporter (https://www.eureporter.co/internet-2/artificial-intelligence/2026/05/14/europes-new-war-on-corruption-can-ai-succeed-where-politicians-failed/) .

Faculty Associate Arvind Narayanan (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/arvind-narayanan) and Affiliate Sayash Kapoor (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sayash-kapoor) outline the problems with “extraordinary government intervention” on AI products (https://knightcolumbia.org/blog/do-ai-risks-require-extraordinary-government-intervention) , including government-enforced restrictions on what companies are allowed to release, and propose ways of improving resilience without restricting innovation.

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
