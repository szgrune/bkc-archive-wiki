** Launching Transparency Hub
------------------------------------------------------------

“Policies and their edit histories tell a story about how the balance between user agency and corporate control has shifted. We believe it's a story worth preserving. And this is exactly what Transparency Hub does.”

Yesterday, the Applied Social Media Lab launched Transparency Hub (https://hub.transparency.berkmancenter.org/) , a platform designed to help people better understand how tech and social media companies use their data. Featuring presentations from ASML Senior Program Manager Kalie Mayberry, Principal Engineer Zoe Robert, and Software Engineer Teagan D’Addeo, the launch webinar introduced both the technical mechanisms at work as well as the broader intentions behind creating the platform.

“As engineers at a historic internet research center within a law school, we felt uniquely positioned to take on the task of archiving these public legal documents,” said Robert. “Through this work, we aim to bring to light the rules that govern our shared digital spaces.”

There was also a brief presentation from Boston University Spark! Program student Kevin Wrenn, who helped build an analytics dashboard (https://analytics.transparency.berkmancenter.org/) using the Transparency Hub database. The dashboard uses data visualization to illustrate key challenges around policy document readability and user rights, highlighting the often restrictive and inaccessible nature of these company policies.

Watch the full event (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6rv9sU59ODo) , and read more about the BU Spark! Program’s Transparency Hub Analytics Dashboard (https://asml.cyber.harvard.edu/2026/03/02/inside-policy-analytics-dashboard/) .

** From Ideas to Impact: BKC Launches Inaugural Student Essay Contest
------------------------------------------------------------

Are you a current Harvard student with something to say (or write) about artificial intelligence’s impact on society?

In February, BKC’s Student Engagement Team—including Program Manager Valerie Gomez, Harvard College student Maya Dummett, and Graduate School of Design student Ami Mehta—hosted an info session and “writing gym” designed to help students prepare for the Center’s inaugural Student Essay Contest co-organized with Tech Policy Press.

Check out this write-up (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2026-02/ideas-impact-bkc-launches-its-inaugural-student-essay-contest) , as well as this recording of the session (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-KWE9NZknls) , featuring Tech Policy Press CEO and Editor Justin Hendrix. ** APPLY: AI and the Law
------------------------------------------------------------

BKC is now accepting applications for AI and the Law: Navigating the New Legal Landscape (https://go.execed.law.harvard.edu/l/868411/2025-11-25/n8k6mx) , developed in partnership between the BKC and Harvard Law School Executive Education.

This three-day course will give senior leaders a foundational understanding of AI and how this new technology is reshaping legal frameworks and the practice of law. Using hands-on learning components, participants will also learn how to apply these novel tools within their own organizations. ** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

In Tech Policy Press, Faculty Associate Petra Molnar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/petra-molnar) notes the ways that migration is reshaped by surveillance technologies (https://www.techpolicy.press/why-global-majority-leadership-matters-for-tech-and-migration-policy-today/) , artificial intelligence, and data-driven governance. She concludes: “if accountability, care, and participation are established where power is most aggressively exercised, they can set precedents for more just technological futures.”

Affiliate Alan Raul (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alan-raul) argues in Bloomberg Law that ICE's aggressive strategy of approaching citizens and non-citizens alike is prohibited by the Fourth Amendment provision against unreasonable searches (https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-exchange-insights-and-commentary/founding-fathers-guarded-against-show-me-your-papers-ice-raids) . Courts, Raul says, must play a role in distinguishing between “ostensible” reasons for government agents’ demanding information and “actual” ones.

Affiliate Sayash Kapoor (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sayash-kapoor) and Faculty Associate Arvind Narayanan (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/arvind-narayanan) are among the coauthors of a paper proposing a new holistic mode of evaluating AI agents. They describe the work in a post on their AI as Normal Technology blog (https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-towards-a-science-of-ai) , suggesting that developers measure reliability as separate from accuracy in order to make up for lags in the former domain.

Affiliate Jack Cushman (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jcushman) , director of the HLS Library Information Lab, shares the Agent Protocols Tech Tree, a visual, videogame-style tech tree of the evolving protocols supporting AI agents. Check out this work in progress at the Library Innovation Lab (https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2026/02/23/agent-protocols-tech-tree/) blog.

In this Guardian op-ed (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/mar/03/anthropic-openai-pentagon-ethics) , Affiliates Nathan Sanders (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/nathan-sanders) and Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) remark on the Department of Defense”s transition from Anthropic to OpenAI technologies given the former”s refusal to allow its models to be used for mass surveillance or fully autonomous weaponry.

Work being done around cryptographic personhood credentials at BKC’s Applied Social Media Lab (https://asml.cyber.harvard.edu/) got a shoutout in this ZDNET article about “Linux ID.” (https://www.zdnet.com/article/linux-kernel-maintainers-new-way-of-authenticating-developers-and-code/)

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
