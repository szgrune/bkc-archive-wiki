** Meet the Community: Hayley Song
------------------------------------------------------------

The Berkman Klein Center is excited to introduce Hae Jin (Hayley) Song as a Fellow. Also an AI Research Fellow at ThoughtWorks, Hayley peers inside today’s most powerful AI systems and seeks to understand how we might make them safer and more predictable.

Her work looks beyond AI’s outputs to uncover the hidden structures that shape how models learn, reason, and sometimes go wrong. By mapping the internal “fingerprints” of generative models, she explores how to explain their behavior, spot failures like bias or deepfakes, and even steer models toward better outcomes.

Read Hayley’s Q&A here (https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2026-01/hayley-song-qa) .

** More from the BKC Community
------------------------------------------------------------

In a feature for Nature, Faculty Associate and metaLAB principal Kim Albrecht presents Lost Science, a data visualization project that seeks to reflect the impact of cuts to research grants in the US (https://www.nature.com/immersive/d41586-026-00088-9/index.html) . From Albrecht: “At a time when political and institutional forces increasingly build walls and enforce division, interdisciplinary collaboration is an essential antidote. The culture of exchange at BKC and metaLAB has been central to how I approach research that operates between data analysis, design, and critical inquiry.”

In The Conversation, Affiliates Bruce Schneier and Nathan Sanders discuss how AI companies are pursuing ways to monetize attention on their platforms (https://theconversation.com/could-chatgpt-convince-you-to-buy-something-threat-of-manipulation-looms-as-ai-companies-gear-up-to-sell-ads-272859) , and how it might impact consumers. “The problem is not the technology itself; fast ways to find information and communicate with friends and family can be wonderful capabilities. The problem is the priorities of the corporations who own these platforms and for whose benefit they are operated.”

In a profile in CEOWorld Magazine, BKC Affiliate Maroussia Lévesque offers insight into her work at the intersection of law, technology, and governance (https://ceoworld.biz/2025/12/31/maroussia-levesque-shaping-the-rules-of-artificial-intelligence/) . “‘I don’t study AI because it’s trendy… I study it because it quietly shapes who has power, who is seen, and who is left out.’”

Faculty Associate Noah Giansiracusa discusses algorithmic pricing (aka dynamic pricing), how it works, and the challenges it presents legally and socially. Read his op-ed from the Boston Globe (https://web.archive.org/web/20251227101111/https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/12/26/opinion/algorithmic-surveillance-pricing-price-discrimination/) , and listen to his interview on WBZ’s NightSide with Dan Rae (https://wbznewsradio.iheart.com/featured/nightside-with-dan-rea/content/2026-01-05-1002-nightside-with-dan-rea-what-to-know-about-algorithmic-pricing/) .

In an article for Tech Policy Press (https://www.techpolicy.press/how-to-make-sense-of-trumps-tiktok-deal/) , Faculty Associate Anupam Chander breaks down President Trump’s deal allowing TikTok to transfer ownership of its app to a group of approved investors, including software company Oracle. “Ultimately, we may have traded the possibility of foreign propaganda for the possibility of domestic propaganda.”

Also in Tech Policy Press, Affiliate Marissa Gerchick co-authors this article summarizing findings from a report (https://www.techpolicy.press/making-sense-of-ai-policy-using-computational-tools/) on using computational tools to track trends and themes around AI legislation in the US. “While our focus here is AI legislation, our research and recommendations can be applied to other policy areas seeing a surge in bills across jurisdictions, thus helping to understand and strengthen emerging legislation.”

For more, visit the BKC website’s Community (https://cyber.harvard.edu/community) section, where writing (and more) from fellows, faculty associates, affiliates, and staff is aggregated.
