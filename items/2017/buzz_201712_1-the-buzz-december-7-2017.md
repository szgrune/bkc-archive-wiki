How the Supreme Court Could Keep Police from Using Your Cellphone to Spy on You (https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/posteverything/wp/2017/11/27/how-the-supreme-court-could-keep-police-from-using-your-cellphone-to-spy-on-you/)
BKC Fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  reports on Carpenter v. United States in this article in the Washington Post (https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/posteverything/wp/2017/11/27/how-the-supreme-court-could-keep-police-from-using-your-cellphone-to-spy-on-you/?utm_term=.9429bd0e4b35) .  "The issue here is not whether the police should be allowed to use that data to help solve crimes. Of course they should. The issue is whether that information should be protected by the warrant process that requires the police to have probable cause to investigate you and get approval by a court," Schneier writes.

Gaming Out Online: Black Lesbian Identity Development and Community Building in Xbox Live (http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10894160.2018.1384293)
In this recently publishedarticle in the Journal of Lesbian Studies (http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10894160.2018.1384293) , BKC Faculty Associate Kishonna Gray (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kgray) explores the experiences of lesbians of color "gayming out online." Gray's ethnographic research reveals that the private online communities formed to support these groups are unique spaces in which marginalized viewpoints and interests are valued.
