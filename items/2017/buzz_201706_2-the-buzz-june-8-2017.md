https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2017/06/06/russias-attempt-to-hack-voting-systems-shows-that-our-elections-need-better-security/?utm_term=.a1241b26ae04
Russia's attempt to hack voting systems shows that our elections need better security. (https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2017/06/06/russias-attempt-to-hack-voting-systems-shows-that-our-elections-need-better-security/?utm_term=.a1241b26ae04)
In this piece for The Washington Post (https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2017/06/06/russias-attempt-to-hack-voting-systems-shows-that-our-elections-need-better-security/?utm_term=.a1241b26ae04) Bruce Schneier covers the importance of cybersecurity in establishing and maintaining trust in democracy. In the wake of a recent NSA leak suggesting Russian hackers may have interfered with voting machines used in the 2016 election, Schneier says, “Democratic elections serve two purposes. The first is to elect the winner. But the second is to convince the loser. After the votes are all counted, everyone needs to trust that the election was fair and the results accurate.”

Q&A with Sonia Livingstone (http://www.iicom.org/themes/item/q-a-with-sonia-livingstone?category_id=283)
In this Q&A with The International Institute of Communications for this month’s Policy World, Professor Sonia Livingstone (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/slivingstone) speaks about some of the challenges facing children’s rights activists, scholars, and policymakers in the digital age. Livingstone offers insight into why she ‘edited’ the UNCRC and explains that all too often children’s rights are overlooked based on the assumption that internet users are adults.
Event: Potential Uses of Miniature Spectrometers to Mitigate the Health Crisis in Developing Countries
https://cyber.harvard.edu/events/2017/06/Fisher#RSVP
Monday, June 12
at 12:00pm

Recent advances in several unrelated fields – miniature mass spectrometry; artificial intelligence; and drug databases – may soon radically increase the ability of public-health workers to assess the chemical composition of pharmaceutical products rapidly, cheaply, and “in the field.” This potential, in turn, offers a variety of ways in which vaccines and medicines could be distributed more efficiently to poor patients in developing countries. William Fisher (http://hls.harvard.edu/faculty/directory/10270/Fisher) , Harvard Law School Professor of Intellectual Property Law and Faculty Director, Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society, will discuss these possibilities and a pilot project in Namibia designed to test them.

Watch: Can We Talk?
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iYPnBdgHdrg
Digital communication technologies are often heralded as comprehensive solutions to complex challenges faced by people living with a disability. However, recent research from Professors Elizabeth Ellcessor and Meryl Alper bring into question the assumption that such technologies are inherently empowering. Drawing on their recent books Giving Voice: Mobile Communication, Disability, and Inequality (https://mitpress.mit.edu/books/giving-voice) (Alper, 2017) and Restricted Access: Media, Disability, and the Politics of Participation (https://nyupress.org/books/9781479853434/) (Elcessor, 2016), they discuss disability, technology, and inclusion in public and private digitally mediated spaces.
