** Online Education After The End of Net Neutrality (https://medium.com/digital-sociology-at-vcu/online-education-after-the-end-of-net-neutrality-d42d69a6c6be)
------------------------------------------------------------

"Online education was built, and sold to stakeholders, on the premise of affordable, accessible internet access," writes Tressie McMillan Cottom in this Medium post (https://medium.com/digital-sociology-at-vcu/online-education-after-the-end-of-net-neutrality-d42d69a6c6be) . "Today’s FCC ruling calls into question if the assumption on which online education was built will remain true."

Net Neutrality, Kanye, and Hot Dogs (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/net-neutrality-kanye-and-hot-dogs-b3cfae106d75)
For a summary of net neutrality and an analysis of how the Day of Action led to increased media coverage and public awareness, check out this this Medium piece (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/net-neutrality-kanye-and-hot-dogs-b3cfae106d75) , co-authored by several members of the BKC community. Using Google Trends and Media Cloud, their research concludes that, "it’s clear that the Day of Action drove a substantial increase in both public and media interest in net neutrality."

Citizen Lab Launches Security Planner (https://citizenlab.ca/2017/12/citizen-lab-launches-security-planner/)
The Citizen Lab (https://citizenlab.ca/) has announced its new project, The Security Planner (https://securityplanner.org/#/) , described as "a new approach in making the first steps of online safety easier to take, even for those who don't feel digitally savvy." The Security Planner is an online platform that offers users safety recommendations based on their personal internet habits and the devices they use. You can learn more about the project and its background here (https://securityplanner.org/#/who-we-are) .

ICYMI: A Pessimist’s Guide to the Future of Technology
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jv3ZKXvDxIY
In this recent Berkman Klein Center lunch talk (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jv3ZKXvDxIY) , author and an award-winning game designer Dr. Ian Bogost offers a new way to think about and plan for the future of technology, which anticipates what might go right and wrong once unproven tech mainstreams quickly. He considers a technology that has not yet mainstreamed—autonomous vehicles—as a test case on how we should think about the future of tech.
