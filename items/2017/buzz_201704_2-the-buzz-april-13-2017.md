The Future of IoT in Cities (https://www.knightfoundation.org/articles/concrete-steps-towards-an-urban-internet-of-things)
A Summary Report

In November 2016, the Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society’sResponsive Communities initiative (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/99650) and the Knight Foundation, under the auspices of the NetGain Partnership (https://netgainpartnership.org/) , convened city officials from around the world who are working on the frontlines of urban Internet of Things. Together with representatives of nonprofit, philanthropic, and research institutions, these officials offered candid assessments of their accomplishments and the challenges that still lie ahead. Read the summary here (https://www.knightfoundation.org/articles/concrete-steps-towards-an-urban-internet-of-things) .
Read: Recent news and commentary from around the community
https://globalvoices.org/2017/03/27/how-memory-and-digital-media-can-pave-the-way-to-peace-in-colombia/
How memory and digital media can pave the way to peace in Colombia (https://globalvoices.org/2017/03/27/how-memory-and-digital-media-can-pave-the-way-to-peace-in-colombia/)
The National Center of Historical Memory in Colombia is working on a new peace building initiative: Alfabetizaciones Digitales. In this article on Global Voices (https://globalvoices.org/2017/03/27/how-memory-and-digital-media-can-pave-the-way-to-peace-in-colombia/) , Andrés Lombana-Bermudez (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alombana)  explains how the program allows victims of the armed conflict to use digital media tools to tell their stories.

http://limn.it/the-illicit-aura-of-information/?utm_content=buffer054dd&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer The illicit aura of information (http://limn.it/the-illicit-aura-of-information/?utm_content=buffer054dd&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer)
Do information leaks change the nature of communication? In this article on limn (http://limn.it/the-illicit-aura-of-information/?utm_content=buffer054dd&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer) , Molly Sauter (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/msauter) says yes. Information leaks, unless used with journalistic integrity, can lead to the creation of conspiracy theories. She examines the cases of #Pizzagate in the 2016 election and Climategate to understand the information auras of these leaks.

https://www.bloomberg.com/view/articles/2017-04-05/trump-and-xi-could-break-the-global-system Trump and XI could break the system (https://www.bloomberg.com/view/articles/2017-04-05/trump-and-xi-could-break-the-global-system)
The United States and China are reliant upon each other in the global economy. In this Bloomberg View article (https://www.bloomberg.com/view/articles/2017-04-05/trump-and-xi-could-break-the-global-system) , Mark Wu (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mwu) reflects on the World Trade Organization and how China may not be following all the rules involved in global trade.
https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/is-it-ethical-to-source-dank-memes-from-nazi-mines
You are almost definitely sharing memes made by Nazis (https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/is-it-ethical-to-source-dank-memes-from-nazi-mines)
Memes were extensively used — especially by right-wing neo-Nazis — as a political tool in the 2016 election. Motherboard (https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/is-it-ethical-to-source-dank-memes-from-nazi-mines) examines the rise of right-wing memes and how they've seeped into the mainstream, while Molly Sauter (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/msauter) and Amy Johnson (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ajohnson) reflect on the problematic nature of many memes.
