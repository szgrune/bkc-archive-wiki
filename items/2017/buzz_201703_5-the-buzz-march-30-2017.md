We are excited to announce the addition of three new members to the Berkman Klein Center Board of Directors, enhancing the Center’s expertise on subjects ranging from computer systems research to the policies governing intellectual property in developing countries.

The new Directors are Ruth Okediji (https://www.law.umn.edu/profiles/ruth-okediji) , a professor at the University of Minnesota Law School who will join the Harvard Law School faculty in July (https://today.law.harvard.edu/leading-ip-scholar-ruth-okediji-joins-harvard-law-faculty/) ; Margo Seltzer (https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/margo/) , the Herchel Smith Professor of Computer Science at Harvard’s John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences, and Director of its Center for Research on Computation and Society; and Rebecca Tushnet (https://www.law.georgetown.edu/faculty/tushnet-rebecca-l.cfm) , a professor at Georgetown Law School, who also joins the Harvard Law School faculty this July (https://today.law.harvard.edu/rebecca-tushnet-joins-harvard-law-faculty-professor/) .

http://www.cnn.com/2017/03/22/opinions/puzzling-out-tsa-laptop-ban/ Why did the White House create the laptop travel ban? (http://www.cnn.com/2017/03/22/opinions/puzzling-out-tsa-laptop-ban/)
In this post (http://www.cnn.com/2017/03/22/opinions/puzzling-out-tsa-laptop-ban/) for CNN, Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) examines the White House's latest ban — prohibiting laptops on certain international flights. He writes, "in the end, national security measures based on secret information require us to trust the government. That trust is at historic low levels right now, so people both in the US and other countries are rightly skeptical of the official unsatisfying explanations. The new laptop ban highlights this mistrust."

https://www.wired.com/2017/03/tech-giants-cant-bear-weight-battling-online-hate/?mbid=nl_31817_p7&CNDID=25748860
Germany’s flawed plan to fight hate speech by fining tech giants millions (https://www.wired.com/2017/03/tech-giants-cant-bear-weight-battling-online-hate/?mbid=nl_31817_p7&CNDID=25748860)
It's no secret that many tech giants — including Twitter and Facebook — have issues with hate speech and abuse on their platforms. Germany's Minister of Justice is proposing a solution: fining social media companies for not responding quickly enough to their users' concerns. In this Wired article (https://www.wired.com/2017/03/tech-giants-cant-bear-weight-battling-online-hate/?mbid=nl_31817_p7&CNDID=25748860) , Kate Coyer (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kcoyer) explains why this isn't a great solution: "The high fines for not taking down illegal content quickly enough give companies an incentive to rather remove content than not—since there are no fines if they wrongly determine to remove content."
