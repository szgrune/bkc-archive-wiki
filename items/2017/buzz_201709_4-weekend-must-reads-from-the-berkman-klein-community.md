New Work: AI & Art
https://kimalbrecht.github.io/ai-senses/

AI Senses visualizes sensor data of the machines that surround us to develop an understanding how they experience the world (https://kimalbrecht.github.io/ai-senses/)

“We’re trying to make machines seem human, but they’re not,” says Kim Albrecht, a designer at Berkman Klein's metaLAB (https://metalabharvard.github.io/) . “They behave in such different ways to how we see and interact with the world.” Albrecht's new project (https://kimalbrecht.github.io/ai-senses/)  translates the hidden senses of computers, tablets, and smartphones into visualizations that raise interesting questions about the artificial intelligence and the growing power of our machines.

Read the coverage in Wired (https://www.wired.com/story/see-the-world-through-the-eyes-of-your-phone/) and Fast Company (https://www.fastcodesign.com/90144612/these-eerie-gifs-show-how-your-phone-feels-hears-and-sees-you) , or try the project for yourself (https://kimalbrecht.github.io/ai-senses/) !

THE SOCIAL NETWORK
Facebook was in headlines this week, and on the minds of our community. Read below for a roundup of commentary from our fellows, alumni, and affiliates:

Facebook’s Ad Scandal Isn’t a ‘Fail,’ It’s a Feature (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/09/23/opinion/sunday/facebook-ad-scandal.html?_r=0)
Revelations from Pro Publica that Facebook had allowed companies to buy ads targeted to users who identify with hateful or violent sentiments strikes Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) as the tip of the iceberg. In the New York Times (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/09/23/opinion/sunday/facebook-ad-scandal.html?_r=2)  Tufekci argues that the combination of scale, automation, profit-motive, and dependence on users to police content reveals deep structural challenges.

Could public pressure cause Facebook to regulate itself? (https://www.marketplace.org/2017/09/25/tech/social-pressure-social-network)
"Facebook has so much market power, essentially it's a monopoly in social networks," says Yochai Benkler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ybenkler)  in this interview for Marketplace Tech (https://www.marketplace.org/2017/09/25/tech/social-pressure-social-network)  on whether greater oversight might be on the horizon. "It's appropriate when you have a fast-moving technology for regulation not to jump ahead. But now it's already 2017."

The More We Connect, the Better It Gets — for Facebook (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/09/26/opinion/facebook-free-basics.html?mcubz=0&_r=0)
Of Facebook's Free Basics program — an attempt to bridge a global digital divide by offering access to a limited number of websites at no charge —  Ellery Biddle (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ebiddle) says the intent is good, but the devil is in the details. In the New York Times (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/09/26/opinion/facebook-free-basics.html?mcubz=0&_r=0) Biddle comments that Facebook "keeps users in a confined space, where the company can monitor and analyze their habits for profit."

Stop and Frisk Online (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2056305117733344)
"Police are increasingly monitoring social media to build evidence for criminal indictments," Berkman Klein fellow Desmond Patton (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dpatton) and co-authors note in their new paper (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2056305117733344) , which describes everyday racism in digital policing as a burgeoning conceptual framework for understanding racialized social media surveillance by law enforcement.

Cross-Border Data Access Primer (https://lawfareblog.com/cross-border-data-access-primer)
How countries agree to share necessary sensitive electronic data, particularly in the investigation of criminal activity, has long been a thorny issue. On the Lawfare blog (https://lawfareblog.com/cross-border-data-access-primer) the authors of our recent primer (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/09/berklett) on a proposed US-UK agreement on data access break down the challenges of integrating a tangle of legal requirements.

Clinic Files Amicus Brief on Behalf of Members of Congress in Support of Access to Law (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2017/sep/19/white-supremacist-statues-must-fall-scientists)
The Berkman Klein Cyberlaw Clinic (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/)  filed a brief this week in  support of Public.Resource.org (http://public.resource.org/)  in a major case addressing whether the text of law can be copyrighted. Often when model codes and standards become part of federal, state, or local regulations, the text is locked behind paywalls. The Clinic argues (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/09/26/clinic-files-amicus-brief-on-behalf-of-members-of-congress-in-support-of-access-to-law/) that Public Resource’s free and open publication of the law is fair use.

Tech and Space Camp (https://zumixboston.wordpress.com/2017/09/17/tech-and-space-camp-zumix-summer-radio-workshop/)
Working with two youth arts and media organizations in the Boston area — ZUMIX and The Urbano Project — Berkman Klein affiliate Mariel Garcia Montes (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mgarciamontes)  spent summer of 2017 developing a media literacy curriculum (https://zumixboston.wordpress.com/2017/09/17/tech-and-space-camp-zumix-summer-radio-workshop/) for ages 13 to 17. By learning radio production skills, a crew of summer camp participants learned greater lessons about how news is created and understood.
