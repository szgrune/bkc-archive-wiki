Open Call for Submissions:
Latin American Collective Book on Youth, Digital Transformation, and Inclusion

Digitally Connected (http://www.digitallyconnected.org/) and a collection of international partners are inviting proposals for articles or creative pieces on digital practices and processes of social inclusion that youth are developing in diverse Latin American contexts. Contributors are invited to explore topics such as civic participation and digital platforms, learning and new literacies, emergent cultures and new identities, rights and responsibilities, privacy, and the digital economy. (We ask that all submissions be written in either Spanish or Portuguese.)

For more information, please visit: http://jovenes.digital/

http://blogs.lse.ac.uk/parenting4digitalfuture/2017/02/21/making-the-familiar-strange-studying-the-syrian-refugee-crisis/ Making the Familiar Strange by Studying the Refugee Crisis (http://blogs.lse.ac.uk/parenting4digitalfuture/2017/02/21/making-the-familiar-strange-studying-the-syrian-refugee-crisis/) "You don’t have to be an academic researching the Syrian refugee crisis to end up studying it; you just need to study people. That’s what happened for me in 2013," writes Meryl Alper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/malper) in this blog (http://blogs.lse.ac.uk/parenting4digitalfuture/2017/02/21/making-the-familiar-strange-studying-the-syrian-refugee-crisis/) for the London School of Economics. http://www.projectinfolit.org/takis-metaxas-smart-talk.html
Separating Truth From Lies (http://www.projectinfolit.org/takis-metaxas-smart-talk.html)
"It is tempting to look at a social problem that has gained greater attention due to technology, such as fake news or trolling, and think the solution will be exclusively technological...But at the end of the day, we will not solve this problem by clicking buttons to receive answers. Much of the responsibility resides with us, the users of the internet," says Takis Metaxas (http://crcs.seas.harvard.edu/people/panagiotis-takis-metaxas) in this interview (http://www.projectinfolit.org/takis-metaxas-smart-talk.html) about his TwitterTrails project, which tracks fake news on the social media platform.

The Alternative Facts of Cable Companies (https://backchannel.com/the-alternate-facts-of-cable-companies-f0cd1e10e66#.dtayimcj6)
Recent crackdowns on Internet provider monopolies by New York's attorney general could help "generate momentum for better, faster, wholesale fiber networks controlled by cities and localities themselves," says Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) in a piece for Backchannel (https://backchannel.com/the-alternate-facts-of-cable-companies-f0cd1e10e66#.t6srdozh6) . "If that happened, retail competition would bloom."

Podcasting for the People (http://www.cbc.ca/radio/q/thursday-feb-16-2017-paying-tribute-to-stuart-mclean-1.3982583/how-podcasting-became-the-medium-for-the-people-1.3983769)

Survival of the Cryptic (http://limn.it/survival-of-the-cryptic/)
"The strategic revelation of government information made the workings of political officials more opaque, rather than legible to the public...This is an indication of the limits of transparency: while it remains a favorable condition for democracy, whether or not it will effectively aid the public in democratic deliberation depends very much upon by whom and for whom transparency is working," writes Sarah Myers West (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swest) in this article (http://limn.it/survival-of-the-cryptic/) for Limn.
