http://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v30/30HarvJLTech601.pdf
Ready Lawyer One: Legal Issues in the Innovation of Virtual Reality  (http://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v30/30HarvJLTech601.pdf)
What challenges do legal scholars and practitioners face as virtual reality technologies become more accessible to everyday consumers? Harvard Law School J.D. candidate and Berkman Klein Center affiliate Crystal Nwaneri (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/cnwaneri) presents a comprehensive lay of the land in this four-part note (http://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v30/30HarvJLTech601.pdf) published in the Harvard Journal of Law and Technology (http://jolt.law.harvard.edu/) . Nwaneri says “it is our job to be ready.”

https://medium.com/@judithd/the-robot-dog-fetches-for-whom-a9c1dd0a458a
The Robot Dog Fetches for Whom? (https://medium.com/@judithd/the-robot-dog-fetches-for-whom-a9c1dd0a458a)
What could happen as society advances from robot pets to robot persuaders? That’s the question that Judith Donath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jdonath) , a member of the Berkman Klein Fellows Advisory Board, asks in this medium article (https://medium.com/@judithd/the-robot-dog-fetches-for-whom-a9c1dd0a458a) . Donath tracks the history of AIBO the robotic dog before gesturing towards more complex and commercially minded examples, such as Alexa.

http://www.gizmodo.co.uk/2017/06/you-almost-definitely-dont-know-all-the-ways-facebook-tracks-you/
You Almost Definitely Don't Know All The Ways Facebook Tracks You (http://www.gizmodo.co.uk/2017/06/you-almost-definitely-dont-know-all-the-ways-facebook-tracks-you/)
Many people take it as common knowledge that most social media networks collect user data. But are they aware of the types of correlations that platforms like Facebook can make with that data? Berkmain Klein Fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) is quoted in this Gizmodo (http://www.gizmodo.co.uk/2017/06/you-almost-definitely-dont-know-all-the-ways-facebook-tracks-you/) article which sheds light on the many ways that Facebook and its extended surveillance network can track its users.

Open Sources Comes of Age (http://www.linuxjournal.com/content/open-source-comes-age)
Open Source has finally come of age, says Berkman Klein Center Alumnus Fellow Doc Searls. In this piece in Linux Journal (http://www.linuxjournal.com/content/open-source-comes-age) , Searls looks at Open Source’s biggest future challenges and references the Organization and Structure of Open Source Development Initiatives (https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/30805146) report from the Cyberlaw Clinic at the Center.

http://blog.castac.org/2017/06/computing-design-disability/
Personal Computing and Personhood in Design and Disability (http://blog.castac.org/2017/06/computing-design-disability/)
Professor Meryl Alper discusses the role that augmented and alternative communication technologies play in the everyday lives of those for whom such devices are ordinary. She writes on Platypus (http://blog.castac.org/2017/06/computing-design-disability/) that “Personalization and ownership radically transform these standardized and medicalized objects in unexpected ways, and this has significant implications for inclusive, person-centered design for all.”

Internet Culture and Protest Culture (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BfQK61oR0HE)
In this keynote from the Personal Democracy Forum 2017, Berkman Klein Fellow An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina) explains how object culture is adapting to both internet culture and protest culture. From tracking how moments become products, to the prevalence of digital hashtags on physical signs, Mina asks the question, "What kind of information flows occur when internet culture and protest culture collide?"
