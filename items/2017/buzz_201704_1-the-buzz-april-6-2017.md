Data analytics and the fight against housing blight (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/99852)
A new working paper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/99852) from the Responsive Communities Lab course, taught by Susan Crawford (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/bio/susan-crawford/) and Waide Warner (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/wwarner) , investigates how communities are solving housing blight with data analytics. Harvard Law Student Bradley Pough (https://www.linkedin.com/in/bradleypough/) and Harvard School of Engineering and Applied Sciences Student Qian Wan (https://www.linkedin.com/in/qian-wan-63416846/) write about the solutions found in New Orleans and Detroit, and how other cities are adopting their technology-driven strategies.

https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2017/mar/30/snoops-buy-your-browsing-history-us-congress Snoops may soon be able to buy your browsing history. Thank the US Congress (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2017/mar/30/snoops-buy-your-browsing-history-us-congress)
The U.S. Congress recently voted against an Internet privacy regulation that would protect consumer's data. In this Guardian article (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2017/mar/30/snoops-buy-your-browsing-history-us-congress) , Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) examines how these new regulation rollbacks will affect Internet users — and how the FCC and FTC might intervene.

http://www.metro.us/boston/mit-researchers-want-to-protect-your-web-privacy/zsJqcA---mkYkXvNePFSU You want real web privacy? Get ready to pay for it (http://www.metro.us/boston/mit-researchers-want-to-protect-your-web-privacy/zsJqcA---mkYkXvNePFSU)
In light of privacy rollbacks, MIT and Stanford researchers have created Splinter, a tool that encrypt's a user's data and splits it between database centers. The catch: users have to pay for the service, which may be an issue. In this Metro article (http://www.metro.us/boston/mit-researchers-want-to-protect-your-web-privacy/zsJqcA---mkYkXvNePFSU) , David O'Brien (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dobrien) explains, "It’s been discussed for years now and could be one alternative path." The problem, though, is that payment for privacy “hasn’t been supported by demand and maybe won’t ever be.”
https://advox.globalvoices.org/2017/03/30/netizen-report-india-had-31-internet-shutdowns-in-2016-how-many-did-your-country-have/
India had 31 Internet shutdowns in 2016. How many did your country have? (https://advox.globalvoices.org/2017/03/30/netizen-report-india-had-31-internet-shutdowns-in-2016-how-many-did-your-country-have/)
Internet shutdowns are often triggered by local and regional authorities as a form of censorship. Ellery Roberts Biddle (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ebiddle)  and Sarah Myers West (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swest) contributed to this Global Advox article (https://advox.globalvoices.org/2017/03/30/netizen-report-india-had-31-internet-shutdowns-in-2016-how-many-did-your-country-have/) on global Internet trends, including challenges and victories.

50 grand challenges for the 21st century (http://www.bbc.com/future/story/20170331-50-grand-challenges-for-the-21st-century)

http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2017/04/03/where-speech-goes-repression-follows-the-global-trend-of-criminalizing-online-speech/
Where speech goes, repression follows: the global trend of criminalizing online speech (http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2017/04/03/where-speech-goes-repression-follows-the-global-trend-of-criminalizing-online-speech/)
Many countries have introduced laws aimed at curbing criminal activity online — but that's not necessarily the effect that they have. Instead, these laws are causing tension between Internet users and their governments. Nani Jansen Reventlow discusses the repercussions of criminalizing online speech in this (http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2017/04/03/where-speech-goes-repression-follows-the-global-trend-of-criminalizing-online-speech/) Council on Foreign Relations (http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2017/04/03/where-speech-goes-repression-follows-the-global-trend-of-criminalizing-online-speech/)  post (http://blogs.cfr.org/cyber/2017/04/03/where-speech-goes-repression-follows-the-global-trend-of-criminalizing-online-speech/) .
