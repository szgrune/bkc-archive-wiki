** Voices from the Atrato River
------------------------------------------------------------
"Members of these organizations use digital media, particularly mobile phones with multimedia recording capabilities, to produce and circulate their own stories of resistance to violence, and their own creative expressions among wider audiences," writes Andrés Lombana-Bermudez (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alombana) in this piece (http://revista.drclas.harvard.edu/book/voices-atrato-river) about the Project Memorias del Rio Atrato, which allows communities to tell their stories.

Changing the AI narrative
"Prominent AI stories pit man versus machine, relying on one of the most fundamental examples of narrative conflict to move the story forward. It's not surprising that stories we tell tend towards this form—that we should position ourselves as the protagonist and pit the AI technology in opposition as the antagonist," says Sara M. Watson (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/swatson) . Her article (https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/we-need-to-tell-better-stories-about-our-ai-future?utm_content=buffer3fb6e&utm_medium=social&utm_source=facebook.com&utm_campaign=buffer) for Motherboard argues that we need to reimagine how we tell stories about AI.

Pros and Cons of the Algorithm Age
A recent Pew Research Center report (http://www.pewinternet.org/2017/02/08/code-dependent-pros-and-cons-of-the-algorithm-age/) examined how algorithms are shaping our world, noting that "algorithms are often elegant and incredibly useful tools used to accomplish tasks. They are mostly invisible aids, augmenting human lives in increasingly incredible ways. However, sometimes the application of algorithms created with good intentions leads to unintended consequences." Several Berkman Klein affiliates, including Judith Donath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jdonath) , Doc Searls (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dsearls) , David Weinberger (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dweinberger) , Justin Reich (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jreich) , and Baratunde Thurston (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bthurston) , contributed their insights about the benefits and challenges surrounding algorithms.

Giving Voice: Mobile Communication, Disability, and Inequality
This newly released book (https://mitpress.mit.edu/books/giving-voice) by Meryl Alper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/malper) explores "technology as a gateway to opportunity and voice as a metaphor for agency and self-representation." While technology is often thought of giving the voiceless a voice, Alper examines how privilege informs the ability to be heard. She will be speaking about her book at MIT on Feb 28 (http://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/event/46/?instance_id=11)
