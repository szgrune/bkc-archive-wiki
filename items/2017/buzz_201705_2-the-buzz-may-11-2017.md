https://www.buzzfeed.com/zeyneptufekci/dear-france-you-just-got-hacked-dont-make-the-same-mistakes?utm_term=.faOj6oqYl#.jeXrKna5X
Dear France: You just got hacked. Don’t make the same mistakes we did (https://www.buzzfeed.com/zeyneptufekci/dear-france-you-just-got-hacked-dont-make-the-same-mistakes?utm_term=.faOj6oqYl#.jeXrKna5X)
Less than two days before France's election this past week, President-elect Emmanuel Macron's campaign was hacked by the Kremlin. In this Buzzfeed article (https://www.buzzfeed.com/zeyneptufekci/dear-france-you-just-got-hacked-dont-make-the-same-mistakes?utm_term=.faOj6oqYl#.jeXrKna5X) , Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) offers pre-election advice to the media, based on the United States' reaction to the hacking of Clinton's campaign: "Before reporting on information from a hack, ask yourself this: Would you go to great lengths to find a way to hack or leak this information if it wasn’t just conveniently dumped in front of you? If not, it’s probably not newsworthy enough to report on."

https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/when-internet-memes-infiltrate-the-physical-world/523887/?utm_source=twb
When Internet memes infiltrate the physical world (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/when-internet-memes-infiltrate-the-physical-world/523887/?utm_source=twb)
The online world is also appearing offline, partially because of the use of memes in political protests and gatherings. In this Atlantic article (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/when-internet-memes-infiltrate-the-physical-world/523887/?utm_source=twb) , An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina) writes, "Today, in many global movement contexts, our meme lifecycles now include physical objects...Objects are worn or brought to events, and when people take pictures of them, they circulate back on the internet, thus continuing the meme’s lifecycle online and offline."

How to prepare for an automated future (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/05/03/upshot/how-to-prepare-for-an-automated-future.html?_r=1)
How can people be proactive about a future filled with artificial intelligence and automation? Judith Donath provided advice to workers in this New York Times article (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/05/03/upshot/how-to-prepare-for-an-automated-future.html?_r=1) : "Focus on learning how to do tasks that still need humans...[including] teaching and caregiving; building and repairing; and researching and evaluating."
