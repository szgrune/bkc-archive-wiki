A French court case against Google could threaten global speech rights.
In this opinion (https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/global-opinions/wp/2016/12/22/a-french-court-case-against-google-could-threaten-global-speech-rights/) for The Washington Post, Nani Jansen Reventlow (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/njansen) , Vivek Krishnamurthy (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkrishnamurthy) , and Chris Bavitz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/cbavitz) outline what's at stake as the French Council of State decides a case case between France's data protection authority and Google.

How do app stores challenge the global Internet governance ecosystem?
App stores challenge the culture of openness and resistance to central authorities cultivated by the pioneers of the Internet, writes Virgilio A. F. Almeida (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/valmeida) , Danilo Doneda, and Carolina Rossini in this paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05218v1) . Could multistakeholder governance bodies bring more inclusivity into the global cyberspace governance ecosystem?

Five ways Trump can make US trade system great — and transparent — again.
In this piece (http://thehill.com/blogs/pundits-blog/the-administration/312781-five-ways-trump-can-make-us-trade-system-great-and) for The Hill, John Stubbs (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jstubbs) offers five recommendations for modernizing trade policy. Among these changes should be making trade policy more transparent and inclusive for the people who elected Trump."

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias
The real name fallacy.
In this post (https://blog.coralproject.net/the-real-name-fallacy/) for the Coral Project blog, J. Nathan Matias (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias) dispels the notion that online behavior would improve if everyone were forced to use their real names. "Not only would removing anonymity fail to consistently improve online community behavior – forcing real names in online communities could also increase discrimination and worsen harassment," he writes.
------------------------------------------------------------

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rbudish
How to make transparency reports better.
"On the precipice of Donald Trump’s presidency, transparency reports about government law enforcement and surveillance requests are more important than ever before," writes Ryan Budish (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rbudish) in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/how-to-make-transparency-reports-better-9342b71ee1ff#.4d610al0m) announcing the publication of the Transparency Reporting Toolkit: Reporting Guide & Template (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2016/transparency_guide_and_template) , which aims to provide companies the tools they need to publish transparency reports that are more consistent, easier to understand, and more effective.
------------------------------------------------------------
Seven steps to building trusted data intermediaries.
Inthis post (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jshulman) on the Mellon Foundation website, James Shulman (https://mellon.org/resources/shared-experiences-blog/spanning-todays-chasms-seven-steps-building-trusted-data-intermediaries/) writes about the foundation of Artstor and the potential for philanthropy's role as a social venture capitalist. "In the many realms that market forces won’t support, however, a great deal of public good can be done by aggregating and managing access to datasets that might otherwise continue to live in isolation," he writes.
