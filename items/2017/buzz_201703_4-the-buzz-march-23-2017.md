Kurst Eichenwald is a journalist with epilepsy — a condition that a Twitter user exploited in an online attack. In this New York Times article (https://mobile.nytimes.com/2017/03/17/technology/social-media-attack-that-set-off-a-seizure-leads-to-an-arrest.html?smid=fb-nytimes&smtyp=cur&_r=0&referer=http://m.facebook.com) about the incident, Vivek Krishnamurthy (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkrishnamurthy) weighs in, stating that "This is an interesting ...case in that there are lots of online attacks that can have physical consequences...But this is distinguishable because it is a targeted physical attack that was personal.”

Fake news and fake solutions (https://advox.globalvoices.org/2017/03/20/fake-news-and-fake-solutions-how-do-we-build-a-civics-of-trust/)
In this post on Global Voices (https://advox.globalvoices.org/2017/03/20/fake-news-and-fake-solutions-how-do-we-build-a-civics-of-trust/) , Ivan Sigal (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/isigal)  addresses the ways in which we need to build a civic system of trust. Patent trolls beware: 40 firms join fight against blockchain IP abuse (http://www.coindesk.com/40-blockchain-firms-unite-in-fight-against-patent-trolls/)
This past week, 40 blockchain companies and organizations joined together to form the Blockchain Intellectual Property Council (http://www.coindesk.com/40-blockchain-firms-unite-in-fight-against-patent-trolls/) to help organizations maintain open source technologies while protecting their products. Patrick Murck (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pmurck) will be one of the chairs of the council, which will work with companies to build a defensive patent strategy.
Listen: Defining an ideology for the 21st century
http://radioopensource.org/welcome-neoliberal-world/

In this episode of Radio Open Source (http://radioopensource.org/welcome-neoliberal-world/) , Yarden Katz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz) weighs in on how neoliberalism affects scientific projects. He argues that "neoliberalism reorders the priority of...projects according to what's sellable and profitable in the short term, while steering science as a whole towards serving the technological interests of private stakeholders."

Watch: This is your brain on terrorism
http://www.vox.com/videos/2017/3/20/14982174/strikethrough-brain-terrorism-media-coverage-overreact

Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) appears in this Vox video (http://www.vox.com/videos/2017/3/20/14982174/strikethrough-brain-terrorism-media-coverage-overreact) about sensationalized terrorism coverage in the media. Schneier highlights how the news tends to focus on stories, instead of statistics, and how this affects psychological reactions to terrorism.
