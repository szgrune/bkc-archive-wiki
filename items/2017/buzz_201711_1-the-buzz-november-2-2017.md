Rather, Tufekci argues that at the present moment, "what we need to fear most is not what artificial intelligence will do to us on its own, but how the people in power will use artificial intelligence to control us and manipulate us in novel, sometimes hidden, subtle and unexpected ways."

Update on the 2018 Triennial 1201 Rule-Making (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/25/update-on-the-2018-triennial-1201-rule-making/#more-3643)
Evelyn Chang reports on current proceedings related to anti-circumvention clauses in the Digital Millennium Copyright Act in this recent blog post (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/25/update-on-the-2018-triennial-1201-rule-making/#more-3643) from the Cyberlaw Clinic (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/) . Anti-circumvention law prohibits using technology to get by "technology protection measures," or TPMs, put in place by copyright owners, to protect things like songs, movies, or software from being copied. However, Chang explains that there has been an unfortunate side effect in that, "the broad reach of 17 U.S.C. § 1201 also jeopardized many otherwise non-infringing and publicly-beneficial activities that may require circumventing TPMs."

Massachusetts Considers Digital Right to Repair (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/12/massachusetts-considers-digital-right-to-repair/)
Alex Noonan covers Massachusetts' two proposed "right to repair" bills in a new blog post (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/12/massachusetts-considers-digital-right-to-repair/)  from the Cyberlaw Clinic (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/) .
