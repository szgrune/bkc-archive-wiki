https://www.wired.com/2017/04/face-law-cant-keep-violence-off-facebook-either/
Want to stop Facebook violence? You won’t like the choices (https://www.wired.com/2017/04/face-law-cant-keep-violence-off-facebook-either/)
Through its live video streaming platform Facebook has become a host to many videos featuring violence. How can the platform prevent the sharing of illegal behaviors without enforcing censorship? In this Wired article (https://www.wired.com/2017/04/face-law-cant-keep-violence-off-facebook-either/) , Kate Coyer (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kcoyer) comments that "We want a free and open internet, and we want a space that we aren’t paying a subscription for. But we also don’t want to encounter some of the worst elements of humanity on there...At a certain point we may have to make a compromise."

http://lithub.com/squid-eggs-traffic-trees-and-more-object-lessons-for-earth-day/
Squid eggs, traffic, trees, and more: object lessons for Earth Day (http://lithub.com/squid-eggs-traffic-trees-and-more-object-lessons-for-earth-day/)
In this LitHub Earth-Day-themed series (http://lithub.com/squid-eggs-traffic-trees-and-more-object-lessons-for-earth-day/) , Matthew Battles (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mbattles) writes about thinking like a species. He notes that, "Belatedly, we’re discovering that the changes we humans make also churn and compose themselves on longer cycles than any individual human, any collective, any society can reasonably think."
http://www.ncuc.org/grace-mutingu-the-evolution-of-trademark-on-the-internet-is-it-sustainable/
Is the evolution of trademark on the Internet sustainable? (http://www.ncuc.org/grace-mutingu-the-evolution-of-trademark-on-the-internet-is-it-sustainable/)
In this post (http://www.ncuc.org/grace-mutingu-the-evolution-of-trademark-on-the-internet-is-it-sustainable/) , Grace Mutung’u (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/gmutungu) writes about her experience attending the Rights Protection Mechanisms working group at ICANN. She writes, "Listening to the proceedings of the RPM working group, there were many in defence of stronger protection for existing brands. Reflecting on these conversations as an African...I cannot help but wonder, do the Rights Protection Mechanisms envisage the rights of those yet to join the Internet?"
