Read: Recent news and commentary from around the community
How pink pussyhats and red MAGA caps went viral
"These objects couldn’t seem more different, and yet...they reflect new ways that people make objects that’s deeply entwined with the internet, enabling physical objects themselves to go viral in much the way that a digital one does," writes An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina) in this article (http://civichall.org/civicist/how-pink-pussyhats-and-red-maga-caps-went-viral/) for Civic Hall, which explores the digital virality of our political climate.
------------------------------------------------------------
Does the size of a protest matter?
In this New York Times (https://www.nytimes.com/2017/01/27/opinion/does-a-protests-size-matter.html?_r=1) article, Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) writes that "in the digital age, the size of a protest is no longer a reliable indicator of a movement’s strength." Instead, she argues that organizing work defines a movement.
------------------------------------------------------------
Stop saying "fake news." It's not helping.
In this post (http://www.ethanzuckerman.com/blog/2017/01/30/stop-saying-fake-news-its-not-helping/) on his blog, Ethan Zuckerman (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ezuckerman)  writes that the focus on fake news, reminds "people of the difficulty of reporting in a digital age, the real problems of verifying information, and the times our journalistic institutions have failed."
What's scarier: Trump's Twitter or his cybersecurity practices?
"Will a guy who won’t even secure his own phone ever be bothered to secure an entire country’s networks?," asks Josephine Wolff (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jwolff) in this article (http://www.slate.com/blogs/future_tense/2017/01/27/trump_s_phone_and_twitter_security_isn_t_as_scary_as_his_cybersecurity_policies.html) for Slate, which explores the public's growing interest in cybersecurity.

The brilliant strategy of Trump's travel ban
In this Medium post (https://medium.com/@judithd/the-brilliant-strategy-of-trumps-travel-order-dd581c203622#.9bs0vn3wp) , Judith Donath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jdonath) writes that the strategy behind Trump's executive order wasn't incompetent. She argues that it was "designed to incite the most anger in those opposing it: the sudden, without warning implementation, the inclusion of green-card holders, its enactment on Holocaust Remembrance Day."
------------------------------------------------------------
Can fact-checking stop the spread of unreliable news?
"I am hopeful that AI nudges, especially when led by communities themselves, offer an exciting direction for the public to manage the role of algorithms in society, while also preserving individual liberty," writes J. Nathan Matias (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias)  in this post (https://medium.com/@natematias/persuading-algorithms-with-an-ai-nudge-25c92293df1d#.kwxs1vrxo) for the MIT Media Lab, which discusses a study and the effects of algorithms on news perception.

How is bitcoin regulated? What are the laws that apply to digital currencies? In this episode of the FutureTech (http://www.futuretechpodcast.com/podcasts/bitcoin-cryptocurrency-fin-tech-lawyer-patrick-murck/) podcast, Berkman Klein fellow Patrick Murck (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pmurck) discusses the answers to these questions, as well as how business owners may be affected by legal implications.
