Racial Projects in Black Box Societies (https://medium.com/digital-sociology-at-vcu/racial-projects-in-black-box-societies-63fdfc0e3fcf)
In this Medium piece (https://medium.com/digital-sociology-at-vcu/racial-projects-in-black-box-societies-63fdfc0e3fcf) , Tressie McMillan Cottom (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tcottom)  shares work from her graduate seminar in Digital Sociology at Virginia Commonwealth University. Drawing on Frank Pasquale's theoretical framework, she has created an infographic that shows how black boxes (or secret algorithms) organize racial projects in digital societies.
https://newsframes.globalvoices.org/2017/10/10/lone-wolves-are-terrorists-too/
Lone Wolves are ‘Terrorists’ Too (https://newsframes.globalvoices.org/2017/10/10/lone-wolves-are-terrorists-too/)
Using data from Media Cloud (https://mediacloud.org/) , Connie Moon Sehat (https://globalvoices.org/author/connie-moon-sehat/) of the Global Voices (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/globalvoicesonline) project questions the assumption that white mass shooters are always labeled "lone wolves," while Muslims are "terrorists." In fact, Muslim perpetrators are also often referred to as lone wolves in mass media, too. She writes, "the larger complication is how we associate mass shooting and terrorism."
http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/future_tense/2017/10/hacking_back_the_worst_idea_in_cybersecurity_rises_again.html
Attack of the Hack Back (http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/future_tense/2017/10/hacking_back_the_worst_idea_in_cybersecurity_rises_again.html)
In this Slate article (http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/future_tense/2017/10/hacking_back_the_worst_idea_in_cybersecurity_rises_again.html) , Josephine Wolff (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jwolff)  argues against the newly revised version of the Active Cyber Defense Certainty Act, suggesting that, "If there were a prize for the worst cybersecurity policy idea that just won’t die, it would have to go to “hacking back,” or making it legal for people to attack the computers that are attacking them."
https://www.swisscom.ch/en/business/enterprise/themen/banking/ico-recht-schweiz.html
ICOs are Permitted in Switzerland, Provided They Comply With the Legal System (https://www.swisscom.ch/en/business/enterprise/themen/banking/ico-recht-schweiz.html)

In this interview with  (https://www.swisscom.ch/en/business/enterprise/themen/banking/ico-recht-schweiz.html) SwissCom (https://www.swisscom.ch/en/business/enterprise/themen/banking/ico-recht-schweiz.html) , Nathan Kaiser (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/nkaiser)  provides insight into Switzerland's use of ICOs, or Initial Coin Offerings. ICOs have typically been used to raise capital for new business ventures via cryptocurrencies, but Kaiser explains that now "many new tokens are designed as tools or utility tokens, the monetary value of which is purely a background function."
Toward a Philosophy of Blockchain: A Symposium: Introduction (http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/meta.12270/full)
In this Metaphilosophy piece (http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/meta.12270/full) , Melanie Swan and Primavera di Filippi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pdefilippi) introduce philosophical scholarship on blockchain technology. "The philosophical themes discussed include mathematical models of reality, signification, and the sociopolitical institutions that structure human life and interaction," as well as "the metaphilosophical theme of how to create a philosophy of anything, specifically a new topic such as blockchain technology."
