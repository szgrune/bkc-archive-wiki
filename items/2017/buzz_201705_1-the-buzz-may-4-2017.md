** Next Gen Podcast Distribution Protocols (https://cyber.harvard.edu/events/2017/05/Podcast)
------------------------------------------------------------

**
Thursday, May 11
8:30am - 5pm
------------------------------------------------------------
The Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society and Tow Center for Digital Journalism will host and facilitate a symposium, in collaboration with the syndicated.media open working group, to address the process of developing standards that support the distribution of syndicated audio content. The event will look back at the evolution of the RSS protocol and look forward at the need for new technical infrastructure to support an expanding podcast distribution landscape.  Participants will have the opportunity to engage in both higher-level policy discussions and technical deep-dives throughout the course of this one-day event.

http://www.apnorc.org/projects/Pages/American-Teens-are-Taking-Breaks-from-Social-Media;-Many-Step-Back-Deliberately,-but-Half-of-Breaks-are-Involuntary.aspx
American teens are taking breaks from social media (http://www.apnorc.org/projects/Pages/American-Teens-are-Taking-Breaks-from-Social-Media;-Many-Step-Back-Deliberately,-but-Half-of-Breaks-are-Involuntary.aspx)
Generation Z is often thought of as the Internet generation. New AP-NORC research (http://www.apnorc.org/projects/Pages/American-Teens-are-Taking-Breaks-from-Social-Media;-Many-Step-Back-Deliberately,-but-Half-of-Breaks-are-Involuntary.aspx) shows, however, that American teens are stepping away from their smartphones and laptops to spend time away from social media sites. Amanda Lenhart (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/alenhart) helped spearhead this study, which demonstrates that 60 percent of teens who take breaks from social media have taken three or more.

Anatomy of a museum Twitter bot (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/anatomy-of-a-museum-twitter-bot-2311d81de243)
L. Kelly Fitzpatrick (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lfitzpatrick) examines the makings of a museum Twitter bot in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/anatomy-of-a-museum-twitter-bot-2311d81de243) . After examining eight different accounts, Fitzpatrick writes, "By stripping away infrastructural supports in exchange for randomized automation, Twitter bots remix the discovery of museum digital collections content."

Combating fake news: An agenda for research and action (https://shorensteincenter.org/combating-fake-news-agenda-for-research/)
A recent conference, sponsored by the Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics, and Public Policy and Ash Center for Democratic Governance, gathered a variety of academics to explore solutions to fake news. Read the final report (https://shorensteincenter.org/combating-fake-news-agenda-for-research/) to learn more about the conference, which included presentations by Yochai Benkler, Cass Sunstein, and more.
https://globalvoices.org/2017/05/02/fighting-fire-with-fire-african-regional-body-proposes-high-costs-for-internet-shutdowns/
Fighting fire with fire: African regional body proposes high costs for Internet shutdown (https://globalvoices.org/2017/05/02/fighting-fire-with-fire-african-regional-body-proposes-high-costs-for-internet-shutdowns/)
How should Internet providers react when governments threaten to shut down the Internet as a form of censorship? The African Network Information Centre (https://www.afrinic.net/) has one possible solution: shut down the Internet for the governments, and revoke access to IP addresses. Grace Mutung'u (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/gmutungu) writes about the effects of this proposal for Global Voices (https://globalvoices.org/2017/05/02/fighting-fire-with-fire-african-regional-body-proposes-high-costs-for-internet-shutdowns/) , and explains how it could affect Internet access in the continent.

https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/american-discourse-version-12/523875/
The age of misinformation (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/american-discourse-version-12/523875/)
In this Atlantic article (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2017/05/american-discourse-version-12/523875/) , Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) writes about the relationship between online aggregation and news consumption, noting that "Facebook and Twitter for social media, and Google and Microsoft for search, must recognize a special responsibility for the parts of their services that host or inform public discourse. They should be upfront about how they promote some stories and de-emphasize others, instead of treating their ranking systems as trade secrets."
