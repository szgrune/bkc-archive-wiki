The Slippery Slope of Internet Censorship in Egypt (https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/the-slippery-slope-of-internet-censorship-in-egypt)
A new research bulletin (https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/the-slippery-slope-of-internet-censorship-in-egypt) from Internet Monitor (https://thenetmonitor.org/)  describes how internet users in Egypt use social media, Google Drive, and other merging techniques to get around government censorship.  According to the bulletin, "Information control in the social media age can be challenging, even for governments like Egypt that pervasively filter political content. Users take to social media not only to discuss and protest filtering but also to disseminate and publicize alternative ways to access blocked content."
https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/developing-a-surveillance-ordinance-in-cambridge-3151eb9b62b2
Developing a surveillance ordinance in Cambridge (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/developing-a-surveillance-ordinance-in-cambridge-3151eb9b62b2)
In this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/developing-a-surveillance-ordinance-in-cambridge-3151eb9b62b2) , Berkman Klein Fellow Ben Green (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bgreen)  reports on a recent meeting about technological surveillance in the city of Cambridge. The meeting focused on questions such as, "who gets to control whether and how new technology is used for surveillance? Will police be permitted to acquire new surveillance technologies without notifying anyone?"
http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/12/massachusetts-considers-digital-right-to-repair/
Massachusetts Considers Digital Right to Repair (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/12/massachusetts-considers-digital-right-to-repair/)
Alex Noonan covers Massachusetts' two proposed "right to repair" bills in a new blog post (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2017/10/12/massachusetts-considers-digital-right-to-repair/) from the Cyberlaw Clinic (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/) . The bills "would require manufacturers of digital devices to provide diagnostic, repair, and service information to independent technicians and owners of devices, information that is currently only available to technicians selected and authorized by the manufacturers," he writes.
https://medium.com/ai-and-christianity/artificial-intelligence-in-christian-thought-and-practice-20ec8635a94f
Artificial Intelligence in Christian Thought and Practice (https://medium.com/ai-and-christianity/artificial-intelligence-in-christian-thought-and-practice-20ec8635a94f)
J. Nathan Matias (http://natematias.com/)  shares the first of in a series of eight articles aimed to introduce the Christian community to AI in this co-authored Medium piece (https://medium.com/ai-and-christianity/artificial-intelligence-in-christian-thought-and-practice-20ec8635a94f) . The series will be an "overview of issues raised by AI in the hope that Christians will be inspired to find new ways to follow Christ faithfully and serve the common good with artificial intelligence," they write.
