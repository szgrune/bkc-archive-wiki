Kenya, Past and Present: How the Internet Shapes a Country
Internet Monitor (https://thenetmonitor.org/)  summer intern Jeanette Si interviews Grace Mutung'u (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/gmutungu) about how the internet is shaping Kenyan society in this Medium piece. (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/kenya-past-and-present-how-the-internet-shapes-a-country-956effe68e9d) Mutung'u and Si discuss censorship, network disruption, and the challenges internet users continue to face in East Africa.

Getting Chief Executives to Take Cyber Risks Seriously
Do chief executives need to improve their technical knowledge to mitigate cyber attacks in the future? IT departments are important, but intelligent cyber security strategies need to involve CEOs and board members, writes Amy Aixi Zhang (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zhang)  in this post (https://www.cfr.org/blog-post/getting-chief-executives-take-cyber-risks-seriously-0) for the Council on Foreign Relations.
https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/07/biomedicalelite
On the Biomedical Elite: Inequality and Stasis in Scientific Knowledge Production
This new report (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/07/biomedicalelite) from authors Yarden Katz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ykatz) and Ulrich Matter examines the relationship between commonly used metrics and funding levels for investigators funded by NIH and finds that inequality has been rising since 1985.
------------------------------------------------------------
https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/43dx3j/twitter-and-tear-gas-review
Twitter and Tear Gas
Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) reviews Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep) 's new book "Twitter and Tear Gas, (https://www.twitterandteargas.org/) " which looks at how the internet enables and challenges protest movements around the world. "Tufekci isn't arguing that modern protests are necessarily less effective, but that they're different," writes Schneier.

Wikipedia Against Censorship
Justin Clark (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jclark)  speaks to Harvard Magazine (http://harvardmagazine.com/2017/06/wikipedia) about the recent Internet Monitor report: Analyzing Accessibility of Wikipedia Projects Around the World (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/04/WikipediaCensorship)  and explains key findings including the reasons behind changing levels of Wikipedia censorship.
Watch: Expanding Access to Medicines and Promoting Innovation: A Practical Approach

On June 26, Global Access in Action (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/globalaccessinaction) (GAiA) hosted its second "Conversation on Global Health, Innovation, and the Digital World" brownbag. GAiA Co-director Quentin Palfrey presented on practical strategies to expand global access to medicines and promote research and development for diseases that primarily affect the world’s most under-resourced populations.
