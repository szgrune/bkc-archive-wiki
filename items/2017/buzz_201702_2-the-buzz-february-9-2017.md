The "fake news" problem is highlighting even bigger issues.
"Labelling content as fake news may help some to navigate the ecosystem of news, but it represents a shallow response to much larger underlying problems. Legislating against fake news may make its controversy disappear for a moment, but has a potentially chilling effect on freedom of expression," writes Nani Jensen Reventlow (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/njansen)  in this article (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/fake-news-highlights-much-bigger-problems-at-play-9e419e4a6f52#.4s2tjmpqb) , which considers issues surrounding the news ecosystem.

How the internet is under threat.
"When President Obama nominated Tom Wheeler as the 31st chairman of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), some activists were wary because of his background as an advocate for cable and wireless interests. But as a friend of his, I was confident that he would be a strong leader, and he did not disappoint me," writes Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) on BackChannel in a post (https://backchannel.com/obamas-fcc-head-is-worried-about-our-online-future-628b8f63efc5#.4jc6g2wtc) that includes a transcript of her discussion with him at Harvard Law School last month. (Video of that talk and accompanying article here (https://today.law.harvard.edu/u-s-communications-crossroads-former-fcc-chair-discusses-road-ahead/) .)

Concord builds a fiber network.
This new case study (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/MF/Concord) by David Talbot (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/98856) , Waide Warner (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/wwarner) , Susan Crawford (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scrawford) , and Jacob White examines the creation of a $3.9 million smart grid, which included a 100-mile fiber-optic network. Read the case study to learn more about how the town is helping its residents curb costs, while increasing revenues.

https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/MF/Chattanooga
Smart grid payback: the Chattanooga example.
This companion case study (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2017/MF/Chattanooga) by David Talbot (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/98856) and Maria Paz-Canales (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mloebel) examines paybacks on Chattanooga's smart grid, which is the first in the United States to offer Internet access speeds of 1 gigabit per second. Learn more about how the city is saving money by offering fiber-to-the-home access.
