African Union Bugged by China: Cyber Espionage as Evidence of Strategic Shifts (https://www.cfr.org/blog/african-union-bugged-china-cyber-espionage-evidence-strategic-shifts)
"A number of African leaders have turned to Chinese investment as a viable alternative to Western development aid;" however, "recent allegations of Chinese cyberespionage of the African Union's headquarters might prompt them to reconsider," explains BKC Fellow, Mailyn Fidler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mfidler) . Fidler analyzes the complex implications of the African Union's reliance on China for technological investments, in this blog post from the Council on Foreign Relations (https://www.cfr.org/blog/african-union-bugged-china-cyber-espionage-evidence-strategic-shifts) .

http://www.niemanlab.org/2018/02/news-in-a-disintegrating-reality-tows-jonathan-albright-on-what-to-do-as-things-crash-around-us/

News in a Disintegrating Reality: Jonathan Albright on What to do as Things Crash Around Us (http://www.niemanlab.org/2018/02/news-in-a-disintegrating-reality-tows-jonathan-albright-on-what-to-do-as-things-crash-around-us/)
"It’s a huge problem. It’s the biggest problem ever, in my opinion, especially for American culture," says BKC faculty associate, Jonathan Albright, regarding fake news, in this interview (http://www.niemanlab.org/2018/02/news-in-a-disintegrating-reality-tows-jonathan-albright-on-what-to-do-as-things-crash-around-us/)  with the Nieman Lab. Albright suggests that studying the content of fake news is not enough; researchers also need to focus on tracking, microtargeting, and profiling, to understand how people are finding this information.

Discovering the Social (http://www.brianckeegan.com/2018/03/discovering-the-social/)
Despite publicly recognizing the social harms its platform enables, Twitter's absence from "research communities examining the intersection of social behavior and technological affordances has been glaringly conspicuous," writes BKC Alum, Brian Keegan (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bkeegan) . Keegan argues that researchers like himself have been exploring these issues for years and have developed potential solutions to address them. However, the solutions "would require fundamental cultural shifts" in Twitter's engagement with researchers, he writes in this blog post (http://www.brianckeegan.com/2018/03/discovering-the-social/) .
