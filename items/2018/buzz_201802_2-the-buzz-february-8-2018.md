Learning the Politics of “Digital Dissensus” (https://civichall.org/civicist/learning-the-politics-of-digital-dissensus/)
"What Trump shows is that in a landscape of widespread dissensus, political leaders can thrive by being the center of attention—even if they are also unpopular," writes BKC fellow An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina) . In this article (https://civichall.org/civicist/learning-the-politics-of-digital-dissensus/) from Civic Hall, Mina historicizes presidents' use of media technologies and analyzes Trump's current discourses.

https://jods.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/issue3-palfrey

Line-Drawing Exercises: Autonomy and Automation (https://jods.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/issue3-palfrey)
"In pursuing automation with such abandon, we threaten our own autonomy as humans. It feels urgent that we examine what we care most about in humanity as we race to develop the science and technology of automation," states BKC codirector John Palfrey (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jpalfrey) . Palfrey summarizes existential fears about AI in this article (https://jods.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/issue3-palfrey) for the MIT Media Lab's Journal of Design and Science.

http://journals.openedition.org/factsreports/4518

The Expansion of Algorithmic Governance: From Code is Law to Law is Code (http://journals.openedition.org/factsreports/4518)
Samer Hassan (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/shassan) and Primavera De Filippi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pdefilippi)  describe how technologies like Blockchain and Machine Learning are transforming our understanding of regulations, in this article (http://journals.openedition.org/factsreports/4518) . "Relying on technological tools and code-based rules as a means to regulate society brings about a variety benefits, mostly related to the ability to automate the law...Yet, regulation by code also comes with important drawbacks that might ultimately disrupt some of the basic tenets of law," they explain.

Saying Goodbye to an Internet Pioneer
We mourn the loss of John Perry Barlow, a visionary since the early days of the Internet, and one of our first fellows, who passed away this week. His writing on intellectual rights in the digital age, "The (https://www.wired.com/1994/03/economy-ideas/) https://www.wired.com/1994/03/economy-ideas/Economy of Ideas (https://www.wired.com/1994/03/economy-ideas/) ," (1994) is still required reading for scholars of the Internet. In a video (https://youtu.be/nu3efDQwULc)  interview reflecting on the first decade of the Internet, he remarked on the power of ideas: “When you spread information, you create demand for it.” In their eulogy (https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2018/02/john-perry-barlow-internet-pioneer-1947-2018) , the Electronic Frontier Foundation excerpts from his essential "Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace (https://www.eff.org/cyberspace-independence) " in which he shares his vision of the Internet as “a world that all may enter without privilege or prejudice
accorded by race, economic power, military force, or station of birth... a world where anyone, anywhere may express his or her beliefs, no matter how singular, without fear of being coerced into silence or conformity.”
