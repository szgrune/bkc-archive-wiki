Artificial Intelligence and the Need for Data Fairness in the Global South (https://medium.com/digitalhks/data-fairness-for-the-global-south-d383b6159b86)
In this Medium post (https://medium.com/digitalhks/data-fairness-for-the-global-south-d383b6159b86) , BKC affiliate Yasodara Córdova (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ycordova)  discusses how countries in the Global South are often not granted access to their own data. Calling for better negotiation policies, she states, "Agreements on the extraction of data must take into account economic and social benefits for the countries where the data is being extracted."

On the Web, Privacy in Peril (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2018/03/facebooks-privacy-problem-may-erode-web-trust-harvard-analyst-says/)
In this interview (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2018/03/facebooks-privacy-problem-may-erode-web-trust-harvard-analyst-says/) with the Harvard Gazette, Vivek Krishnamurthy (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkrishnamurthy)  discusses Facebook, Cambridge Analytica and U.S. privacy protections. "The system was structured to allow, at the time, all of this information to leak out. It was an invitation to come in and take this information. So the original sin in this story is the fact that [a developer] was able to export this much data pertaining to 50 million people."

https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/19/opinion/facebook-cambridge-analytica.html

Facebook’s Surveillance Machine (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/19/opinion/facebook-cambridge-analytica.html?mtrref=tagteam.harvard.edu&assetType=opinion)
In this article (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/19/opinion/facebook-cambridge-analytica.html?mtrref=tagteam.harvard.edu&assetType=opinion) from the New York Times, BKC faculty associate Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep)  describes the recent Cambridge Analytica incident as "an all-too-natural consequence of Facebook’s business model, which involves having people go to the site for social interaction, only to be quietly subjected to an enormous level of surveillance."

https://slate.com/technology/2018/03/cambridge-analytica-and-the-long-history-of-computer-science-and-psychology.html?wpsrc=sh_all_dt_tw_ru

The Long History of Computer Science and Psychology Comes Into View (https://slate.com/technology/2018/03/cambridge-analytica-and-the-long-history-of-computer-science-and-psychology.html)
In this Slate article (https://slate.com/technology/2018/03/cambridge-analytica-and-the-long-history-of-computer-science-and-psychology.html?wpsrc=sh_all_dt_tw_ru) , BKC fellow Luke Stark (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lstark)  argues that, "the integration of digital media devices and psychological techniques is one of the most underappreciated developments in the history of computing." Understanding that legacy, he argues, can "help us stop the next Cambridge Analytica."

https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=9436

How Not to Regulate Social Networks (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=9436)
BKC affiliate Nikolas Guggenberger (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/nguggenberger)  comments on the Network Enforcement Act, designed to combat hate speech on the internet, in this guest commentary (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=9436) for the University of Muenster News. Guggenberger suggests that the act has not been successful, arguing that it "is not fit for purpose, sets dangerous disincentives – to the detriment of freedom of opinion – and is poorly thought out from a legal point of view."

https://www.coindesk.com/no-blockchain-island/

No Blockchain is an Island (https://www.coindesk.com/no-blockchain-island/)
"What are the various elements or forces that influence the governance of blockchain-based networks or applications?" asks BKC faculty associate Primavera de Filippi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pdefilippi) , in this post (https://www.coindesk.com/no-blockchain-island/) for Coindesk. Following the work of Lawrence Lessig, de Filippi argues that we must analyze blockchain technologies through an ecosystemic approach, rather than focusing on individual behaviors.

In this Oct 2016 piece (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2016/10/information-fiduciary/502346/) for The Atlantic, BKC faculty chair Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) and Jack M. Balkin (https://law.yale.edu/jack-m-balkin) introduced the idea of "information fiduciaries" as a way to protect individual privacy rights.

"The information age has created new kinds of entities that have many of the trappings of fiduciaries—huge online businesses, like Facebook, Google, and Uber, that collect, analyze, and use our personal information—sometimes in our interests and sometimes not," they write. "T]he important question is whether these businesses, like older fiduciaries, have legal obligations to be trustworthy. The answer is that they should.
