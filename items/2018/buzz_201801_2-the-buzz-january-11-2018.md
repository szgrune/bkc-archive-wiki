Community-Owned Fiber Networks: Value Leaders in America (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2018/01/communityfiber)

In this new pricing study (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publications/2018/01/communityfiber) from the Responsive Communities (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/responsivecommunities) project, David Talbot (https://cyber.harvard.edu/node/98856) , Kira Hessekiel (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/khessekiel) , and Danielle Kehl find that community-owned fiber networks generally charge less for entry-level broadband service than do competing private providers.

https://today.law.harvard.edu/evisort/

HLS Students Harness Artificial Intelligence to Revolutionize how Lawyers Draft and Manage Contracts (https://today.law.harvard.edu/evisort/)

Harvard Law Today (https://today.law.harvard.edu/evisort/) reports on four law students' work in creating the new search engine, Evisort (http://evisort.com/) . Mentor to the project, Cyberlaw Clinic (https://cyber.harvard.edu/teaching/cyberlawclinic)  director Chris Bavitz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/cbavitz) , states that, "By tapping into the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning to review agreements, Evisort has real potential to lower costs without sacrificing accuracy and precision."

https://medium.com/@schock.one/notes-on-designjustice-and-digital-technologies-3cfc75cdc95

Notes on #DesignJustice and Digital Technologies (https://medium.com/@schock.one/notes-on-designjustice-and-digital-technologies-3cfc75cdc95)

In this Medium piece (https://medium.com/@schock.one/notes-on-designjustice-and-digital-technologies-3cfc75cdc95) , BKC faculty associate Sasha Costanza-Chock (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scostanzachock)  describes the concept of design justice and offers questions about what the future of the movement will bring. Digital technology designers "have an ethical imperative to systematically advance democratic participation in all stages of the digital technology design process, and especially to center historically marginalized communities in this process," Costanza-Chock writes.

https://medium.com/@nanijansenreventlow/24-hours-of-privilege-c5e0af7a4cb6

24 Hours of Privilege (https://medium.com/@nanijansenreventlow/24-hours-of-privilege-c5e0af7a4cb6)

In this Medium piece (https://medium.com/@nanijansenreventlow/24-hours-of-privilege-c5e0af7a4cb6) , BKC affiliate Nani Jansen Reventlow (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/njansen)  imagines a life filled with the privilege of a white, middle-aged man. Reventlow states that her piece, "seeks to convey what daily life isn’t like for many of us. We should speak up about that more. All of us. And more often."

https://www.cnn.com/2018/01/04/opinions/security-of-nearly-every-computer-has-just-gotten-a-lot-worse-opinion-schneier/index.html

The Security of Pretty Much Every Computer on the Planet has Just Gotten a Lot Worse (https://www.cnn.com/2018/01/04/opinions/security-of-nearly-every-computer-has-just-gotten-a-lot-worse-opinion-schneier/index.html)

BKC fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  reports on newly identified vulnerabilities in cybersecurity in this CNN article (https://www.cnn.com/2018/01/04/opinions/security-of-nearly-every-computer-has-just-gotten-a-lot-worse-opinion-schneier/index.html) . With regard to security threats, Schneier claims that, "more are coming, and they'll be worse. 2018 will be the year of microprocessor vulnerabilities, and it's going to be a wild ride."
