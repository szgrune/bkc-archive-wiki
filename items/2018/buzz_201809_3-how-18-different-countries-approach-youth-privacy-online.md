Just Say No to ‘Just Say No’ (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/just-say-no-to-just-say-no-7726d5cea63c)
Berkman Klein Affiliate Mariel García-Montes (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mgarciamontes) publishes main findings and takeaways from her graduate thesis on approaches to youth privacy online in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/just-say-no-to-just-say-no-7726d5cea63c) . García-Montes examines the practices of 18 organizations in the field of youth privacy advocacy in the Americas and shares insights into the tensions between protection and censorship, agency and surveillance.

How to Exercise the Power You Didn't Ask For (https://hbr.org/2018/09/how-to-exercise-the-power-you-didnt-ask-for)
"The problems arising from a surveillance-heavy digital ecosystem are getting more difficult and more ingrained," says Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) in this piece for the Harvard Business Review (https://hbr.org/2018/09/how-to-exercise-the-power-you-didnt-ask-for) . Internet companies need "information fiduciaries" to ensure that the consumer's best interest is kept at heart.

Playing with AI Fairness (https://pair-code.github.io/what-if-tool/ai-fairness.html)
David Weinberger (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dweinberger) , a member of the Berkman Klein Fellows Advisory Board, teases out multiple contrasting perspectives (https://pair-code.github.io/what-if-tool/ai-fairness.html) of what fairness means. “Which sense of fairness is the fairest in the land? There is no one right answer, but we probably can agree that humans, not computers, are the ones who should answer this question,” he says.

https://today.law.harvard.edu/the-politics-of-facebook-and-what-to-do-about-it/

The politics of Facebook and what to do about it (https://today.law.harvard.edu/the-politics-of-facebook-and-what-to-do-about-it/)
"The problem with Facebook is Facebook,” declares Si (https://mediastudies.virginia.edu/people/sv2r) va Vaidhyanathan (https://mediastudies.virginia.edu/people/sv2r)  in his recent talk as part of the Berkman Klein Center's luncheon series (http://cyber.harvard.edu/events)  on the role Facebook has played in public discourse and democracy. According to Vaidhyanathan, Facebook is not just a social network, but a political tool and a major player in the media ecosystem. "What sort of media ecosystem do we need, do we deserve, to be able to operate responsibly as citizens in a democratic republic? We have not even asked that first question, but we really need to; it’s rather urgent."
