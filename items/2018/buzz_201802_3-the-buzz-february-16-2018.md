Risk Assessment Tools and Criminal Justice (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/a-letter-to-the-members-of-the-criminal-justice-reform-committee-of-conference-of-the-massachusetts-2911d65969df)
In this letter  (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/a-letter-to-the-members-of-the-criminal-justice-reform-committee-of-conference-of-the-massachusetts-2911d65969df) — signed by Harvard and MIT-based faculty, staff, and researchers including BKC's Chris Bavitz (http://hls.harvard.edu/faculty/directory/10055/Bavitz) , Ryan Budish (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rbudish) , Urs Gasser (http://hls.harvard.edu/faculty/directory/10298/Gasser) , Kira Hessekiel (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/khessekiel) , Mason Kortz (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mkortz) , and Jonathan Zittrain  (http://hls.harvard.edu/faculty/directory/10992/Zittrain)  — is directed to the Massachusetts Legislature to inform its consideration of risk assessment tools as part of ongoing criminal justice reform efforts in the Commonwealth.

"In light of the extraordinarily rapid pace of technical development with respect to the sorts of RA tools under consideration; the relatively nascent state of our understanding of such tools and the consequences of their implementation; the far-ranging impacts these tools can have once implemented; the risk that institutional inertia might make it difficult to move away from them once they are adopted; and the complex and multivariate interplay between the use of RA tools and other aspects of the criminal justice system, we submit that the appropriate approach here is not a mandate in favor of adoption. Rather, we believe that the time is ripe for study, reflection, and development of transparent processes and comprehensive best practices."

John Perry Barlow and the Foundational Values of the Net (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/john-perry-barlow-and-the-foundational-values-of-the-net-c27cf5385478)
Charles Nesson shares his thoughts (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/john-perry-barlow-and-the-foundational-values-of-the-net-c27cf5385478) on how John Perry Barlow (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jbarlow) (1947-2018) helped build the idea of what the Internet could be. "More than his contemporaries, he saw the dimensional change that we were going through with Internet connectivity. The change from the pre-net world to what he could see as the cyber-world. He saw that as somehow deeper, more more encompassing than others. And in doing that he offered a vision of a future that people could connect with."

“Fake News” is a Feeling (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/fake-news-is-a-feeling-f339979d0687)
BKC fellow Luke Stark (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lstark)  reports on Facebook's new plan to combat misinformation and fake news, but questions its utility. "Until the platform and others like it recognize 'fake news' is as much about the feelings of their users as it is about news — and that they profit from emotionally driven misinformation and propaganda — our “fake news” problem is only going to get worse" Stark writes in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/fake-news-is-a-feeling-f339979d0687) .

https://www.nytimes.com/2018/02/12/business/computer-science-ethics-courses.html

Tech’s Ethical ‘Dark Side’: Harvard, Stanford and Others Want to Address It (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/02/12/business/computer-science-ethics-courses.html)
"As we start to see things, like autonomous vehicles, that clearly have the ability to save people but also cause harm, I think that people are scrambling to build a system of ethics," states MIT Media Lab director Joi Ito (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jito)  in this article (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/02/12/business/computer-science-ethics-courses.html?hp&action=click&pgtype=Homepage&clickSource=story-heading&module=first-column-region&region=top-news&WT.nav=top-news) about incorporating ethics in AI curricula from the New York Times. The MIT-Harvard course, "The Ethics and Governance of Artificial Intelligence (https://www.media.mit.edu/courses/the-ethics-and-governance-of-artificial-intelligence/) ", co-taught by Ito and BKC's Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) , is among the first of its kind.

Sandra Cortesi über das Potenzial der digitalen Welt (https://www.migrosmagazin.ch/sandra-cortesi-ueber-das-potenzial-der-digitalen-welt)
BKC fellows and Youth and Media (http://youthandmedia.org/) director Sandra Cortesi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/scortesi)  shares insights into her work in this interview with (https://www.migrosmagazin.ch/sandra-cortesi-ueber-das-potenzial-der-digitalen-welt) Migros Magazin (https://www.migrosmagazin.ch/sandra-cortesi-ueber-das-potenzial-der-digitalen-welt) . In this USA Today (https://www.usatoday.com/story/tech/2018/02/15/encryption-we-cant-live-without-law-enforcement-cant-live/336101002/) article, BKC senior researcher David O'Brien (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dobrien)  weighs in on the US's role as a global leader in setting encryption policy. The report provides nine normative recommendations on encryption policy to ensure strong cybersecurity while enabling lawful law enforcement access to the plaintext of encrypted information in limited circumstances.

Spotlight on the Ethics and Governance of AI
https://youtu.be/shlTn7swxNI

Edmond Awad (http://web.media.mit.edu/~awad/) , a postdoctoral associate at MIT Media Lab at the Scalable Cooperation group helped develop a tool to get a grasp on what constitutes the basic ethical sense of humanity. Put in a life or death situation, what should a self-driving car do? Moralmachine.mit.edu (http://moralmachine.mit.edu/) has collected millions of profiles in ethical judgment. Awad describes how this may be able to help program more reliably good decision-making for all kinds of artificial intelligence.
