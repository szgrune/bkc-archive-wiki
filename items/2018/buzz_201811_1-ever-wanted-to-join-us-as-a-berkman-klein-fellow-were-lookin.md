We invite applications from people working on a broad range of opportunities and challenges related to Internet and society, which may overlap with ongoing work at the Berkman Klein Center and may expose our community to new opportunities and approaches.

We encourage applications from scholars, practitioners, innovators, engineers, artists, and others committed to understanding and advancing the public interest who come from — and have interest in — countries industrialized or developing, with ideas, projects, or activities in all phases on a spectrum from incubation to reflection.

Weekend Reads: Research, commentary, and tools from our community
https://metalabharvard.github.io/projects/laughingroom/

The Laughing Room November 16–18, 2018 (https://metalabharvard.github.io/projects/laughingroom/)
THE LAUGHING ROOM, supported by metaLAB@Harvard (https://metalabharvard.github.io/projects/laughingroom/) , is an interactive art installation in which participants enter an artificially intelligent room, designed like a sitcom set, that plays a laugh track whenever the participants say something that the room’s algorithm deems to be worth laughing with. The piece is on display at the Main Branch of the Cambridge Public Library Nov 16-18.

https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2018/10/26/a-victory-for-software-preservation-dmca-exemption-granted-for-spn/

A Victory for Software Preservation: DMCA Exemption Granted for SPN (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2018/10/26/a-victory-for-software-preservation-dmca-exemption-granted-for-spn/)
The HLS Cyberlaw Clinic (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/) , representing the Software Preservation Network, received an exemption (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2018/10/26/a-victory-for-software-preservation-dmca-exemption-granted-for-spn/)  to the Digital Millennium Copyright Act anti-circumvention provisions to allow libraries, archivists, museums, and other cultural heritage institutions to preserve software and software-dependent materials. Motherboard (https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/zm9az5/copyright-law-just-got-better-for-video-game-history)  also interviewed Kendra Albert (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kalbert) , a clinical instructional fellow at the Cyberlaw Clinic, about how this ruling benefits video game history.

https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.10731

Law and Adversarial Machine Learning (https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.10731)
A new paper (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.10731.pdf)  from BKC affiliate Ram Shankar Siva Kumar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ram-shankar-siva-kumar) , fellow Salomé Viljoen (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sviljoen) , senior researcher David O’Brien (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dobrien)  and clinical instructional fellow Kendra Albert (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kalbert)  explores how some aspects of computer crime, copyright, and tort law interface with perturbation, poisoning, model stealing and model inversion attacks to show how some attacks are more likely to result in liability than others.

https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2018/10/29/caselaw-access-project-cap-launches-api-and-bulk-data-service/

Caselaw Access Project Launches API and Bulk Data Service (https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2018/10/29/caselaw-access-project-cap-launches-api-and-bulk-data-service/)
The Library Innovation Lab (https://lil.law.harvard.edu/)  at Harvard Law School Library launched its Caselaw Access Project  (https://case.law/) API (https://api.case.law/v1/)  and bulk data service, which puts the full corpus of published U.S. case law online for anyone to access for free. Read more about the Caselaw Access Project's API and bulk data service in Fortune (http://fortune.com/2018/10/29/harvard-law-caselaw/) .

https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2018/10/29/professor-who-defined-dangerous-speech-how-trumps-rhetoric-pittsburgh-are-linked/

An expert on ‘dangerous speech’ explains how Trump’s rhetoric and the recent spate of violence are and aren’t linked (https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2018/10/29/professor-who-defined-dangerous-speech-how-trumps-rhetoric-pittsburgh-are-linked/)
“There have been a number of phrases that Trump has used repeatedly that have been picked up by other people on and offline that I suspect have true harmful effect,” explains Berkman Klein faculty associate Susan Benesch (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sbenesch)  in a Q&A with the Washington Post (https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2018/10/29/professor-who-defined-dangerous-speech-how-trumps-rhetoric-pittsburgh-are-linked/?utm_term=.728c02613bed)  about “dangerous speech” in the context of Donald Trump’s rhetoric.

https://www.cbc.ca/radio/spark/411-1.4879512/restaurants-have-strict-standards-to-protect-customers-tech-platforms-don-t-1.4879520

Restaurants have strict standards to protect customers. Tech platforms don't (https://www.cbc.ca/radio/spark/411-1.4879512/restaurants-have-strict-standards-to-protect-customers-tech-platforms-don-t-1.4879520)
Online tech platforms should become “information fiduciaries,” argues Berkman Klein co-founder Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain)  in this CBC radio interview (https://www.cbc.ca/radio/spark/411-1.4879512/restaurants-have-strict-standards-to-protect-customers-tech-platforms-don-t-1.4879520) . "The basic idea behind the fiduciary relationship is that the way they use that sensitive data should be to advance our interests and where their interests happen to conflict with ours, they've got to favor ours over theirs," he says.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3273111

Common-Knowledge Attacks on Democracy (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3273111)
BKC fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  and Henry Farrell assert “that scaling up computer security arguments to the level of the state, so that the entire polity is treated as an information system with associated attack surfaces and threat models, provides the best immediate way to understand these [soft cyber attacks] and how to mitigate them,” in a new Berkman Klein research paper (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3273111) .

https://www.fastcompany.com/90259343/trump-has-twitter-alexandria-ocasio-cortez-is-winning-instagram

Trump has Twitter. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez is winning Instagram (https://www.fastcompany.com/90259343/trump-has-twitter-alexandria-ocasio-cortez-is-winning-instagram)
BKC Research Affiliate An Xiao Mina (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/axmina)  and Ray Drainville explore Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s use of Instagram in an article for Fast Company (https://www.fastcompany.com/90259343/trump-has-twitter-alexandria-ocasio-cortez-is-winning-instagram) , providing examples of her Instagram posts and contrasting them with those from other political figures. “What’s striking about Ocasio-Cortez’s style is just how digitally native it seems,” Mina and Drainville write.

https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/11/do-big-social-media-platforms-have-effective-ad-policies/574609/

We Tested Facebook’s Ad Screeners and Some Were Too Strict (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/11/do-big-social-media-platforms-have-effective-ad-policies/574609/)
Do big social media platforms have effective ad policies? In this article for The Atlantic (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/11/do-big-social-media-platforms-have-effective-ad-policies/574609/)  Former BKC fellow Nathan Matias (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jnmatias)  and a team of researchers explain the findings of their work on Facebook and Google’s advertising policies. BKC Affiliate Vivek Krishnamurthy (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkrishnamurthy)  also lends insight into the work.

http://everydaymatters.rpi.edu/dont-leave-it-all-to-facebook-the-u-s-should-do-more-to-protect-our-data-privacy/

Don’t Leave It All to Facebook: The U.S. Should Do More To Protect Our Data Privacy (http://everydaymatters.rpi.edu/dont-leave-it-all-to-facebook-the-u-s-should-do-more-to-protect-our-data-privacy/)
