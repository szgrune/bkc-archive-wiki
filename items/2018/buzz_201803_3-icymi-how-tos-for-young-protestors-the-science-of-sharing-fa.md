The Science of Fake News (http://science.sciencemag.org/content/359/6380/1094.full)
"We must redesign our information ecosystem in the 21st century. This effort must be global in scope, as many countries, some of which have never developed a robust news ecosystem, face challenges around fake and real news that are more acute than in the United States," write the authors of this piece (http://science.sciencemag.org/content/359/6380/1094.full) from Science, including BKC's Yochai Benkler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ybenkler)  and Jonathan Zittrain (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jzittrain) . The article summarizes current work on "fake news" and calls for future interdisciplinary research.

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/svosoughi

The Spread of True and False News Online (http://science.sciencemag.org/content/359/6380/1146)
"How do truth and falsity diffuse differently, and what factors of human judgment explain these differences?" ask BKC fellow Soroush Vosoughi (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/svosoughi)  with co-authors Deb Roy and Sinan Aral in this new article (http://science.sciencemag.org/content/359/6380/1146.full) from Science. Vosoughi, Roy, and Aral analyzed rumor cascades on Twitter and found that falsehoods diffuse faster than truths, in part because they represent "novel" stories, which people are more likely to share.

Paw, La Mexicana que Desarolla el Acceso a una Cultura Libre (https://distintaslatitudes.net/paw-la-mexicana-desarrolla-acceso-una-cultura-libre)
In this interview (https://distintaslatitudes.net/paw-la-mexicana-desarrolla-acceso-una-cultura-libre) from Distintas Latitudes,  BKC affiliate Paola Villareal (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pvillarreal) , featured here in a special series ("Las Hackers") about women developers, discusses discovering computers and programming as an adolescent, and cultivating an interest in open software. She describes how these past experiences are now reflected in her current work as director of product engineering at Creative Commons.

Youth in Front: Urgent Advice about Youth Activism for Students and Teachers (http://blogs.edweek.org/edweek/edtechresearcher/2018/03/youth_in_front_urgent_advice_about_youth_activism_for_students_and_teachers.html)
BKC faculty associate Justin Reich (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jreich)  worked with over 100 volunteers, sourcing advice from young protesters and adult allies to create Youth in Front (http://www.youthinfront.org/) , an online resource for student organizers. "Young people are powerful civic actors, and during their apprenticeship of citizenship, their voices deserve to be heard. The youth in our community are leaders; the adults in our community are supporting them as they march up front," Reich states in this post (http://blogs.edweek.org/edweek/edtechresearcher/2018/03/youth_in_front_urgent_advice_about_youth_activism_for_students_and_teachers.html) from EdWeek.

Be the (Online) Change You Want to See (https://influenceonline.co.uk/2018/03/14/online-change-want-see/)
CIPR President's Medal Award (http://newsroom.cipr.co.uk/cluetrain-authors-honoured-and-thrilled-by-presidents-medal/) winner David Weinberger (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dweinberger) 's recent online lecture is summarized in this article (https://influenceonline.co.uk/2018/03/14/online-change-want-see/) from Influence Online. In his lecture, Weinberger examines the internet's effect on businesses, and subsequently society at large. "I do think that the Internet is shaping us, but not because technology is our master and shapes and controls us, but because it has enabled us to be more of who we are and we’re not going back," Weinberger concludes.
