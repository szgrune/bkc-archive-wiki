Open Access Week 2018: October 22-28
https://hlrdm.library.harvard.edu/open-access-week-2018

International Open Access Week is an opportunity to take action in making openness the default for research — to raise the visibility of scholarship, accelerate research.

This year's theme is "designing equitable foundations for open knowledge."

From the coordinators (http://www.openaccessweek.org/profiles/blogs/theme-of-2018-international-open-access-week-to-be-designing-equi) :

"While governments, funders, universities, publishers, and scholars are increasingly adopting open policies and practices, how these are actually implemented is still in flux. As open becomes the default, all stakeholders must be intentional about designing these new, open systems to ensure that they are inclusive, equitable, and truly serve the needs of a diverse global community."

Read more about Open Access Week 2018, and if you are at Harvard, join one of the activities (https://hlrdm.library.harvard.edu/open-access-week-2018)  to learn more about making research open.

It’s Not Enough For AI To Be “Ethical”; It Must Also Be “Rights Respecting” (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/its-not-enough-for-ai-to-be-ethical-it-must-also-be-rights-respecting-b87f7e215b97)

“Human rights deserves pride of place among the navigational aids that are guiding the development and deployment of AI systems that advance the common good while recognizing the fundamental dignity of each and every one of us,” write Berkman Klein Affiliate Vivek Krishnamurthy (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/vkrishnamurthy) , Program Coordinator Hannah Hilligoss (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/hhilligoss)  and Harvard Law alumnus Filippo A. Raso in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/its-not-enough-for-ai-to-be-ethical-it-must-also-be-rights-respecting-b87f7e215b97)  on the importance of thinking about both the human rights implications and the ethics of AI.

https://www.infoq.com/presentations/ethics-academia-industry

Ethics in Computing, from Academia to Industry (https://www.infoq.com/presentations/ethics-academia-industry)

Kathy Pham (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kpham)  highlights considerations of ethics, social responsibility, and long-term impacts of software industry products. In her talk (https://www.infoq.com/presentations/ethics-academia-industry) and panel (https://www.infoq.com/presentations/panel-ethics-computing)  Pham covers actions being taken in academia and industry today, as well as actionable paths forward to create cultures that build more responsible software and services for all people.

https://es.weforum.org/events/annual-meeting-of-the-new-champions/sessions/getting-digital-rights-right?tab=LiveBlogs&stream=day-1-83b8ec05-ddc8-46ed-af52-25a3cf1796bf&stream-item=us-china-trade-war-escalates

Tech Leadership in an Era of Digital (Mis)Trust (https://es.weforum.org/events/annual-meeting-of-the-new-champions/sessions/getting-digital-rights-right?tab=LiveBlogs&stream=day-1-83b8ec05-ddc8-46ed-af52-25a3cf1796bf&stream-item=us-china-trade-war-escalates)

From China's Cybersecurity Law to the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation, new policies aim to protect the privacy and security of personal data. They aim to answer the question, How can trust be strengthened in the digital economy?

Berkman Klein Executive Director Urs Gasser (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ugasser) joins President of Estonia Kersti Kaljulaid, Miki Ebara, Adam Ghetti, and Lee Xiaodong for a conversation at the World Economic Forum's Annual Meeting of the New Champions in Tianjin, China.

https://medium.com/hci-design-at-uw/what-we-lose-when-we-move-from-social-to-market-exchange-e1f80164b50

What We Lose When We Move From Social to Market Exchange (https://medium.com/hci-design-at-uw/what-we-lose-when-we-move-from-social-to-market-exchange-e1f80164b50)

Berkman Klein Faculty Associate Benjamin Mako Hill (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/mako)  published a paper (https://medium.com/hci-design-at-uw/what-we-lose-when-we-move-from-social-to-market-exchange-e1f80164b50)  on the Couchsurfing and Airbnb websites that covers trends in their use and what is expected of each service. He argues that their work shows a shift away from social exchange and toward markets, and describes what this means for the sharing economy. The paper will be presented at the ACM Conference on Computer-supported Cooperative Work and Social Computing.

https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/11/opinion/internet-hacking-cybersecurity-iot.html

Internet Hacking Is About to Get Much Worse (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/11/opinion/internet-hacking-cybersecurity-iot.html)

In his New York Times Op-Ed (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/11/opinion/internet-hacking-cybersecurity-iot.html) , Berkman Klein Fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  discusses computer insecurity and the direct dangers posed to physical spaces. Schneier argues that if our devices, information, and institutions are to remain secure, smart government regulation is inevitable. "We must engage with the future of internet security as a policy issue. Doing so requires a multifaceted approach, one that requires government involvement at every step."

https://medium.com/@cfiesler/the-black-mirror-writers-room-teaching-technology-ethics-through-speculation-f1a9e2deccf4

The Black Mirror Writer's Room: Teaching Technology Ethics through Speculation (https://medium.com/@cfiesler/the-black-mirror-writers-room-teaching-technology-ethics-through-speculation-f1a9e2deccf4)

Speculation around ethical regulation of futuristic technologies lends itself to compelling discussions about rights and the role of government in a technologically evolving society. Berkman Klein Faculty Associate Casey Fiesler (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/casey-fiesler)  engages her students in this approach (https://medium.com/@cfiesler/the-black-mirror-writers-room-teaching-technology-ethics-through-speculation-f1a9e2deccf4) : "There are a lot of important things we can be doing in the world to help, but I’d like to think that teaching technologists to think critically about responsibility and ethics is one way to keep our lives from into turning into a Black Mirror episode."

https://www.cfr.org/blog/three-problems-indias-draft-data-protection-bill

Three Problems with India’s Draft Data Protection Bill (https://www.cfr.org/blog/three-problems-indias-draft-data-protection-bill)

India is attempting to create a complex new framework for data protection that will require firms to physically host user data, but they might be moving too fast. Berkman Klein Fellow, Chinmayi Arun (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/carun) , discusses serious implications that the bill's features might pose for users, digital service companies, and governments in this Net Politics Blogpost. (https://www.cfr.org/blog/three-problems-indias-draft-data-protection-bill)  "This access to all personal data by the state poses an enormous threat to the right to privacy given the weak safeguards that exist in India against state surveillance. In combination with the data localization requirement, the Indian government will have unprecedented access to information about Facebook users."

https://www.wired.com/story/facebook-domestic-disinformation-algorithmic-megaphones/

Algorithmic Megaphones and Free Speech (https://www.wired.com/story/facebook-domestic-disinformation-algorithmic-megaphones/)

Where is the line between authentic free speech and the manipulation of speech? Is the right to speech equated with the right to reach millions of people? Renee Diresta (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/rdiresta) , Berkman Klein Affiliate, discusses these questions in her recent article (https://www.wired.com/story/facebook-domestic-disinformation-algorithmic-megaphones/)  about Facebook's account takedowns and the robust complaints around censorship that Facebook gamers and fake account creators adopt to fight back.

https://medium.com/s/story/stop-saying-privacy-is-dead-513dda573071

Stop Saying Privacy is Dead (https://medium.com/s/story/stop-saying-privacy-is-dead-513dda573071)

It can be easy to give in to privacy nihilism. In this essay (https://medium.com/s/story/stop-saying-privacy-is-dead-513dda573071) , Berkman Klein Faculty Associate Woodrow Hartzog (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/woodrow-hartzog)  and Evan Selinger, argue that privacy still exists. You just have to know where to look for it so you know what to fight for.

http://players.brightcove.net/45228659001/default_default/index.html?videoId=5846394387001

Evolving Technology Means Change to Legal Finance  (http://players.brightcove.net/45228659001/default_default/index.html?videoId=5846394387001)

New emerging technologies create opportunities, notably for greater efficiencies and financial inclusion in the global economy. But they also create new risks among issues such as money laundering, terrorist financing, and other illegal activities. This video (https://www.imf.org/en/News/Seminars/Conferences/2018/07/24/2018-seminar-on-law-and-financial-stability)  showcases Berkman Klein Affiliate Patrick Murck's (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/pmurck)  work on transformation in the financial services landscape due to globalizing and evolving technologies.
