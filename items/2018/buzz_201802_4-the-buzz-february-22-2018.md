New Work  Why Inclusion Matters for the Future of Artificial Intelligence
https://aiandinclusion.org/
AI-based technologies — and the vast datasets that power them — are reshaping a broad range of sectors of the economy and are increasingly affecting the ways in which we live our lives. But to date these systems remain largely the province of a few large companies and powerful nations, raising concerns over how they might exacerbate inequalities and perpetuate bias against underserved and underrepresented populations.

We are excited to share a new resource (https://aiandinclusion.org/) that assembles what we’ve learned from our collaborative work on AI and inclusion over the past year. The research, findings, and ideas illuminate key takeaways and will serve as a roadmap for future work on AI and inclusion. Learn more by reading Why Inclusion Matters for the Future of Artificial Intelligence (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/why-inclusion-matters-for-the-future-of-artificial-intelligence-2cb9d3b1b92b) , or dive right in to the resource at aiandinclusion.org.

If a Protest Takes Place on a National Internet, Does it Make a Sound? (https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/irans-national-information-network-faster-speeds-but-at-what-cost)
BKC affiliate Simin Kargar (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/skargar)  reports on critiques of Iran's state-controlled National Information Network (NIN), in this new blog post (https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/irans-national-information-network-faster-speeds-but-at-what-cost) from the Internet Monitor (https://cyber.harvard.edu/research/internetmonitor) . Concerns about NIN include lack of access to global information and fears about user-privacy. "Despite Iran’s concerted efforts to popularize the NIN’s application, appealing to users and acquiring their trust may be much harder than the government had envisioned," Kargar writes.

A Ruling Over Embedded Tweets Could Change Online Publishing (https://www.wired.com/story/embedded-tweets-copyright-law/)
A federal judge has ruled that embedding a tweet that contains an image may be considered copyright infringement. "The ruling is disappointing and may result in an increase in similar litigation, but all hope is not lost. The news organizations still have a number of potential defenses, including fair use," states Kendra Albert (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kalbert) , in this Wired piece (https://www.wired.com/story/embedded-tweets-copyright-law/) .

Defining the Dilemmas of Artificial Intelligence (https://spectrum.mit.edu/winter-2018/defining-the-dilemmas-of-artificial-intelligence/)
"I try to not just stay within academia...but actually talk to people who are making policy in the hopes that something can be done,” explains BKC Fellow Kate Darling (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/kdarling)  in this interview (https://spectrum.mit.edu/winter-2018/defining-the-dilemmas-of-artificial-intelligence/) with the MIT Spectrum, about her work on robotic technologies, ethics, and social policy.

https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/a-lack-of-emotional-intelligence-is-fueling-misogyny-and-racism-at-google-and-across-silicon-2d50f3b4c6db

A Lack of Emotional Intelligence is Fueling Misogyny and Racism at Google — and Across Silicon Valley (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/a-lack-of-emotional-intelligence-is-fueling-misogyny-and-racism-at-google-and-across-silicon-2d50f3b4c6db)
BKC fellow Luke Stark (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lstark)  argues for greater emotional intelligence within tech industries, in this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/a-lack-of-emotional-intelligence-is-fueling-misogyny-and-racism-at-google-and-across-silicon-2d50f3b4c6db) . Stark writes that having zero tolerance policies for any kind of bigotry is "both simple common sense, and long overdue: without greater emotional intelligence in the tech sector, we’re all put at risk by the intolerance at the heart of digital media."

Inclusive Sensory Ethnography: Studying New Media and Neurodiversity in Everyday Life (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1461444818755394?journalCode=nmsa)
Check out BKC faculty associate Meryl Alper (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/malper) 's new article (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1461444818755394?journalCode=nmsa) on inclusive sensory ethnography in New Media and Society. Alper introduces this methodology as an alternative to traditional, external sense-based ethnography, writing, "I propose inclusive sensory ethnography to account for greater neurodiversity in how humans process sensory input, as well as a fuller range of multi-sensory encounters with new media."
