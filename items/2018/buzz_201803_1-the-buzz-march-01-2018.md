New Work  The Yemen War Online: Propagation of Censored Content on Twitter
https://thenetmonitor.org/bulletins/the-yemen-war-online-propagation-of-censored-content-on-twitter
As a devastating civil war rages on in Yemen, online political networks that coalesce around the war use Twitter to propagate censored content from the open web, making it available to users behind open-web filtering regimes.

Mapping the activity of 11,894 Twitter accounts from April 2016 to November 2017 showed that government attempts to corral social media users into government-friendly media bubbles does not work, although government filters make it more difficult to access some content. Instead, social media users coalesce into self-defined media spheres aligned around social and political affinities.

Youth Gun Violence Prevention in a Digital Age (http://pediatrics.aappublications.org/content/early/2018/02/27/peds.2017-2438.full)
The firearm death rate in the United States is 10 times higher than other high-income, industrialized nations, and gun violence is particularly acute in large cities, where it tends to cluster in marginalized communities of color. Writing in Pediatrics (http://www.aappublications.org/) , Fellow Desmond Upton Patton (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dpatton)  and fellow researchers "envision innovative youth gun violence prevention strategies in which social media and advances in artificial intelligence are used to identify unique characteristics in social media data that might indicate when, how, and why gun violence may occur."

Towards Digital Constitutionalism? Mapping Attempts to Craft an Internet Bill of Rights (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1748048518757121?journalCode=gazb)
Analyzing the substantive content of 32 different declarations of Internet rights BKC Executive Director Urs Gasser (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ugasser)  and co-authors Lex Gill and Dennis Redeker introduce the term "digital constitutionalism," to describe a "constellation of initiatives that seek to articulate a set of political rights, governance norms, and limitations on the exercise of power on the Internet," in this piece (http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1748048518757121?journalCode=gazb) published in the International Communication Gazette.

http://www.epw.in/journal/2017/51/privacy-after-puttaswamy-judgment/machine-learning-and-artificial-intelligence.html

Interactions with the Right to Privacy: Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence (http://www.epw.in/journal/2017/51/privacy-after-puttaswamy-judgment/machine-learning-and-artificial-intelligence.html)
Artificial intelligence and big data present new challenges to protecting our right to privacy both online and offline. In her essay (http://www.epw.in/journal/2017/51/privacy-after-puttaswamy-judgment/machine-learning-and-artificial-intelligence.html) for Economic & Political Weekly (paywall) Suchana Seth (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sseth) , describes how existing socio-technical systems are not engineered to balance conflicting goals and tensions around privacy, and describes some possible solutions.

https://www.cnn.com/2018/02/27/opinions/ai-free-press-democracy-opinion-vishwanath/index.html

When a robot writes your news, what happens to democracy? (https://www.cnn.com/2018/02/27/opinions/ai-free-press-democracy-opinion-vishwanath/index.html)
How soon will artificial intelligence replace journalists and editors? Writing for CNN (https://www.cnn.com/2018/02/27/opinions/ai-free-press-democracy-opinion-vishwanath/index.html) , BKC faculty associate Arun Vishwanath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/avish) says it’s already happening, and describes where it could go if we aren't careful.

Parkland Shooting ‘Crisis Actor’ Videos Lead Users to a ‘Conspiracy Ecosystem’ on YouTube, New Research Shows (https://spectrum.mit.edu/winter-2018/defining-the-dilemmas-of-artificial-intelligence/)
"It’s not YouTube getting gamed. It’s that YouTube has allowed this to flourish,” states BKC faculty associate Jonathan Albright (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/jalbright) , in this interview (https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/the-switch/wp/2018/02/25/parkland-shooting-crisis-actor-videos-lead-users-to-a-conspiracy-ecosystem-on-youtube-new-research-shows/?utm_term=.39a233b6cac8)  about online conspiracy ecosystems with the Washington Post. Albright explains how rumors of "crisis actors" after the recent Parkland school shooting are part of a much larger trend of conspiracy content, facilitated by YouTube's recommendation algorithms.

Putting the J(ustice) in FAT (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/putting-the-j-ustice-in-fat-28da2b8eae6d)
In this Medium post (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/putting-the-j-ustice-in-fat-28da2b8eae6d) , BKC Fellow Ben Green (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bgreen)  calls for the disentanglement of the concepts of unfairness and injustice within the Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency movement. "[H]ighlighting fairness at the expense of justice reinforces the dangerous presumption that technical systems are politically-neutral tools. While this view is appealing to computer scientists, it denies the well-established fact that algorithms exist in a social, political, and legal context where technical systems can bolster power, alter perceptions, and shroud behavior."
