New Research: Evaluating the human rights impacts of six current uses of AI
https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/artificial-intelligence-human-rights
In Artificial Intelligence & Human Rights: Opportunities & Risks (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/artificial-intelligence-human-rights)  we provide a first-of-its-kind analysis of how current deployments of artificial intelligence impact human rights — for uses ranging from medical diagnoses to criminal justice to the grading of homework assignments.

"Human rights law provides an agreed set of norms and a shared language and institutional infrastructure for helping to ensure that the promises of AI are met and its greatest perils are avoided," say Berkman Klein researchers Filippo Raso, Hannah Hilligoss, Vivek Krishnamurthy, Christopher Bavitz, and Levin Kim. "By digging deep into current AI implementations, we see how they impact the full range of human rights guaranteed by international law...[and] gain insight into the unequal distribution of the positive and negative impacts of AI on human rights throughout society."

Read more here (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/artificial-intelligence-human-rights)

Is privacy privacy? (http://rsta.royalsocietypublishing.org/content/376/2128/20170358)
Berkman Klein Fellow Alexandra Wood  (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/awood) and Kobbi Nissim (http://people.cs.georgetown.edu/~kobbi/) , a Professor at Georgetown University, publish a paper (http://rsta.royalsocietypublishing.org/content/376/2128/20170358)  on the differing and evolving notions of privacy across normative and technical perspectives. “Adopting an understanding of privacy that is consistent across its technical and normative dimensions will be critical to ensuring personal data are adequately safeguarded over the long term,” the authors write, noting that this is a difficult feat to achieve.
