"The only way to reliably protect elections from both malice and accident is to use something that is not hackable or unreliable at scale; the best way to do that is to back up as much of the system as possible with paper," writes Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier) in this Guardian article (https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2018/apr/18/american-elections-hack-bruce-scheier) .

Listen: Privacy and Your Data
http://nhpr.org/post/facebooks-data-scandal-and-you#stream/0

Facebook's Data Scandal and You? (http://nhpr.org/post/facebooks-data-scandal-and-you#stream/0)
"When we engage social media, and digital media more broadly, whether it is our phones, our fitbits...the central premise of the business model is that we are getting free or lower-cost services in exchange for giving virtually unlimited access to all types of data," explains Leah Plunkett (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/lplunkett) in this interview with New Hampshire Public Radio (http://nhpr.org/post/facebooks-data-scandal-and-you#stream/0) .

https://www.kcrw.com/news-culture/shows/press-play-with-madeleine-brand/when-it-comes-to-privacy-are-comcast-and-verizon-worse-than-facebook#seg-privacy-beyond-facebook-what-other-companies-know-about-us

Privacy Beyond Facebook: What Other Companies Know About Us (https://www.kcrw.com/news-culture/shows/press-play-with-madeleine-brand/when-it-comes-to-privacy-are-comcast-and-verizon-worse-than-facebook#seg-privacy-beyond-facebook-what-other-companies-know-about-us)
"Our data is a very, very valuable asset, and increasingly, companies are realizing that they're not going to make money by turning that over for free, they're going to make money using machine learning algorithms and other forms of computational inference to sell information that data provides about us," shares Salome Viljoen (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/sviljoen)  in this interview with KCRW (https://www.kcrw.com/news-culture/shows/press-play-with-madeleine-brand/when-it-comes-to-privacy-are-comcast-and-verizon-worse-than-facebook#seg-privacy-beyond-facebook-what-other-companies-know-about-us) .

Student Work and Activities
https://today.law.harvard.edu/emerging-technologies-privacy-design/

Emerging Technologies: Privacy by Design (https://today.law.harvard.edu/emerging-technologies-privacy-design/)
In this article (https://today.law.harvard.edu/emerging-technologies-privacy-design/) for Harvard Law Today, HLS student Rocky Li, describes a recent symposium hosted by students of Prof. Urs Gasser’s Comparative Digital Privacy seminar. Experts from government, private practice, industry, and academia discussed privacy-related topics such as the difficulty of defining privacy to a comparison of the regulatory regimes in the U.S. and Europe.
