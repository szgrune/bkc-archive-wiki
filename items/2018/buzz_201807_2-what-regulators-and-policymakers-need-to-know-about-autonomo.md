New Research: What Regulators & Policymakers Need to Know About Autonomous Vehicles

http://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2018-07/4-policy-papers-autonomous-vehicles

As vehicles are increasingly automated, it becomes not only imperative to improve human-machine interaction, but also to anticipate what this means for the future of labor, explore how these vehicles will push the limits of existing governance frameworks as they drive across geographic boundaries, and identify the necessary forms of transparency needed to build new accident liability regimes.

Aida Joaquin Acosta (/people/ajoaquinacosta) , with researchers from the Berkman Klein Center, are excited to share this series of introductory policy papers on autonomous vehicles (AVs) aimed at policymakers around the globe who are beginning to grapple with the realities of regulating AVs within their jurisdictions. These papers describe key regulatory issues for AVs, a range of policy and governance frameworks, and suggestions for policy innovation and experimentation.

Learn more (http://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2018-07/4-policy-papers-autonomous-vehicles)

http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/5-technological-factors-regulators-and-policymakers-need-know
AVs are more than a vehicle with a camera and a hands-free steering wheel; AVs are a transformative technology. They promise to change the traditional paradigms of mobility, road safety, communication technologies, and conceptions of privacy, as well as challenge ethical norms and change the way humans interact with robots. This policy primer (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/5-technological-factors-regulators-and-policymakers-need-know) explores these key technological dimensions of AVs, giving an inclusive, high-level view of AV technology, and presents five essential elements policymakers should know.

http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/smart-move-24-essentials-swot-analysis-policymakers-need-consider
This SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats) analysis (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/smart-move-24-essentials-swot-analysis-policymakers-need-consider) seeks to expand policymakers’ governance toolbox, bridging information asymmetries between regulators and technologists and helping them design comprehensive policies to proactively address the unintended consequences of AVs.

http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/what-governments-across-globe-are-doing-seize-benefits-autonomous-vehicles
Learning from one another’s experiences can inspire creative actions and facilitate improved AV policies. However, the impact of the initiatives will depend on the legal, social, and economic environment in which they are developed and implemented. This policy paper (http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/what-governments-across-globe-are-doing-seize-benefits-autonomous-vehicles) introduces some of the main regulatory actions being undertaken in the international arena in relation to AVs. We cover initiatives from the G7 international meetings, the United States, the European Union, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, China, and New Zealand.

http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/3-practical-tools-help-regulators-develop-better-laws-and-policies
This policy paper (http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/3-practical-tools-help-regulators-develop-better-laws-and-policies) analyzes several categories of regulatory challenges surrounding AVs and introduces three practical tools (Legal Interfaces, Law Labs, and Structured Dialogues) that can be utilized by policymakers and regulators in developing effective AV policies.
