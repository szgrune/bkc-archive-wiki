This Op-Ed Wasn't Written by AI (https://edition.cnn.com/2018/01/29/opinions/ai-job-threats-opinion-vishwanath/index.html)
BKC faculty associate Arun Vishwanath (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/avish)  describes the downsides to AI-run automated retail stores in this piece from CNN (https://edition.cnn.com/2018/01/29/opinions/ai-job-threats-opinion-vishwanath/index.html) . The solution, Vishwanath writes, "is not to stop the march of progress but to prepare for it with forward thinking investments in education, human capital and public policy."

https://www.nytimes.com/2018/01/30/opinion/strava-privacy.html

The Latest Data Privacy Debacle (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/01/30/opinion/strava-privacy.html)
"Data privacy is more like air quality or safe drinking water, a public good that cannot be effectively regulated by trusting in the wisdom of millions of individual choices," writes faculty associate Zeynep Tufekci (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/zeynep)  in this piece (https://www.nytimes.com/2018/01/30/opinion/strava-privacy.html) for The New York Times about recent reports that a fitness app may have inadvertently revealed the location of military bases and personnel.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3108238

Rebalancing Regulation of Speech: Hyper-Local Content on Global Web-Based Platforms (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3108238)
In this new paper (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3108238) , BKC fellow Chinmayi Arun (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/carun)  describes the difficulties that arise when platforms' "misguided reactions to government pressure to regulate harmful speech threaten freedom of expression," and offers ideas for approaching research on this global issue.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3108238

Don't Make AI Artificially Stupid in the Name of Transparency (https://www.wired.com/story/dont-make-ai-artificially-stupid-in-the-name-of-transparency/)
"Fully tapping the power of machine learning may well mean relying on results that are literally impossible to explain to the human mind," argues David Weinberger (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dweinberger) in this op-ed  (https://www.wired.com/story/dont-make-ai-artificially-stupid-in-the-name-of-transparency/) for WIRED. (For a longer version of David's piece, see "Optimization over Explanation (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/optimization-over-explanation-41ecb135763d) " on the BKC Medium collection.)

https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/01/spectre-meltdown-cybersecurity/551147/

The New Way Your Computer Can Be Attacked (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/01/spectre-meltdown-cybersecurity/551147/)
BKC fellow Bruce Schneier (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/bschneier)  discusses the recently discovered microprocessor vulnerabilities, Spectre and Meltdown, in this new article (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/01/spectre-meltdown-cybersecurity/551147/) from The Atlantic. "Un-patchable vulnerabilities in the deepest recesses of the world’s computer hardware is the new normal. It’s going to leave us all much more vulnerable in the future," he writes.

https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ebietti

Data is Power: Towards Additional Guidance on Profiling and Automated Decision-Making in the GDPR (https://journals.winchesteruniversitypress.org/index.php/jirpp/article/view/45)
"[H]ow can we protect people’s privacy when intimate information can be predicted from seemingly mundane data?" BKC affiliate Elettra Bietti (https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ebietti)  considers the social implications of automated profiling in this co-authored piece published in IRP&P (https://journals.winchesteruniversitypress.org/index.php/jirpp/article/view/45) .

https://medium.com/@natematias/the-american-museum-of-exploding-cars-and-toys-that-kill-you-5123f35cb271

The American Museum of Exploding Cars and Toys That Kill You (https://medium.com/@natematias/the-american-museum-of-exploding-cars-and-toys-that-kill-you-5123f35cb271)
"How do lawsuits grow our understanding of the risks and harms of new technologies?" asks J. Nathan Matias (http://natematias.com/) , BKC alum. In this Medium piece (https://medium.com/@natematias/the-american-museum-of-exploding-cars-and-toys-that-kill-you-5123f35cb271) , Matias considers these questions and describes his visit to the American Museum of Tort Law - a trip he recommends for all tech engineers and designers.
