** A Harm-Reduction Framework for Algorithmic Fairness (http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/harm-reduction-framework-algorithmic-fairness)
------------------------------------------------------------
When algorithms are left to make important decisions, the results can have a profound impact on individuals, argue Alex Wood (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/awood) , Effy Vayena (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/evayena)  and Micah Altman (https://informatics.mit.edu/people/micah-altman) , in a new report (http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/harm-reduction-framework-algorithmic-fairness)  published by IEEE Security & Privacy. After studying foreseeable and unforeseeable harms, the authors propose a harm-reduction framework for making algorithms more fair.

** Bridging the Gap: Computer Science and Legal Approaches to Privacy (https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v31/02.-Article-Wood-7.21.pdf)
------------------------------------------------------------
Recent data breaches have shown that data privacy is an increasing concern. A new article (http://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2018/bridging-gap-between-computer-science-and-legal-approaches-privacy)  from BKC's Alex Wood (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/awood) , David O’Brien (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/dobrien) , Urs Gasser (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ugasser) , and their coauthors, published in the Harvard Journal of Law & Technology (https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v31/02.-Article-Wood-7.21.pdf) , proposes a methodology for thinking about privacy through models from both computer science  and the law.

https://morethancode.cc/

#MoreThanCode: Practitioners Reimagine the Landscape of Technology for Justice and Equity (https://morethancode.cc/T4SJ_fullreport_082018_AY_web.pdf)
A new report (https://morethancode.cc/T4SJ_fullreport_082018_AY_web.pdf)  from the Technology for Social Justice Project (T4SJ) analyzes the current technology ecosystem through interviews and focus groups with almost 200 technology practitioners, offering recommendations for inclusivity, diversity, and new ideas.

Digital Expungement (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3214335)
"Digital technology might lead to the extinction of criminal rehabilitation. In the digital era, criminal history records that were expunged by the state remain widely available... " This article (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3214335) from the Maryland Law Review by Eldar Haber (http://cyber.harvard.edu/people/ehaber) "offers a legal framework for reducing the collateral consequences of expunged criminal records by offering to re-conceptualize the public nature of criminal records."
