** I, search bot (https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/10/31/1106504/ai-search-could-break-the-web/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Benjamin Brooks shows how the future of AI search is at stake in a legal case against Perplexity AI. He opines that copyright isn’t the right tool for addressing digital economy issues raised by AI search: “AI pioneers should seize this opportunity to lay the foundations for a smart, equitable, and scalable reward system.”

https://ash.harvard.edu/articles/sunset-and-renew-section-230-should-protect-human-speech-not-algorithmic-virality/

** Repeal and renew (https://ash.harvard.edu/articles/sunset-and-renew-section-230-should-protect-human-speech-not-algorithmic-virality/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Allison Stanger, with Audrey Tang and Jaron Lanier, argues that we must reimagine CDA Section 230 to protect free speech and curtail harassment. "By sunsetting Section 230 and implementing new legislation that holds proprietary algorithms accountable for demonstrable harm, we can finally extend First Amendment protections to the digital public square, something long overdue."

https://www.techpolicy.press/the-weaponization-of-things-israels-technoviolence-a-litmus-test-for-technologists/

** Technology & violence (https://www.techpolicy.press/the-weaponization-of-things-israels-technoviolence-a-litmus-test-for-technologists/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Afsaneh Rigot argues that military tactics in Lebanon and Gaza have global import: "The weaponization of communication technologies...raises vital questions about the design and use of technology, particularly regarding its potential for dual-use." She calls for technology design that prevents weaponization—and for pushback "on the normalization of techno-terror."

https://www.computer.org/csdl/magazine/co/2024/11/10718663/213P6JLCBCo

** AI hacking (https://www.computer.org/csdl/magazine/co/2024/11/10718663/213P6JLCBCo)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier discusses AI and security. "AIs are already being trained to find vulnerabilities in computer code, and it’s a straightforward extension to believe that they will soon be able to find tax loopholes. And then they’ll be trained to find loopholes in other systems of rules. And eventually, they will be able to do that sort of thing faster and more effectively than humans."

https://seas.harvard.edu/news/2024/10/integrating-traditional-and-academic-knowledge
