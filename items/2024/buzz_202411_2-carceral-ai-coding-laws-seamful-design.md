** Prediction and punishment (https://www.carceral-ai.com/download-pdf)
------------------------------------------------------------

Dasha Pruss and coauthors (including Petra Molnar and Marissa Gerchick) critique carceral uses of AI and offer suggestions for curbing them. "Despite their high price tag and rhetoric of reform, in practice, carceral AI systems like PredPol and LASER cause the most harm to those our society tends to criminalize—communities of color, immigrants, people with disabilities, and poor people."

https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686937

** Code-ifying the law (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686937)
------------------------------------------------------------

Ben Green and coauthors identify disciplinary divides in the ways that computer scientists and legal experts translate law into code. "Our findings demonstrate that the epistemic divide between computer science and law makes the translation of law into software fertile ground for errors, misunderstandings, and policy distortions."

https://www.techpolicy.press/what-kafka-can-teach-us-about-privacy-in-the-age-of-ai/

** Reconsidering privacy law (https://www.techpolicy.press/what-kafka-can-teach-us-about-privacy-in-the-age-of-ai/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Inspired by Kafka, Woodrow Hartzog suggests that privacy law recenter baseline protections, rather than individual consent, to protect from AI-specific threats. "The societal structure model that Dan [Solove] and I propose is built primarily around...imposing affirmative substantive obligations on those that are collecting our personal information or surveilling us to act in ways that are consistent with what we want for society, for human values."

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06753

** Seamful systems (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06753)
------------------------------------------------------------

Can AI errors help users? Upol Ehsan and coauthors propose that "seamful design" in explainable AI can be leveraged to boost users' understanding and agency. "While black-boxing AI systems can make the user experience seamless, hiding the seams risks disempowering users to mitigate fallouts from AI mistakes."

Speaker at the lectern during the first BKC x AISST event.
