** Fediverse Schema Observatory (https://asml.cyber.harvard.edu/fediverseobservatory/)
------------------------------------------------------------

How can we make it easier for users to control their experience and presence on social media? ASML Senior Engineer Darius Kazemi shares his new tool, the Fediverse Schema Observatory, which aims to capture basic information about content shared on the Fediverse in order to help engineers create software that’s compatible across servers. Before the tool is officially launched, we want to hear from you! Whether you’re plugged into the Fediverse or you’re a general Internet user, your feedback on the safety and value of this tool is needed. Read Darius' post and request for comments here. (https://asml.cyber.harvard.edu/fediverseobservatory/)

** Loneliness & phones (https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/are-our-phones-making-us-lonelier/id1457513598?i=1000674829185)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jeffrey Hall discusses how people can heal toxic relationships with their phones. “And that's where I think this conversation gets really watered down, is that if we just try to throw the baby out with the bathwater [and say that] all media is bad, we forget there are huge, meaningful ways that social media, texting, can be part of an ecosystem of connection.”

** AI, grades, plagiarism (https://slate.com/life/2024/10/ai-chat-gpt-students-cheating-detection.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

When does students' AI use constitute a violation of existing plagiarism policies? Jane Rosenzweig explores one Massaschusetts case which "tells us less about district cheating policies and more about what happens when an education system that so often emphasizes grades over learning ends up on a collision course with a technology that enables students to bypass learning in favor of grades."

https://www.techpolicy.press/the-critical-role-of-research-in-the-fight-for-algorithmic-accountability/

** Research & accountability (https://www.techpolicy.press/the-critical-role-of-research-in-the-fight-for-algorithmic-accountability/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Marissa Gerchick and Olga Akselrod make a case for independent research, detailing the ACLU's complaint against a hiring technology company which offers AI interviewing and candidate-evaluation tools. "Academic researchers...play a critical role in the fight for accountability when algorithmic tools are used in high-stakes areas like hiring."

https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/10/transnational-ai-and-corporate-imperialism?lang=en

** Global systems, global harms (https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/10/transnational-ai-and-corporate-imperialism?lang=en)
------------------------------------------------------------

Chinmayi Arun examines the impact of AI tools on the Majority World, arguing that it constitutes a form of global imperialism owing to the unequal protections that citizens are afforded worldwide. "If AI’s harms emerge from its global political economy, advocates and regulators may find that coordinated global action, not just local action, is necessary."

https://www.linkedin.com/posts/anitab-org_ghc24-abie-award-winners-activity-7249539079338405890-lZMM/
