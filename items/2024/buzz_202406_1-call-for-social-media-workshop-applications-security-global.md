** Digital safety (https://publicinfrastructure.org/podcast/101-diana-freed/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Diana Freed sits down with Ethan Zuckerman to talk about digital safety for young people and survivors of intimate partner violence. "[W]e need to have transparency and system design that allows people to easily understand information flow and who has access to their information while they’re using the technologies and also how to disconnect and protect themselves."

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4626426

** Spyware decade (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4626426)
------------------------------------------------------------

Mailyn Fidler chronicles the emergence of modern spyware within a broader socio-technical marketplace and provides recommendations for regulations moving forward. "[C]ountries should focus on building and strengthening multilateral coalitions of the willing, rather than on strong-arming existing multilateral institutions into working on the problem."

https://time.com/6979557/unregulated-border-technology-migration-essay/

** High surveillance (https://time.com/6979557/unregulated-border-technology-migration-essay/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Petra Molnar explains the need for change in how experimental technologies are used at borders. "The biggest problem...is that the opaque and discretionary world of border enforcement and immigration decision-making is built on societal structures underpinned by intersecting systemic racism and historical discrimination against people migrating."

https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3589335.3651481

** DAO governance (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3589335.3651481)
------------------------------------------------------------

Samer Hassan and coauthors undertake a quantitative analysis of the Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) ecosystem. "Most DAOs have a short lifespan and low participation. There is also a positive correlation between community size and voting power concentration. Like other online communities, DAOs seem to follow the iron law: becoming increasingly oligarchic as they grow."

https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/global-ai-regulation-protecting-rights-leveraging-collaboration-b0da8e6a704d

** Global AI regulation (https://medium.com/berkman-klein-center/global-ai-regulation-protecting-rights-leveraging-collaboration-b0da8e6a704d)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lis Sylvan and Niharika Vattikonda report on the global Network of Internet & Society Centers' recent gathering of AI regulation experts. "Though approaches across the globe may be different, a common cross-cutting theme of the work is balance: protecting rights versus supporting innovation, legislating a critical technology while its capacity and impact is still developing, and providing necessary limitations while allowing nimble innovation."

https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/seeing-data-structure

** Seeing Like a Data Structure (https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/seeing-data-structure)
------------------------------------------------------------

Barath Raghavan and Bruce Schneier narrate how we landed in our current technological environment; by understanding how we got here, they argue, we can better craft a way to live with technology. "Data structures dominate our world and are a byproduct of the rational modern era, but they are ushering in an age of chaos. We need to embrace and tame, but not extinguish, this chaos for a better world."

https://theconversation.com/drakes-beef-with-kendrick-lamar-isnt-nearly-as-important-as-his-tiff-with-tupac-shakurs-estate-over-using-the-dead-rappers-voice-229471

** Beefs with AI (https://theconversation.com/drakes-beef-with-kendrick-lamar-isnt-nearly-as-important-as-his-tiff-with-tupac-shakurs-estate-over-using-the-dead-rappers-voice-229471)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jabari Evans dives into the legal gray area that AI technologies occupy within creative fields. "Because AI large language models are designed to be trained on a wide range of sources for original work, it is still difficult to determine, without proof of intent, what is outright theft and what is simply a product of this range of influences."
