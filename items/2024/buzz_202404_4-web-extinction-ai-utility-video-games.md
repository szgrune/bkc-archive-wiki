** End of the world wide web (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2024/04/generative-ai-search-llmo/678154/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Judith Donath and Bruce Schneier warn that, without intervention, the continued expansion of generative AI will end the Web's utility as a public resource. "It is too late to stop the emergence of AI. Instead, we need to think about what we want next, how to design and nurture spaces of knowledge creation and communication for a human-centric world."

https://www.citationneeded.news/ai-isnt-useless/

** Kind of useful (https://www.citationneeded.news/ai-isnt-useless/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Molly White finds generative AI lackluster for advertised and commonly attempted uses but potentially well suited for some tasks—but the social utility of those tasks is questionable. “When critics dismiss AI outright, I think in many cases this weakens the criticism, as readers who have used and benefited from AI tools think ‘wait, that’s not been my experience at all’.”

Plus, Chris Bavitz delivers a "Last Lecture" to the HLS class of 2024, focusing on the promises and perils of generative AI (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/with-an-ai-assist-bavitz-urges-students-to-look-beyond-the-binary/) .

https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/04/19/cyberlaw-clinic-testifies-before-copyright-office-in-dmca-hearings/

** Video games (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/04/19/cyberlaw-clinic-testifies-before-copyright-office-in-dmca-hearings/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Cyberlaw Clinic testifies before the Copyright Office on exemptions to §1201 of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, which prohibits the circumvention of certain technical measures that protect copyrighted material. In earlier filings, the Clinic "(1) advocated for the removal of the single-user restriction on software outside the physical premises of an eligible institution...and (2) argued that preserved video games should be available to scholars remotely."

Plus, Kendra Albert discusses video game history (https://gdcvault.com/play/1034220/What-s-New-in-Game) on a Game Developers Conference panel.

https://www.polcommtech.com/post/counter-speech-as-content-moderation-with-kesa-white

** Dog whistles (https://www.polcommtech.com/post/counter-speech-as-content-moderation-with-kesa-white)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kesa White joins Elizabeth Dubois to discuss her work on "dog whistling," and argues that, while counter speech can be helpful in limited situations, it does not fix the issue of hate speech online. "Humor is really one of those big things that is used in a lot of these communities to kind of tow the line between being flagged for moderation, but then also being able to convey their message across to their followers or their echo chambers, whoever it may be."
