** Artificial Worldviews (https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2023/12/mapping-chat-gpt-metalab)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kim Albrecht's project to map the knowledge base of ChatGPT focuses on ChatGPT's outputs to learn about the chatbot's worldview. "When you request something from ChatGPT, after a while, you get some kind of intuition about how that system behaves, how it acts, what it’s capable and incapable of doing, how it’s structuring the world.”

https://carrcenter.hks.harvard.edu/making-a-movement

** Reflections on human rights (https://carrcenter.hks.harvard.edu/making-a-movement)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jonathan Zittrain, Allissa Richardson, Bruce Schneier, and Martha Minow are among a group of scholars who provide reflections on the 75th Anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in Making a Movement: The History and Future of Human Rights. Their reflections focus on the public/private technology divide (Zittrain), human rights in the internet age (Schneier), social media's capacity to visually show experiences of marginalized communities (Richardson), and the remarkableness of the universal community's ability to come together and adopt the Declaration 75 years ago (Minow). The full collection is here. (https://carrcenter.hks.harvard.edu/making-a-movement)

https://rebootingsocialmedia.org/2023/12/15/the-right-to-information-in-the-private-sector/

** Information access (https://rebootingsocialmedia.org/2023/12/15/the-right-to-information-in-the-private-sector/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lisa Austin argues for a rethinking of rights to information in the private sector. "What the right to information requires is that our digitally-mediated world be created in such a way (through law and policy as well as technological means) that it can be independently knowable and interrogated and that this ability is equitably distributed."

https://www-msnbc-com.cdn.ampproject.org/c/s/www.msnbc.com/msnbc/amp/rcna128771

** A dangerous path (https://www-msnbc-com.cdn.ampproject.org/c/s/www.msnbc.com/msnbc/amp/rcna128771)
------------------------------------------------------------

Leah Plunkett describes how algorithms can lead adults to increasingly private content by underage social media users. "We need to fundamentally change the game for youth digital privacy and safety, both online and off, so that youth rights are central to social media and other tech success, not stuck on the sidelines."

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051221144315

** Harm & safety online (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051221144315)
------------------------------------------------------------

Zahra Stardust and collaborators investigate how social media platforms perform safety. "Platforms respond to and draw upon external controversies and media panics to selectively construct matters of concern related to safety and harm. They then reactively propose solutions that serve as justification for further investment in and scaling up of automated, data-intensive surveillance and verification technologies."
