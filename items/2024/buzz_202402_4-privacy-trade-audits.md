** Facial recognition (https://www.aclu.org/news/privacy-technology/when-it-comes-to-facial-recognition-there-is-no-such-thing-as-a-magic-number)
------------------------------------------------------------

Marissa Gerchick and Matt Cagle argue that facial recognition test scores mislead communities about the technology's harms. "The problems of facial recognition run deep and beyond the software itself. Facial recognition is dangerous if it’s inaccurate — a problem that testing aims to address — but also dangerous even if it could hypothetically be perfectly accurate."

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4714663

** Trade and privacy (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4714663)
------------------------------------------------------------

Anupam Chander explains the intersection between international trade law and privacy laws. "We can protect privacy even while enabling trade in digital services. Trade disciplines need not undermine privacy, but rather help ensure that claims of privacy protection are not merely disguised protectionism."

https://www.techpolicy.press/ensuring-digital-services-act-audits-deliver-on-their-promise/

** Digital Services Act audits (https://www.techpolicy.press/ensuring-digital-services-act-audits-deliver-on-their-promise/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jason Pielemeier, Ramsha Jahangir, and Hilary Ross critique the EU DSA audits. "Effective risk assessment and assurance requires committing to a set of shared public principles based on international human rights, as well as detailed guidelines on how to implement those principles, a standardized process to assess that implementation, and a mechanism to accredit and train assessors."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sz9eogMk6lQ&embeds_referring_euri=https%3A%2F%2Fcyber.harvard.edu%2F&source_ve_path=Mjg2NjQsMjM4NTE&feature=emb_title

** Deep Learning (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sz9eogMk6lQ&embeds_referring_euri=https%3A%2F%2Fcyber.harvard.edu%2F&source_ve_path=Mjg2NjQsMjM4NTE&feature=emb_title)
------------------------------------------------------------

Maroussia Lévesque facilitated a conversation between Justice Rosalie Silberman Abella and Martha Minow on equity, equality, and fairness in the time of algorithms. "You cannot satisfy, mathematically, multiple conceptions of equality at the same time. It's impossible," said Professor Minow, discussing the importance of explicit choices when creating an algorithm.

https://www.bstjournal.com/article/id/11480/

** Posthumanism (https://www.bstjournal.com/article/id/11480/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Magda Romanska investigates posthuman disability studies and where the field fits in with conversations around modern technologies. "By probing the limits of human and machine collaboration, the field of posthuman disability studies can also provide a broader theoretical framework for critical theory, including potential reframing of race, gender, sexuality, and other identity theories."
