** Interventions for Online Harassment of Journalists (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2024/white-paper-interventions-online-harassment-journalists)
------------------------------------------------------------

In a new BKC white paper, Molly Cinnamon recommends interventions to interrupt the harassment of journalists online and, ultimately, the chilling of free speech. "By elevating existing solutions and harnessing collective power across industries, we can disrupt the dogpile and empower speech without reprisal—for journalists and anyone online."

** Olympic takedowns (https://lumendatabase.org/blog_entries/articles-about-olympic-athlete-at-center-of-takedown-campaign)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lumen uncovers evidence of a coordinated and potentially automated fraudulent DMCA takedown campaign targeting articles about a Russian Olympian. Lumen explains how it relates to a broader ecosystem of takedown requests to suppress information online. "The effort to remove that information involves sending tens of thousands of slightly different, but mostly identical notices from dozens of countries and fake senders, but with just a handful of possible descriptions."

https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/oa-edit/10.4324/9781003472148/social-processes-online-hate-joseph-walther-ronald-rice

** Online hate (https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/oa-edit/10.4324/9781003472148/social-processes-online-hate-joseph-walther-ronald-rice)
------------------------------------------------------------

Joseph B. Walther and Ronald Rice edit a new open access volume, Social Processes of Online Hate. The book brings together a range of international experts covering an array of themes, including the abuse of women, antisemitism, pornography, radicalization, and extreme political youth movements. Contributors examine the specific social factors and processes that facilitate these forms of hate and propose new approaches for explaining them.

https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/08/02/clinic-publishes-update-to-security-researchers-guide-to-legal-risk/

** Risks of security research (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/08/02/clinic-publishes-update-to-security-researchers-guide-to-legal-risk/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kendra Albert and Sunoo Park updated their A Researcher’s Guide to Some Legal Risks of Security Research with new case law and developments. "In a landscape of legal uncertainty, it’s not all about jumping through legalistic hoops: bear in mind that it will often reduce your legal risk to conduct your research in ways that are aligned with widely accepted norms in the field and be respectful of the interests of others who might be impacted by your research."

https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-crowdstrike-outage-and-market-driven-brittleness

** CrowdStrike outage (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-crowdstrike-outage-and-market-driven-brittleness)
------------------------------------------------------------

Barath Raghavan and Bruce Schneier explain how prioritizing expediency has filled our technology infrastructure with many single points of failure. "Today’s internet systems are too complex to hope that if we are smart and build each piece correctly the sum total will work right. We have to deliberately break things and keep breaking them."

https://ecfr.eu/article/operation-regulation-strengthening-latin-americas-ai-governance/

** AI in Latin America (https://ecfr.eu/article/operation-regulation-strengthening-latin-americas-ai-governance/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Víctor Muñoz argues that the European Union should collaborate with Latin American countries attempting to regulate AI. "Building on its relationship with the region, the EU has many avenues of cooperation available with Latin American governments to ensure that AI is developed and used in a way that benefits both regions and contributes to a stable and secure global environment."
