** Algorithmic love (https://time.com/6694129/dating-app-inequality-essay/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Apryl Williams describes dating app algorithms as a reinforcer of longstanding racial biases that privilege whiteness and deprioritize matching women of color. "If dating apps continue to follow the tech industry’s trend of building algorithms that accept and amplify outdated forms of social injustice, they will fail their burgeoning and diverse user base."

https://hls.harvard.edu/today/how-artificial-intelligence-can-help-build-real-intelligence-in-the-classroom/

** AI in the classroom (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/how-artificial-intelligence-can-help-build-real-intelligence-in-the-classroom/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Sarah Newman and Sebastian Rodriguez explain MetaLAB's AI Pedagogy Project. "The project’s main goal is to empower educators who are interested in considering the implications of AI—not to force anyone to adopt it." As Newman explains, "We want this to be a starting place, to use it as a way for educators to reflect on their own pedagogy and the goals of their teaching.”

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13675494231223572

** Not so new (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13675494231223572)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nishant Shah offers two early moments in computational history to reframe conversations around generative AI. "These two accounts are not meant to be exhaustive or definitive ways of understanding the new questions that emerge in the face of Gen-AI-authored narratives, but they open up a way of refusing the breathless rhetoric of newness and unexpectedness."

https://www.latimes.com/opinion/story/2024-02-12/fentanyl-border-migrants-immigration-mexico-overdose-misinformation

** Lies, language, loss (https://www.latimes.com/opinion/story/2024-02-12/fentanyl-border-migrants-immigration-mexico-overdose-misinformation)
------------------------------------------------------------

Susan Benesch and Catherine Buerger use the context of fentanyl and border crossings to explain the dangers of combining facts, lies, and emotional language. "It is especially hard to refute this kind of misinformation, which draws not only on partial truths but also on genuine fear and grief."

https://www.washingtonpost.com/books/2024/02/14/who-owns-sentence-swift-book-review/

** Copyright history (https://www.washingtonpost.com/books/2024/02/14/who-owns-sentence-swift-book-review/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Current fellow Madhavi Sunder reviews Who Owns This Sentence?, which weaves through the history of copyright and grapples with key current issues. "[C]opyright, the authors point out, is the legal matrix underlying the wealth of nations today, with copyrights in design, software and popular culture accounting for nearly all the valuation of 'six of the largest corporations in the world.'"
