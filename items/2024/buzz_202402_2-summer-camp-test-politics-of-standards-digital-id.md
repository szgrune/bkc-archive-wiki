** AI can't get your kid into camp (https://fortune.com/2024/02/02/forget-turing-test-ai-pass-summer-camp-test-take-over-tech/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kathy Pham proposes a way of evaluating whether AI is equipped to help people with vexing everyday tasks. "The Summer Camp Test hints at what we need more of in AI: Systems built to solve real problems, from the mundane (like summer camp logistics) to the game-changing (like novel pharmaceutical research)."

Plus, in a separate op-ed (https://www.context.news/ai/opinion/at-tech-companies-lets-share-the-reins) , Pham argues that a broader set of AI builders are needed for current models and tools to reach their full potential.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4714812

** Politics of AI standards (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4714812)
------------------------------------------------------------

Alicia Solow-Niederman analyzes examples of standards across the world, demonstrating they are neither objective nor neutral, which "may undermine the functional utility of standards as soft law regulatory instruments." Given that, "there is a real question whether it is possible to embrace the politics of standards and still have them meaningfully serve as standards."

https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/02/04/clinic-supports-finale-doshi-velez-elena-l-glassman-in-submitting-ai-comment-to-nist/

** Improving AI regulation (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/02/04/clinic-supports-finale-doshi-velez-elena-l-glassman-in-submitting-ai-comment-to-nist/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Professors Finale Doshi-Velez and Elena Glassman, with assistance from the Cyberlaw Clinic, submitted an administrative comment on AI to the National Institute of Standards and Technology. The comment urges “the creation of an open benchmarking platform that would empower marginalized communities to define criteria for measuring how well [large language models] represent their language and culture.”

https://developmentmusings.medium.com/where-are-you-vs-how-are-you-cb5e81bcaf24

** Development vs. surveillance (https://developmentmusings.medium.com/where-are-you-vs-how-are-you-cb5e81bcaf24)
------------------------------------------------------------

Tunde Okunoye contemplates what investment in digital identification projects in Africa may achieve—and who they may benefit. "Are digital IDs in Africa merely tools for surveillance in the hands of increasingly authoritarian African regimes, or can they really become tools for transformative development?"

https://cyber.harvard.edu/story/2024-01/visualizing-ai-data-tools-bostons-urban-spaces
