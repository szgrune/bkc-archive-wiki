** New frontier, old harms (https://jacobin.com/2024/02/artificial-intelligence-frontier-colonialism)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nathan Sanders and Bruce Schneier argue that big tech uses the conception of AI as a new "frontier" to excuse exploitation and a lack of oversight or accountability. "The race to build the next great AI app is not the same as the California gold rush. But the potential that outsize profits will warp our priorities, values, and morals is, unfortunately, analogous."

https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378430928_Airtime_Travel_Rethinking_Appropriation_in_Global_HCI_and_Futures_of_Electronic_Exchange

** Appropriation in HCI (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378430928_Airtime_Travel_Rethinking_Appropriation_in_Global_HCI_and_Futures_of_Electronic_Exchange)
------------------------------------------------------------

Daniel Mwesigwa and Christopher Csíkszentmíhalyi reexamine appropriation in Human-Computer Interaction. "[T]he ‘appropriation’ we had initially identifed – commonly understood as unexpected modifications or adaptations of artifacts by users – was only one of several stages of appropriation conducted both by formal and informal users in the space of the Global System for Mobile Communications."

https://theworld.org/media/2024-02-28/chile-pulls-approval-giant-google-data-center

** Water for data (https://theworld.org/media/2024-02-28/chile-pulls-approval-giant-google-data-center)
------------------------------------------------------------

Current fellow Lauren Bridges discusses a Chilean environmental court's decision to suspend the approval for Google to build a giant data center due to water access concerns.  "Development of data centers happens and then local communities are facing either water restrictions or power restrictions ... and it's going to leave policymakers with several problems on their hands."
