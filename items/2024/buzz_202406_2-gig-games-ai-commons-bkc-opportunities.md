** Gigs as games (https://hbr.org/2024/06/how-micro-choices-and-games-motivate-gig-workers)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lindsey Cameron finds that gig workers gain a sense of agency from making micro-choices about their work, which provides motivation in an often unstable industry. "While the algorithm dictated many elements of their work, drivers still felt that they were able to make important, strategic choices that influenced the outcomes they achieved."

https://codingrights.org/docs/Federated_AI_Commons_ecosystem_T20Policybriefing.pdf

** Commons for AI (https://codingrights.org/docs/Federated_AI_Commons_ecosystem_T20Policybriefing.pdf)
------------------------------------------------------------

Joana Varon, Sasha Costanza-Chock, and Timnit Gebru argue that "the centralization of power through AI is not inevitable" and urge the G20 to center the public interest. "[W]e call on the G20 to support an alternative AI ecosystem where communities work with developers to build small, task-specific models that meet their needs, rather than the 'one-model-for-everything' approach taken by Big Tech."

https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/en/publications/publication/did/the-making-of-misinformed-choice-digital-technologies-in-election-cycles-insights-from-nine-countries-in-asia

** Misinformed choice (https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/en/publications/publication/did/the-making-of-misinformed-choice-digital-technologies-in-election-cycles-insights-from-nine-countries-in-asia)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nishant Shah and Anushree Majumdar explain that information overload is hampering the ability of individuals to make informed election choices. "Misinformed Choice has emerged as one of the biggest threats to stability of democratic practices because it gives the citizens the illusions of having acquired adequate and accurate information by verifying it at the layer of the stack where it has the effect, rather than at the layer of the stack where the manipulation happens."

https://www.e-flux.com/journal/146/610938/the-hacking-of-culture-and-the-creation-of-socio-technical-debt/

** Datafied culture (https://www.e-flux.com/journal/146/610938/the-hacking-of-culture-and-the-creation-of-socio-technical-debt/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kim Córdova and Bruce Schneier argue that the increased prominance of data and algorithms have shifted power and culture from nations to corporations. "[F]ragmentation of shared cultural identity reflects how the data surveillance business is rewriting both the established order of global power, and social contracts between national governments and their citizens."

https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2024/07/harry-lewis-computers-humanity

** Counterfeit humanity (https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2024/07/harry-lewis-computers-humanity)
------------------------------------------------------------

Harry R. Lewis crafts a view of the future of AI and humanity by reflecting on the past six decades of computer advancement. "It is easy to train a computer to mimic an evil human—that is already happening in sectors where it is profitable. But how could computers be trained to value civilization itself, with so little agreement among humans about what that entails?"

https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10547110

** Comp sci & responsible AI (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10547110)
------------------------------------------------------------

Virgílio Almeida, Jussara Almeida, and Wagner Meira discus how computer science can contribute to governance of responsible artificial intelligence technologies and applications. "Decentralization is key to the design of both responsible AI applications that are amenable to governance and tools to support the governance process itself."
