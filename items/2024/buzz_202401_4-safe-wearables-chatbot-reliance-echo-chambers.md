** Safety in wearable tech (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4693396)
------------------------------------------------------------

Zahra Takhshid makes an argument for mandating “privacy frictions” in wearable technology. "Privacy frictions are tangible measures that can put the reasonable bystander on notice. While this human-centered privacy design may impact the users’ experience, it ensures a future where privacy continues to be relevant and valued."

https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2024/01/chatbots-change-human-communication/677154/

** Bland biased bots (https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2024/01/chatbots-change-human-communication/677154/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Albert Fox Cahn and Bruce Schneier describe potential future impacts of increased human interactions with—and reliance on—LLMs. "Which path we take depends on how we train the chatbots of tomorrow, but it also depends on whether we invest in strengthening the bonds of civil society today."

https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10388309?source=authoralert

** Everything echo chambers (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10388309?source=authoralert)
------------------------------------------------------------

Dariusz Jemielniak and coauthors review the research literature on echo chambers in online social networks: "a significant limitation of existing research is the lack of attention given to the reinforcement of beliefs within echo chambers...In this study, we shed light on the concept of the usefulness of echo chambers, challenging the prevailing notion that they are solely detrimental."
