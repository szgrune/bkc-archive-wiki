** Defending moderation (https://www.techpolicy.press/the-word-censorship-has-an-actual-meaning-a-defense-of-content-moderation/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Current fellow Dylan Moses distinguishes censorship and content moderation, arguing that conflating them creates problems and dangers. “[C]ontent moderation at most major platforms usually evokes notions of censorship because speech people care most about – certain forms of political speech – are usually moderated heavily. But not all speech is equal.”

https://integrityinstitute.org/podcast/workplace-ethics-and-activism-with-nadah-feteih

** Workplace activism (https://integrityinstitute.org/podcast/workplace-ethics-and-activism-with-nadah-feteih)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nadah Feteih discusses tech ethics and activism from within the industry, particularly from trust and safety team members. “It’s not as binary as [what can be done from inside versus from outside the companies]...There’s a lot of pressure that we can put on all different sides and we need well-intentioned people both on the inside and on the outside.”

https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/04/17/clinic-represents-bkc-researchers-joins-advocates-in-racial-profiling-case/

** Racial profiling (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2024/04/17/clinic-represents-bkc-researchers-joins-advocates-in-racial-profiling-case/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Cyberlaw Clinic, representing Michael Bennett and Zahra Stardust, filed an amicus brief with the aim of increasing transparency around racially-targeted, online police investigative techniques. "When conducted at scale, online investigations subject scores of innocent individuals to suspicion based on race and affiliation rather than individualized suspicion."

Shira Gur-Arieh.
