** Gig workers vs. customers (https://hbr.org/2024/04/how-gig-work-pits-customers-against-workers)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lindsey Cameron and Kalie Mayberry explain why some methods for effecting change in the labor market, e.g., customer boycotts, haven't seen success in the gig economy. "Gig work is structured in a way that strengthens the alignment between customers and companies and deepens the divide between customers and workers, leading to systemic imbalances in its service triangle."

https://jacobin.com/2024/03/high-tech-ai-mexico-border

** Hyper-surveillance (https://jacobin.com/2024/03/high-tech-ai-mexico-border)
------------------------------------------------------------

Reflecting on the human aspect of migration, Petra Molnar expresses deep concerns about the technology being deployed at the US border. "Drones and surveillance towers are complemented by inland surveillance, comprising technologies such as remote license-plate readers, facial-recognition cameras at checkpoints along highways, and various fiber-optic sensor systems—an ever-tightening net."

https://lpeproject.org/blog/six-reactions-to-the-proposed-tiktok-ban/

** TikTok attack (https://lpeproject.org/blog/six-reactions-to-the-proposed-tiktok-ban/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In an LPE Project collection, Anupam Chander and Elettra Bietti offer unenthusiastic reactions to the TikTok legislation passed by the US House of Representatives. Chander notes that "[w]e should worry when politicians target a foreign-owned speech platform because of the content it carries." Bietti adds that "[i]f Americans’ rights to free expression and privacy are to mean anything, they must entail protections against the government and against foreign and US data and speech intermediaries."

Plus, don't miss the Institute for Rebooting Social Media's April 12 event, Dangerous Dancing?: TikTok, National Security, and the First Amendment (https://rebootingsocialmedia.org/events/dangerous-dancing-tiktok-national-security-and-the-first-amendment/) , moderated by Anupam Chander.

https://literalhumans.com/democracy-social-media-and-the-worlds-biggest-election-year-with-sahar-massachi/

** Bettering the net (https://literalhumans.com/democracy-social-media-and-the-worlds-biggest-election-year-with-sahar-massachi/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Sahar Massachi explores challenges and potential solutions for safeguarding online democracy in a rapidly shifting digital world. "Not all content deserves to go viral...things [used to] go viral once every two weeks, now things go micro-viral all the time, and that's just bad because it gives incentives to exploit peoples' lizard brain [where] people reshare before really digesting [content] and [contemplating whether] it passes the bar to share."

https://www.theguardian.com/global/commentisfree/2024/mar/28/sam-bankman-fried-prison-crypto-regulation

** Crypto crises (https://www.theguardian.com/global/commentisfree/2024/mar/28/sam-bankman-fried-prison-crypto-regulation)
------------------------------------------------------------

Molly White argues that the crypto sector is no more prepared to prevent the next FTX fiasco than it was before the conviction and sentencing of Sam Bankman-Fried. "Rather than protecting investors from these predatory crypto schemes, financial regulators and enforcers only stepped in once it was time to pick up the pieces and comb through the rubble of millions of people’s shattered investments."

https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/04/why-ai-fairness-conversations-must-include-disabled-people/

** Ableism and AI fairness (https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/04/why-ai-fairness-conversations-must-include-disabled-people/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Current fellow Maitreya Shah discusses his interest in analyzing different schools of thought on “AI fairness,” or movements seeking to mitigate AI bias against people in marginalized groups. “Let people with disabilities participate in the development and the deployment of technologies. Let them decide what is good for them, let them decide how they want to define or shape their own identities.”
