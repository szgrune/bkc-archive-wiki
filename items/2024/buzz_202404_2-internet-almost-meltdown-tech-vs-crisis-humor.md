** Attending to close calls (https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/backdoor-in-xz-utils-that-almost-happened)
------------------------------------------------------------

Bruce Schneier sounds the alarm on an internet meltdown near-miss. Though crisis was averted, he argues, we must pay attention. “There’s an important moral to the story of the [XZ Utils] attack and its discovery: The security of the global internet depends on countless obscure pieces of software written and maintained by even more obscure unpaid, distractible, and sometimes vulnerable volunteers. It’s an untenable situation...”

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666188824000376?via%3Dihub

** Cosmolocal production (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666188824000376?via%3Dihub)
------------------------------------------------------------

Vasilis Kostakis and Nikiforos Tsiouris advocate for a commons-based technology framework, challenging the notion that high-tech will solve major socio-environmental crises. "In a state of emergency, it is audacious to place all our hopes for tackling the ecological crisis and wealth inequality in technology—worse even, in technology that is yet to materialize."

https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14742837.2024.2331550

** Datafied social movements (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14742837.2024.2331550)
------------------------------------------------------------

Stefania Milan and David Beraldo put the studies of data and social movements in conversation with each other. "Because people’s ‘information diet’ is at the core of public opinion and values formation, the dynamics related to the production, circulation and consumption of information are constitutive of social movements in contemporary societies"

https://www.techpolicy.press/can-ai-rescue-democracy-nope-its-not-funny-enough/

** Funnier than AI (https://www.techpolicy.press/can-ai-rescue-democracy-nope-its-not-funny-enough/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Susan Benesch and Cathy Buerger argue that AI does not have the human qualities, like humor, necessary to supplant human counterspeakers in democratic discourse—but AI may serve as useful support. "Many social media users who are not (yet) counterspeakers, on the other hand, indicated that they would welcome an AI coach that could make suggestions for how to respond."
