** Children's wellbeing (https://hls.harvard.edu/today/dojs-lawsuit-against-tiktok-signals-more-aggressive-policing-of-childrens-privacy-online-says-harvard-law-expert/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In Harvard Law Today, Leah Plunkett discusses the evolving legal landscape of children's data privacy, including a recent US Department of Justice suit against TikTok. "So, it’s not just about what data is being collected about children, and with whom they are interacting. It’s also, what are they being told? What are they learning or not learning?"

https://www.nytimes.com/2024/08/20/technology/meta-section-230-lawsuit.html

** Mass unfollowing? (https://www.nytimes.com/2024/08/20/technology/meta-section-230-lawsuit.html)
------------------------------------------------------------

The New York Times chronicles the latest in Ethan Zuckerman’s lawsuit against Meta. Utilizing an undercited part of Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act, Zuckerman “is asking the court to pre-emptively protect an effort to build software that filters content because an internet user simply does not want to see it,” allowing him to “release software that lets users automatically unfollow everyone on Facebook.”

https://lumendatabase.org/blog_entries/local-news-false-positives

** Collateral damage (https://lumendatabase.org/blog_entries/local-news-false-positives)
------------------------------------------------------------

Lumen details a slew of wrongful takedown requests, many of which target local news URLs. “While the exact mechanisms behind this campaign are unclear, both its scale and the wrongful inclusion of news articles in unrelated requests point toward an automated system, one that functions without regard for any possible collateral damage to the information ecosystem.”

https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07892

** A (https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07892) nonymous personhood (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07892)
------------------------------------------------------------

With a large team of coauthors, Wendy Seltzer and Tom Zick consider the efficacy of implementing "personhood credentials" as means to deter bad actors online while maintaining users' anonymity. "We encourage governments, technologists, and standards bodies to prioritize the development, piloting, and adoption of personhood credentials to reduce AI-powered scalable deception."
