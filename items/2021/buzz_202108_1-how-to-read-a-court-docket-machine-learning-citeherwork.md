** How to Read a Court Docket (https://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/files/2021/07/How-To-Read-A-Docket-v1.0.pdf)
------------------------------------------------------------

In a free public guide from the Cyberlaw Clinic (https://hls.harvard.edu/dept/clinical/clinics/cyberlaw-clinic/) , Kendra Albert and Mason Kortz share important tips for reading and understanding court dockets. "Reading a docket can tell you information that you won’t always find in a case, including how the facts and claims evolved over time, what happened in between opinions, and why things resolved the way that they did."

https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/12895/26701

** Insights from Machine Learning Researchers (https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/12895/26701)
------------------------------------------------------------

Baobao Zheng and others surveyed hundreds of researchers on the ethics of artificial intelligence. Their findings "should help to improve how researchers, private sector executives, and policymakers think about regulations, governance frameworks, guiding principles, and national and international governance strategies for AI."

https://www.wihe.com/article-details/194/researching-gaming-and-showing-why-citations-matter/

** #CiteHerWork, "the Gray Test," and Video Game Research (https://www.wihe.com/article-details/194/researching-gaming-and-showing-why-citations-matter/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Kishonna Gray explains how she created #CiteHerWork and shares her journey engaging digital studies and Black studies. Gray started the hashtag so people could acknowledge women of color producing knowledge. "The Grey Test" was developed to establish some minimum standard not only for citing but also engaging the work of women scholars and people of color.
