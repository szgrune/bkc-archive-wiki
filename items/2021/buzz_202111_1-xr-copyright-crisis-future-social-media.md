** Youth and Extended Reality (https://cyber.harvard.edu/publication/2021/youth-and-extended-reality)
------------------------------------------------------------

A new report from the Youth and Media team explores youth engagement with XR — augmented, virtual, and mixed realities. "These immersive technologies hold great promise for learning, creativity, and self-expression, while coming with risks connected to accessibility, privacy, and safety."

https://youtu.be/yU01Qhrvb0M?t=15

** Copyright's Public Health Crisis (https://youtu.be/yU01Qhrvb0M?t=15)
------------------------------------------------------------

In a keynote address for the Global Congress on Intellectual Property and the Public Interest, Professor Ruth Okediji explains the panedmic's impact on copyright law — and explores the idea of a public health exception. "Libraries have always had the right to buy and to lend books, which is the core of their mission, so why stop now?"

https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/10/facebook-oversight-data-independent-research/620557/

** Social Media Secrecy and the Public Interest (https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2021/10/facebook-oversight-data-independent-research/620557/)
------------------------------------------------------------

In The Atlantic, Faculty Associate Susan Benesch writes that the lack of transparency among major social media companies is at odds with the public interest. "Effective oversight requires rigorous, regular, independent scrutiny, and the United States needs to cut permanent windows into the tech world’s black boxes."

https://cyber.harvard.edu/events/technology-future-we-want-imagining-positive-futures-social-media
