** Algorithmic Reparation (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20539517211044808)
------------------------------------------------------------

Jenny L. Davis, Apryl Williams, and Michael W. Yang lay the framework for a new way of thinking about "fairness" in machine learning. "We propose algorithmic reparation as a foundation for building, evaluating, adjusting, and when necessary, omitting and eradicating machine learning systems."

https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1369118X.2021.1986102

** The Decolonial Turn in Data and Technology Research (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1369118X.2021.1986102)
------------------------------------------------------------

Nick Couldry and Ulises Ali Mejias trace research on how society is transformed by data extraction for profit. Data colonialism offers “the most comprehensive perspective – historically, geopolitically, societally – on the unfolding contemporary developments of data and technology.”

https://restofworld.org/2021/rest-of-the-world-reaction-facebook-papers/

** Shrugging at the Facebook Papers (https://restofworld.org/2021/rest-of-the-world-reaction-facebook-papers/)
------------------------------------------------------------

evelyn douek explains why the Facebook Papers have failed to make as much of an impact internationally. The stories on Facebook’s actions overseas are “the most damning, in my opinion," douek told Rest of World. But “without anyone sifting through the files from that perspective and writing about it in local press, it’s understandable it gets less attention.”

https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/why-online-anonymity-matters

** Why Online Anonymity Matters (https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/why-online-anonymity-matters)
------------------------------------------------------------

Afsaneh Rigot writes that requirements for real identities online pose immense safety and security issues, especially for marginalized persons. “Banning anonymous or pseudonymous speech online is a prime example of looking for an easy fix while harming those who are already the most marginalized.”

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0093650220910345

** The Hidden Costs of Requiring Accounts (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0093650220910345)
------------------------------------------------------------

Benjamin Mako Hill and Aaron Shaw found that when 136 wikis on Fandom.com began requiring user accounts, there was a significant decrease in high-quality contributions. "Although the change deters a large portion of low-quality participation, the vast majority of deterred contributions are of higher quality."
