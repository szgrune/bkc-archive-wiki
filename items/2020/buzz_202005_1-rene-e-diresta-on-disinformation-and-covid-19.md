------------------------------------------------------------
Through brief interviews, The Breakdown shares an inside look into how members of the Berkman Klein Center community are working on and grappling with difficult topics, and brings in peer experts to discuss and break down these complicated issues. The first season of The Breakdown focuses on disinformation and features guests from the Assembly fellows, student fellows, and forum.

In the first episode, Oumou Ly, staff fellow at the Berkman Klein Center, and Renée DiResta, technical research manager at Stanford Internet Observatory, discuss what can be learned about disinformation from the current pandemic.

** Unfair fight: small businesses vs. disinformation (https://digital.hbs.edu/platforms-crowds/unfair-fight-small-businesses-vs-disinformation/)
------------------------------------------------------------

"In its more dangerous manifestations, online disinformation can defraud customers and endanger local businesses." Assembly fellow Neal Ungerleider describes the damage mis- and disinformation can have on to small businesses.

https://digital.hbs.edu/platforms-crowds/how-brands-unwittingly-fund-disinformation/

** How brands unwittingly fund disinformation (https://digital.hbs.edu/platforms-crowds/how-brands-unwittingly-fund-disinformation/)
------------------------------------------------------------

Assembly  fellow Maggie Engler explains that the use of software to buy and sell advertising online means that companies often do not know where their ads are being placed. The result?  A quarter of a billion dollars going towards funding of disinformation websites.

Over the past two months, the 2020 Assembly fellows have leveraged their expertise in journalism, policymaking, and data science to develop a combination of papers, database prototypes, and other technical tools designed to tackle the spread of disinformation in a tangible way.
