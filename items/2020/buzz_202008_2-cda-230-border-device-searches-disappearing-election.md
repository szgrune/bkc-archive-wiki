Oumou Ly is joined by Daphne Keller of the Stanford Cyber Policy Center to discuss the Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act, content moderation and Big Tech platforms, and recent events that propelled them into the spotlight in recent months.

http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2020/08/12/cyberlaw-clinic-files-brief-for-hirc-on-device-searches-at-the-border/

** Cyberlaw Clinic Files Brief on Device Searches at the Border (http://clinic.cyber.harvard.edu/2020/08/12/cyberlaw-clinic-files-brief-for-hirc-on-device-searches-at-the-border/)
------------------------------------------------------------

The Cyberlaw Clinic submitted an amicus brief on the behalf of the HIRC that emphasized the chilling effects on speech of warrantless, suspicionless device searches at the border. The brief highlighted the ways in which such government conduct deters the free exercise of expression and association.

https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2020/08/why-news-sounds-same/615070/

** The Amazing Disappearing Election (https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2020/08/why-news-sounds-same/615070/)
------------------------------------------------------------
Ethan Zuckerman uses Media Cloud to explain the narrowing of the media agenda. “2020 may demonstrate an uncomfortable truth: The months of coverage of conventions and debates is more helpful in providing advertising revenue to the news industry than it is in informing citizens about the political decisions they are asked to make.”
