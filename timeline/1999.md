# 1999

**Cyberlaw's founding-text year.** [[lawrence-lessig|Lawrence Lessig]] publishes both
*Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace* — the book that coins "code is law" and becomes
the field's central reference — and "The Law of the Horse," his own argument for why
"cyberlaw" deserves to be treated as a real field of study, not just old law applied to
a new medium. The same year, [[lawrence-lessig|Lessig]], [[jonathan-zittrain|Jonathan
Zittrain]], and Charles Nesson co-edit *Open Code / Open Content / Open Law*, an early
statement of the openness ethos that runs through [[copyright-and-open-access]] for the
next two decades. Zittrain's own "The Un-Microsoft Un-Remedy" (on the Microsoft
antitrust case) is an early instance of the platform-power skepticism that resurfaces
in [[platform-power-and-antitrust]] a quarter-century later.
