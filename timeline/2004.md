# 2004

The archive's busiest early year, dominated by two recurring internal series:
**"Berkman Briefing"** (Mary Bridges' internal newsletter — WSIS, SCO v. IBM, free
software, SXSW, the Diebold voting-machine leak — a precursor, in form and cadence, to
the later Berkman Buzz), and BKC's own **iTunes/DRM case study**, a multi-part
empirical study (green paper, white paper, case study) of how copyright, contract, and
technology together shaped the then-new digital-music business — an early instance of
the [[urs-gasser|Urs Gasser]]-led empirical-policy method that recurs throughout this
corpus. [[lawrence-lessig|Lawrence Lessig]] publishes *Free Culture*;
[[john-palfrey|John Palfrey]] argues ICANN's "foray into global internet democracy"
failed, opening [[internet-governance-and-icann]]; and BKC's Cyberlaw Clinic
predecessor files its *Capitol v. Alaujan* amicus brief (Zittrain, Fisher, Nesson,
Palfrey) on P2P file-sharing liability, continuing [[copyright-and-open-access]].
