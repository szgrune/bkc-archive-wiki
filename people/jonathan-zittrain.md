# Jonathan Zittrain

Harvard Law professor and **co-founder / faculty director of the Berkman Klein
Center** — the archive's central figure, recurring from the Buzz years to the present.
His throughline is a distinctive set of conceptual frames that the field keeps adopting:
**"generativity"** (the open system's capacity for unanticipated innovation, and its
fragility — coined in his 2006 paper and expanded into *The Future of the Internet and
How to Stop It*, 2008), the **"right to be forgotten"** and intermediary law,
**"intellectual debt"** (relying on AI answers we can't explain), the **"Great
Deplatforming"**, and most recently **AI agents and trust**. BKC's own publications
archive traces this back further still — a 1999 essay collection with
[[lawrence-lessig|Lawrence Lessig]] and Charles Nesson, the co-authored
[[internet-filtering-and-openness|Access Denied/Controlled/Contested]] trilogy on
global internet filtering (2008–2011), and "Don't Panic" (2016), on the encryption
"Going Dark" debate. He moves fluidly between [[content-moderation-and-speech]],
[[algorithmic-accountability]], and [[ai-safety-and-agents]], usually supplying the
governance-and-trust vocabulary the debate then runs on.
