# Digital Natives & Youth Privacy

BKC's **Youth and Media** research program (later the Digital Natives project),
the publications backfill's single richest cluster and the origin of the phrase
"digital natives" itself: [[john-palfrey|John Palfrey]] and Urs Gasser's *Born
Digital* (2008) frames a generation growing up native to networked technology, and
the program spends the next decade empirically testing what that actually means for
**privacy, reputation, and safety** — COPPA's real-world misinterpretation, sexting
prosecutions applied to teenagers' own consensual behavior, bullying "in a networked
era," and a run of Pew-style survey reports (with Mary Madden and Amanda Lenhart) on
teens' own privacy practices and attitudes. From 2014 the focus shifts to
**institutional** privacy — student data in K-12 cloud/ed-tech systems, co-authored
with [[leah-plunkett|Leah Plunkett]] — and by 2015–2018 goes global (Latin America,
"Digitally Connected") under Sandra Cortesi's leadership. Distinct from the 2025
[[ai-chatbots-and-mental-health]] thread in era and register, but a clear intellectual
ancestor: both ask what protecting young people online actually requires.
