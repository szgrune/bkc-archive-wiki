# Internet Governance & Institutional Design

BKC's classic **internet-governance** research program, newly surfaced by the
publications-archive backfill (1993–2026, primarily 2007–2017) — distinct from the
2025-anchored [[ai-governance-and-regulation]] thread in that its subject is the
internet's own institutions rather than AI regulation. The throughline is
**multistakeholder governance as a design problem**: an independent review of ICANN's
accountability and transparency, "interop" as both technical and legal challenge
(digital identity infrastructure, cloud computing across borders), and a running
argument for treating governance groups themselves as an object of study — "what
makes a multistakeholder body legitimate and effective?" A parallel strand asks
whether internet governance needs its own **constitutional** vocabulary ("Towards
Digital Constitutionalism?"). [[jonathan-zittrain]], [[john-palfrey]], and Urs Gasser
(BKC's former executive director, the most-published author in this corpus at 86
items) anchor nearly all of it — see a dedicated page on Gasser once created. The
2017 pivot to "A Layered Model for AI Governance" shows this research line feeding
directly into what becomes [[ai-governance-and-regulation]].
