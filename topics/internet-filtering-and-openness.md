# Internet Filtering, Censorship & Openness

The **OpenNet Initiative** — a joint Harvard/Toronto/Oxford project — is the
defining BKC research program the publications backfill surfaces here: a trilogy of
edited volumes, *[[jonathan-zittrain|Jonathan Zittrain]]*, *[[john-palfrey|John
Palfrey]]*, Ron Deibert, and Rafal Rohozinski's **Access Denied → Access Controlled →
Access Contested** (2008–2011), mapping state internet filtering as it spread from a
handful of authoritarian states to a normalized global practice. Alongside it, annual
**circumvention-tool** usage and evaluation reports (Hal Roberts, Ethan Zuckerman,
Jillian York, Robert Faris) tracked which anti-censorship tools people actually used,
and the follow-on **Internet Monitor** project (2013–2014) generalized the exercise
into a standing "state of the internet's health" annual report — the likely source of
BKC's own "Internet Health" publication category. By the late 2010s the same team
(Robert Faris, Helmi Noman, John Kelly) turns from cataloguing filtering tools to
studying censorship as an instrument of specific conflicts (Yemen's civil war,
regional Middle East disputes).
